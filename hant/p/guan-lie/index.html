觀獵

〔唐〕王維

風勁角弓鳴，將軍獵渭城。

草枯鷹眼疾，雪盡馬蹄輕。

忽過新豐市，還歸細柳營。

回看射鵰處，千里暮雲平。

英译 English translation

Watching the Hunt

Guān Liè
Wang Wei · Tang · Five-character regulated verse

The wind is strong; the horn-bows twang —

The general hunts by Weicheng town.

The grass is withered; sharp are the falcons' eyes;

The snow is gone; the horses' hooves fly light.

Suddenly they're past the Xinfeng market,

And back again at Willow Camp tonight.

He turns to look where he shot down the eagle:

A thousand li of evening clouds lie flat and bright.

About this poem

Admired for its opening — the sound of the bows comes before we even see the general — and its sweeping close. Willow Camp (细柳营) was the camp of the famously disciplined Han general Zhou Yafu; "shooting the eagle" (射雕) was the mark of a supreme archer.

Earlier translations 旧译

“The Hunt”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

The bows of horn are twanging, and bitter blows the North,

As from the town of Wei-ch'eng the hunters issue forth.

The hawk's eye gazes keenly across the prairie dry.

The snow is gone, and lightly the horsemen gallop by.

To Hsin-feng town we sweep along; then back to Hsi-liao.

Lo! where we shot the eagle rolls the clouded sunset now!

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain. Lightly adapted: line 5: "Hsin-li" corrected to "Hsin-feng" (Xinfeng).
