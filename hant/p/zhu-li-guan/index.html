竹裡館

〔唐〕王維

獨坐幽篁裡，彈琴復長嘯。

深林人不知，明月來相照。

英译 English translation

The Bamboo Grove Lodge

Zhú Lǐ Guǎn
Wang Wei · Tang · Five-character quatrain

Alone I sit among the dark bamboo,

I pluck my zither, then I whistle long.

Deep in the woods, where no one knows I'm here,

The bright moon comes to shine upon my song.

About this poem

Another Wangchuan poem. "Long whistling" (长啸) was a Daoist breathing art and a free spirit's way of expressing feeling. No one hears the music — except the moon, which comes as a friend.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Overlooked”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Beneath the bamboo grove, alone,

I seize my lute and sit and croon;

No ear to hear me, save mine own;

No eye to see me, save the moon.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.

“The Moon”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

In bamboo thicket hid, sitting alone am I.

First my guitar I strum; then stop to whistle a while.

Amid the grove so thick, no mortal can me spy.

But we behold each other, the lucent Moon and I.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
