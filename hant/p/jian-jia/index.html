蒹葭

〔先秦〕詩經

蒹葭蒼蒼，白露為霜。

所謂伊人，在水一方。

溯洄從之，道阻且長。

溯游從之，宛在水中央。

蒹葭萋萋，白露未晞。

所謂伊人，在水之湄。

溯洄從之，道阻且躋。

溯游從之，宛在水中坻。

蒹葭采采，白露未已。

所謂伊人，在水之涘。

溯洄從之，道阻且右。

溯游從之，宛在水中沚。

英译 English translation

The Reeds

Jiān Jiā
The Book of Songs · Pre-Qin · Four-character verse

The reeds are green, so green;

The white dew turns to frost.

The one that I am longing for

Is by the water, lost.

Upstream I go to find her —

The road is hard and long;

Downstream I go to find her —

She seems in midstream — gone.

The reeds are lush and thick;

The white dew is not dry.

The one that I am longing for

Is where the banks rise high.

Upstream I go to find her —

The road is steep to climb;

Downstream I go to find her —

She's on an isle, each time.

The reeds are bright and full;

The white dew's lingering still.

The one that I am longing for

Is at the water's sill.

Upstream I go to find her —

The road winds left and right;

Downstream I go to find her —

She's on a sandbar, out of sight.

About this poem

The most haunting love song of the Book of Songs: an autumn river, reeds white with frost, and a beloved — 伊人, "that person" — always glimpsed across the water or in the middle of the stream, never reached. It has been read as courtship, as the search for a worthy man, and as the pursuit of every unattainable ideal.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Këen këa”James Legge, 1876

Reed and rush are dark and green;

As hoar-frost the white dew is seen.

Him, the man I have in mind,

By this water I should find.

Searching, up the stream I haste,

On a long and toilsome quest.

Downwards then I turn, and see!

In the mid-stream standeth he.

He is there but far removed;

Vain has all my searching proved.

Reed and rush luxuriant rise;

Still undried the white dew lies.

Him, the man I have in mind,

On the stream’s edge I should find.

Upwards first my course I keep,

Though the way is rough and steep.

Downwards then, and what to see?

In the mid-stream standeth he,

On the islet, far removed;—

Vain has all my searching proved.

Reed and rush grow thick and tall;

Ceases not the dew to fall.

Him, the man I have in mind,

On the stream’s bank I should find.

Upwards first I go along,

But the hard path leads me wrong.

Downwards then my steps I turn,

And in mid-stream him discern,

On the island, far removed;—

Vain has all my searching proved.

Translated by James Legge, from The She King; or, The Book of Ancient Poetry, Translated in English Verse (1876) · public domain.
