靜女

〔先秦〕詩經

靜女其姝，俟我於城隅。

愛而不見，搔首踟躕。

靜女其孌，貽我彤管。

彤管有煒，說懌女美。

自牧歸荑，洵美且異。

匪女之為美，美人之貽。

英译 English translation

The Gentle Girl

Jìng Nǚ
The Book of Songs · Pre-Qin · Four-character verse

The gentle girl, so pretty,

Waits for me at the corner of the wall;

She hides — I cannot see her —

I scratch my head, and pace, and call.

The gentle girl, so lovely,

Gave me a crimson reed;

How brightly it is glowing —

I love its beauty, indeed.

From the pasture she brought me a shoot of grass,

Truly lovely, and so rare.

It's not that you're so lovely, little grass —

It's that you come from my lady fair.

About this poem

A lovers' tryst: the girl playfully hides; the young man frets; then he treasures her humble gifts — a red reed pipe, a sprout of grass from the pasture — because she gave them. 匪女之为美，美人之贻 — "it's not you that's beautiful, but the beauty who gave you" — is the whole logic of love in a line.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Tsing neu”James Legge, 1876

O sweet maiden, so fair and retiring,

At the corner I’m waiting for you;

And I’m scratching my head, and inquiring

What on earth it were best I should do.

Oh! the maiden, so handsome and coy,

For a pledge gave a slim rosy reed.

Than the reed is she brighter, my joy;

On her loveliness how my thoughts feed!

In the pastures a t‘e blade she sought,

And she gave it, so elegant, rare.

Oh! the grass does not dwell in my thought,

But the donor, more elegant, fair.

Translated by James Legge, from The She King; or, The Book of Ancient Poetry, Translated in English Verse (1876) · public domain.

“Trysting Time”L. Cranmer-Byng, 1909

A pretty girl at time o' gloaming

Hath whispered me to go and meet her

Without the city gate.

I love her, but she tarries coming.

Shall I return, or stay and greet her?

I burn, and wait.

Truly she charmeth all beholders,

'Tis she hath given me this jewel,

The jade of my delight;

But this red jewel-jade that smoulders,

To my desire doth add more fuel,

New charms to-night.

She has gathered with her lily fingers

A lily fair and rare to see.

Oh! sweeter still the fragrance lingers

From the warm hand that gave it me.

Translated by L. Cranmer-Byng, from A Lute of Jade (1909) · public domain.
