獨坐敬亭山

〔唐〕李白

眾鳥高飛盡，孤雲獨去閒。

相看兩不厭，只有敬亭山。

英译 English translation

Sitting Alone on Jingting Mountain

Dú Zuò Jìng Tíng Shān
Li Bai · Tang · Five-character quatrain

The flocks of birds fly high and disappear;

A lonely cloud drifts off without a care.

We gaze and gaze, and never tire of it —

Just Jingting Mountain, and I, sitting there.

About this poem

Written in 753 near Xuancheng, when Li Bai was past fifty and long out of favour. Birds and clouds — like friends and fortune — all leave him; only the mountain stays, and the two regard each other like old friends who need no words.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Companions”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

The birds have all flown to their roost in the tree,

The last cloud has just floated lazily by;

But we never tire of each other, not we,

As we sit there together,—the mountains and I.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.

“The Lover of Scenery”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

All the Birds had flown away.

One Cloud its aimless circle ran,

Unwearied gazing on each other,

I and Ching-ting Shan.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain. Lightly adapted: line 4: "It" changed to "I"; in the Chinese the lone cloud drifts off and it is the poet who sits gazing with Jingting Mountain.
