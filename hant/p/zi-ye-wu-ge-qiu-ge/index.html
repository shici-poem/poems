子夜吳歌·秋歌

〔唐〕李白

長安一片月，萬戶搗衣聲。

秋風吹不盡，總是玉關情。

何日平胡虜，良人罷遠征。

英译 English translation

Midnight Song of Wu: Autumn

Zǐ Yè Wú Gē Qiū Gē
Li Bai · Tang · Yuefu ballad

One sheet of moonlight over Chang'an lies;

From ten thousand homes the fulling-mallets pound.

The autumn wind blows on and never dies —

All of it heartache, for the Jade Gate bound.

When will we crush the raiders and have peace,

So my good man's long campaigns may cease?

About this poem

Women beat cloth on stones to soften it before sewing winter clothes for husbands at the frontier, so the sound of "pounding clothes" (捣衣) was the sound of autumn longing. The Jade Gate Pass (玉门关) was the gateway to the western frontier. Li Bai turns an old southern love-song form into the voice of a whole city's grief.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Under the Moon”L. Cranmer-Byng, 1909

Under the crescent moon's faint glow

The washerman's bat resounds afar,

And the autumn breeze sighs tenderly.

But my heart has gone to the Tartar war,

To bleak Kansuh and the steppes of snow,

Calling my husband back to me.

Translated by L. Cranmer-Byng, from A Lute of Jade (1909) · public domain.

“Absence”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

Our homely Moon in quiet wanes away.

Around me dully thuds the washing bar:

Nor drops the Wind long Autumn from its wings.

While all my heart is at the frontier far.

Ah! when will all our foes be beaten back,

And my dear husband finish distant war?

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain. Lightly adapted: line 1: "slender" changed to "homely", Fletcher's own reading in the 1918 first edition; the Chinese has a broad moon over Chang'an, not a waning crescent.
