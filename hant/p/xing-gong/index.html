行宮

〔唐〕元稹

寥落古行宮，宮花寂寞紅。

白頭宮女在，閒坐說玄宗。

英译 English translation

The Travelling Palace

Xíng Gōng
Yuan Zhen · Tang · Five-character quatrain

The old travelling palace, lonely and decayed;

Its flowers bloom red, with no one there to see.

White-haired palace maids are all that stayed —

They sit and talk of Xuanzong's days, idly.

About this poem

Twenty characters holding half a century. The women were chosen as girls to serve Emperor Xuanzong, who never came back after the rebellion; now white-haired, they reminisce about him among the red flowers. Critics have called it the most concise poem of the Tang.

Earlier translations 旧译

“At an Old Palace”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Deserted now the Imperial bowers

Save by some few poor lonely flowers

One white-haired dame,

An Emperor's flame,

Sits down and tells of bygone hours.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.

“The Ancient Palace”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

The ancient Palace lies in desolation spread.

The very garden flowers in solitude grow red.

Only some withered dames with whitened hair remain,

Who sit there idly talking of the Mystic Monarch dead.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain. Lightly adapted: line 4: "mystic monarchs" changed to "the Mystic Monarch"; the Chinese names one emperor, Xuanzong ("Mystic Ancestor").
