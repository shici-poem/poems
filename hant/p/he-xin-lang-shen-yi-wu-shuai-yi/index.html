賀新郎（甚矣吾衰矣）

〔宋〕辛棄疾

甚矣吾衰矣。

悵平生、交遊零落，只今餘幾！

白髮空垂三千丈，一笑人間萬事。

問何物、能令公喜。

我見青山多嫵媚，料青山、見我應如是。

情與貌，略相似。

一尊搔首東窗裡。

想淵明、停雲詩就，此時風味。

江左沉酣求名者，豈識濁醪妙理。

回首叫、雲飛風起。

不恨古人吾不見，恨古人、不見吾狂耳。

知我者，二三子。

英译 English translation

Congratulating the Bridegroom (How Old I've Grown)

Hè Xīn Láng Shèn Yǐ Wú Shuāi Yǐ
Xin Qiji · Song · Ci lyric

How old I've grown, how frail!

I grieve my lifelong friends have scattered far —

How many now remain?

My white hair hangs for nothing, three thousand yards long;

With one laugh I dismiss the world's affairs.

What could still make me glad, you ask?

I find the green mountains so charming —

And I'd guess they find me charming too.

In heart and looks,

We're much alike, we two.

A cup of wine; I scratch my head by the east window;

I think of Tao Yuanming, his "Lingering Clouds" just done —

He must have felt like this.

Those south of the River, drunk on fame —

What do they know of the deep wisdom of cloudy wine?

I turn and shout — the clouds fly, the wind rises!

I don't regret I never saw the ancients;

I regret the ancients never saw my wild delight.

Those who know me

Are just two or three.

About this poem

Written in retirement around 1200, in a pavilion named after Tao Yuanming's poem "Lingering Clouds", which mourns absent friends. 我见青山多妩媚，料青山见我应如是 — "I find the green mountains charming; I expect they find me so too" — is among the most quoted lines Xin Qiji wrote: a lonely man finding his only equal in the mountains. "The clouds fly, the wind rises" echoes Liu Bang's Song of the Great Wind.
