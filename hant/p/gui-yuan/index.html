閨怨

〔唐〕王昌齡

閨中少婦不知愁，春日凝妝上翠樓。

忽見陌頭楊柳色，悔教夫婿覓封侯。

英译 English translation

A Young Wife's Lament

Guī Yuàn
Wang Changling · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

The young wife in her chamber doesn't know what sorrow is;

One spring day, all made up, she climbs the jade-green tower.

She suddenly sees the green of the willows along the road —

And rues the day she sent her man to seek a lordship's power.

About this poem

A masterpiece of the sudden turn. The willows by the road — the trees of parting — make her realise in an instant what she gave up when she encouraged her husband to win a title (封侯) at the frontier.

Earlier translations 旧译

“At the Wars”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

See the young wife whose bosom ne'er has ached with cruel pain!—

In gay array she mounts the tower when spring comes round again.

Sudden she sees the willow-trees their newest green put on,

And sighs for her husband far away in search of glory gone.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.

“Tears in the Spring”L. Cranmer-Byng, 1909

Clad in blue silk and bright embroidery

At the first call of Spring the fair young bride,

On whom as yet Sorrow has laid no scar,

Climbs the Kingfisher's Tower. Suddenly

She sees the bloom of willows far and wide,

And grieves for him she lent to fame and war.

Translated by L. Cranmer-Byng, from A Lute of Jade (1909) · public domain.

“"In the Spring"”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

"Within her peaceful chamber, no care the maid oppressed;

Until the verdant Tower she climbed one springtide, gaily dressed.

The stir of sprouting foliage beyond the street she saw.

Regret she'd sent her love to fame rose swelling in her breast."

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
