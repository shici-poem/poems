春怨（金昌緒）

〔唐〕金昌緒

打起黃鶯兒，莫教枝上啼。

啼時驚妾夢，不得到遼西。

英译 English translation

Spring Lament (Jin Changxu)

Chūn Yuàn Jīn Chāng Xù
Jin Changxu · Tang · Five-character quatrain

Go, shoo that oriole away!

Don't let it sing upon the bough —

Its singing woke me from my dream

Before I could reach Liaoxi, somehow.

About this poem

A young wife chases a singing oriole out of the tree — because it woke her just as, in her dream, she was about to reach her husband at the frontier in Liaoxi, in the far north-east. The whole story unfolds backwards, one line at a time.

Earlier translations 旧译

“At Dawn”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Drive the young orioles away,

Nor let them on the branches play;

Their chirping breaks my slumber through

And keeps me from my dreams of you.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.

“A Lover's Dream”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

Oh, drive the golden orioles

From off our garden tree!

Their warbling broke the dream wherein

My lover smiled to me.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
