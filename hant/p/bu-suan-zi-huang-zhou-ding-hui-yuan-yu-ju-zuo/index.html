卜算子·黃州定慧院寓居作

〔宋〕蘇軾

缺月掛疏桐，漏斷人初靜。

誰見幽人獨往來，縹緲孤鴻影。

驚起卻回頭，有恨無人省。

揀盡寒枝不肯棲，寂寞沙洲冷。

英译 English translation

Song of Divination: Written While Lodging at Dinghui Temple, Huangzhou

Bǔ Suàn Zǐ Huáng Zhōu Dìng Huì Yuàn Yù Jū Zuò
Su Shi · Song · Ci lyric

A waning moon hangs in the sparse wutong tree;

The water-clock runs dry; the world's begun to rest.

Who sees the hermit coming and going, lonely, free? —

A lone wild goose's shadow, faint, now east, now west.

Startled, it turns its head and looks around,

With grief that no one in the world can see.

It tries each frozen branch, and will not settle down —

The sandbar, lonely, cold as cold can be.

About this poem

Written in 1080, soon after Su Shi reached Huangzhou after months in prison and a near death sentence; he lodged at Dinghui Temple. The lone goose that refuses every cold branch and settles on a freezing sandbar is Su Shi himself — wounded and startled, yet unwilling to compromise.
