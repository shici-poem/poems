隴西行（其二）

〔唐〕陳陶

誓掃匈奴不顧身，五千貂錦喪胡塵。

可憐無定河邊骨，猶是春閨夢裡人。

英译 English translation

Song of Longxi (II)

Lǒng Xī Xíng Qí Èr
Chen Tao · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

They swore to sweep the Xiongnu out, with no thought for their life;

Five thousand men in sable and brocade fell in the northern dust.

Alas — the bones along the Wuding River, white and bare,

Are still the living men in the spring dreams of each wife.

About this poem

The most heartbreaking war poem in Chinese: the wives at home, not yet knowing, still dream of their husbands — who are already bones by the Wuding River ("River of No Fixed Course") on the northern frontier. "Sable and brocade" was the uniform of elite Han troops.

Earlier translations 旧译

“An Oath”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

They swore the Huns should perish: they would die if needs they must

And now five thousand, sable-clad, have bit the Tartar dust.

Along the river-bank their bones lie scattered where they may,

But still their forms in dreams arise to fair ones far away.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.
