旅夜書懷

〔唐〕杜甫

細草微風岸，危檣獨夜舟。

星垂平野闊，月湧大江流。

名豈文章著，官應老病休。

飄飄何所似，天地一沙鷗。

英译 English translation

Thoughts While Travelling at Night

Lǚ Yè Shū Huái
Du Fu · Tang · Five-character regulated verse

Fine grass, a gentle breeze along the shore;

A tall mast, and my lonely boat by night.

The stars hang low above the boundless plain;

The moon wells up as the great River takes flight.

Fame — has my writing ever won me that?

Old and sick, I let my office pass me by.

Drifting, drifting — what is it I'm like?

A lone gull between the earth and sky.

About this poem

Written in 765 as Du Fu, having lost his patron in Chengdu, sailed down the Yangtze. The vast, luminous middle couplet — stars sagging to the plain, the moon surging in the current — sets up the final image: one small gull in the immensity of the universe.

Earlier translations 旧译

“My Reflection by Night”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

Some scattered grass. A shore breeze blowing light.

A giddy mast. A lonely boat at night.

The wide-flung stars o'erhang all vasty space.

The moonbeams with the Yangtze's current race.

How by my pen can I to fame attain?

Worn out, from office better to refrain.

Drifting o'er life—and what in sooth am I?

A sea-gull floating twixt the Earth and Sky.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
