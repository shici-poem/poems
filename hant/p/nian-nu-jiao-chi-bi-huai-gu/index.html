念奴嬌·赤壁懷古

〔宋〕蘇軾

大江東去，浪淘盡，千古風流人物。

故壘西邊，人道是，三國周郎赤壁。

亂石穿空，驚濤拍岸，捲起千堆雪。

江山如畫，一時多少豪傑。

遙想公瑾當年，小喬初嫁了，雄姿英發。

羽扇綸巾，談笑間，檣櫓灰飛煙滅。

故國神遊，多情應笑我，早生華髮。

人生如夢，一尊還酹江月。

英译 English translation

The Charms of Niannu: Remembering the Past at Red Cliff

Niàn Nú Jiāo Chì Bì Huái Gǔ
Su Shi · Song · Ci lyric

The Great River flows east;

Its waves have washed away

The gallant heroes of a thousand years.

West of the old fort, there, they say,

Is Red Cliff, where young Zhou fought in the Three Kingdoms' day.

Jagged rocks pierce the clouds,

Wild breakers pound the shore,

Rolling up a thousand drifts of snow.

River and mountains, like a painting —

How many heroes were here, long ago!

I think of Gongjin in those years,

With Little Qiao just newly wed,

Heroic in his bearing, brilliant in his prime;

A feather fan, a silken cap — and, laughing, chatting,

He burned their masts and oars to flying ash and vanished smoke.

My spirit wanders in that ancient land —

How the tender-hearted would laugh at me,

For greying, as I have, before my time!

Life is but a dream:

Let me pour this cup to the river and the moon.

About this poem

Written in 1082 during Su Shi's exile at Huangzhou, at a cliff on the Yangtze that he took — perhaps wrongly — for the site of the Battle of Red Cliff (208), where the young Wu general Zhou Yu (Gongjin), newly married to the beauty Xiao Qiao, burned Cao Cao's fleet. This lyric founded the "heroic and unrestrained" (豪放) school of ci: it should be sung, a contemporary said, by a big man from the west beating an iron clapper, not by a girl with castanets. The ending turns from heroism to the dream of life, and a libation to the eternal moon.
