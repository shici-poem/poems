芙蓉樓送辛漸

〔唐〕王昌齡

寒雨連江夜入吳，平明送客楚山孤。

洛陽親友如相問，一片冰心在玉壺。

英译 English translation

Seeing Xin Jian Off at Lotus Tower

Fú Róng Lóu Sòng Xīn Jiàn
Wang Changling · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

Cold rain spread over the river as night came into Wu;

At dawn I see you off; the hills of Chu stand lone.

If kin and friends in Luoyang ask about me, tell them true:

"A heart of ice in a pot of jade" — that's all I own.

About this poem

Wang Changling, slandered and demoted, sees a friend off to Luoyang from the Lotus Tower at Zhenjiang. 一片冰心在玉壶 — a heart pure as ice in a jade vessel, unchanged by slander — became an idiom for integrity.

Earlier translations 旧译

“A Message”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Onwards tonight my storm-beat course I steer,

I speed you forth at dawn; lone mountains fade;

Should friends in Lo-yang ask about my cheer,

Tell them, "an icy heart in vase of jade."

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain. Lightly adapted: lines 2-3 reworded: the poet is seeing a friend off, not leaving himself, and the message is for his friends in Luoyang.

Show the original 1898 text

Onwards tonight my storm-beat course I steer,

At dawn these mountains will for ever fade;

Should those I leave behind enquire my cheer,

Tell them, "an icy heart in vase of jade."
