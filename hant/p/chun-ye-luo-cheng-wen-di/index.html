春夜洛城聞笛

〔唐〕李白

誰家玉笛暗飛聲，散入春風滿洛城。

此夜曲中聞折柳，何人不起故園情。

英译 English translation

Hearing a Flute on a Spring Night in Luoyang

Chūn Yè Luò Chéng Wén Dí
Li Bai · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

Whose jade flute sends its notes in secret flight,

Scattered by spring winds through Luoyang's every street?

Hearing "Breaking Willows" in the tune tonight,

Whose heart would not for home and garden beat?

About this poem

"Breaking Willows" (折杨柳) was a farewell tune: friends parting gave each other a willow twig, because 柳 (willow) sounds like 留 (stay). Hearing it played by an unseen flautist in the spring night, the poet realises the whole city must be thinking of home.

Earlier translations 旧译

“"Soft Stillness and the Night Become the Touches of Sweet Harmony"”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

From what clear flute unseen these flying trills

With which the Wind of Spring the City fills?

Amid the strains the Willow's plucked anew—

In what sweet Garden—how my bosom thrills!

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain. Lightly adapted: line 3: "the Flower" changed to "the Willow's"; the tune heard is "Breaking the Willow", the parting song.
