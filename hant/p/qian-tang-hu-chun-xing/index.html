錢塘湖春行

〔唐〕白居易

孤山寺北賈亭西，水面初平雲腳低。

幾處早鶯爭暖樹，誰家新燕啄春泥。

亂花漸欲迷人眼，淺草才能沒馬蹄。

最愛湖東行不足，綠楊陰裡白沙堤。

英译 English translation

A Spring Walk by Qiantang Lake

Qián Táng Hú Chūn Xíng
Bai Juyi · Tang · Seven-character regulated verse

North of Gushan Temple, west of Lord Jia's pavilion,

The lake has just come level; the clouds hang low and near.

Here and there, early orioles vie for sunny trees;

Whose new swallows peck the spring mud here?

A riot of flowers begins to dazzle the eye;

The tender grass just covers the horses' hooves.

Best I love the lake's east side — I can't walk it enough:

The white-sand causeway in the green willows' shade it moves.

About this poem

Hangzhou's West Lake (then called Qiantang Lake) in early spring, when Bai Juyi was its governor (822–824). Every detail is "just beginning" — early orioles, new swallows, grass just tall enough to hide a hoof. The Bai Causeway, the white-sand dyke he loved, still carries his name.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Springtide”L. Cranmer-Byng, 1909

By the lone convent on the hill

Past Chia’s pavilion to the west,

Almost upon the waters still

The quiet clouds lean down and rest.

In green pavilions of warm trees

The golden builders toil and sing;

While swallows dip along the leas,

And dabble in the ooze of Spring.

A thousand flowers, a thousand dreams,

Bright pageants in confusion pass.

See yonder, where the white horse gleams

His fetlocks deep in pliant grass.

Beside the eastern lake there calls

The path my loving footstep knows,

And in the long embankment walls

The willow shade invites repose.

Translated by L. Cranmer-Byng, from A Lute of Jade (1909) · public domain. Lightly adapted: lines 1–2 reworded: the original read the Chia (贾) Pavilion as "merchants faring from the west"; lines 14–15 reworded to restore "best I love the lake’s east shore", which the original turned into its opposite.

The lonely convent on the hill

Draws merchants faring from the west;

Almost upon the waters still

The quiet clouds lean down and rest.

In green pavilions of warm trees

The golden builders toil and sing;

While swallows dip along the leas,

And dabble in the ooze of Spring.

A thousand flowers, a thousand dreams,

Bright pageants in confusion pass.

See yonder, where the white horse gleams

His fetlocks deep in pliant grass.

Beside the eastern lake there calls

No laughing throng, no lover goes;

But in the long embankment walls

The willow shade invites repose.
