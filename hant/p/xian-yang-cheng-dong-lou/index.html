咸陽城東樓

〔唐〕許渾

一上高城萬里愁，蒹葭楊柳似汀洲。

溪雲初起日沉閣，山雨欲來風滿樓。

鳥下綠蕪秦苑夕，蟬鳴黃葉漢宮秋。

行人莫問當年事，故國東來渭水流。

英译 English translation

The East Tower of Xianyang

Xián Yáng Chéng Dōng Lóu
Xu Hun · Tang · Seven-character regulated verse

I climb the city wall, and grief spreads ten thousand li;

The reeds and willows look like islets on a southern shore.

Clouds rise from the stream; the sun sinks behind the hall's high eaves;

The mountain rain is coming — wind fills the tower's every door.

Birds land in the green weeds of the Qin park as evening falls;

Cicadas cry in yellow leaves where Han palaces stood before.

Traveller, don't ask about the things of long ago —

Past the old capital, from the east, the Wei flows as of yore.

About this poem

Xianyang, capital of the Qin, lies across the Wei River from Chang'an. 山雨欲来风满楼, "the mountain rain is coming; the wind fills the tower", became the proverb for the tense moment before a storm breaks — political or otherwise. The ruined Qin and Han palaces foreshadow the fall of the Tang itself.
