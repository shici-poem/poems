採蓮曲（其二）

〔唐〕王昌齡

荷葉羅裙一色裁，芙蓉向臉兩邊開。

亂入池中看不見，聞歌始覺有人來。

英译 English translation

Lotus-Picking Song (II)

Cǎi Lián Qǔ Qí Èr
Wang Changling · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

Lotus leaves and silken skirt are cut from the same green;

Lotus blooms beside her face unfold on either side.

Lost amid the pond, she cannot now be seen —

Until a song rings out, and you know where she hides.

About this poem

The girl is so like the lotus — green skirt, pink cheeks — that she vanishes among the flowers; only her song gives her away.

Earlier translations 旧译

“The Song of the Nenuphars (first stanza)”L. Cranmer-Byng, 1909

Leaves of the Nenuphars and silken skirts the same pale green,

On flower and laughing face alike the same rose-tints are seen;

Like some blurred tapestry they blend within the lake displayed:

You cannot part the leaves from silk, the lily from the maid.

Only when sudden voices swell

Do maidens of their presence tell.

Translated by L. Cranmer-Byng, from A Lute of Jade (1909) · public domain.
