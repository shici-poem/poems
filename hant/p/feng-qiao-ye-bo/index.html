楓橋夜泊

〔唐〕張繼

月落烏啼霜滿天，江楓漁火對愁眠。

姑蘇城外寒山寺，夜半鐘聲到客船。

英译 English translation

Night Mooring at Maple Bridge

Fēng Qiáo Yè Bó
Zhang Ji · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

The moon has set; crows caw; frost fills the sky;

By river maples and fishing fires, I lie in grief, half asleep.

Outside Gusu's walls, the Cold Mountain Temple stands nearby;

Its midnight bell rings out to the traveller's boat, slow and deep.

About this poem

Written around 756 near Suzhou (Gusu) by a traveller fleeing the An Lushan rebellion. Sight, sound and feeling blend in the dark: setting moon, cawing crows, frost, red maples and fishing lights — and finally the bell. The poem made Hanshan ("Cold Mountain") Temple famous; crowds still gather there on New Year's Eve to hear its bell.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Anchored by Night”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

The failing of the moonlight the cawing crow awakes,

And glitters all the sky above with shining frosty flakes.

The maples on the river bank, the lamps the fishers bear,

Cast gloomy shadows through the night that vex our rest with care.

From yonder Chill Hill Temple by Soochow's ancient town

The sudden booming of the bell, the midnight calling down,

Comes with a clang that startles our ship-borne comrades' ears.

Imagination's pulses beat quick with shadowy fears.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
