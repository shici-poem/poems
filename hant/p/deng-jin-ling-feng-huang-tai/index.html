登金陵鳳凰臺

〔唐〕李白

鳳凰臺上鳳凰遊，鳳去臺空江自流。

吳宮花草埋幽徑，晉代衣冠成古丘。

三山半落青天外，二水中分白鷺洲。

總為浮雲能蔽日，長安不見使人愁。

英译 English translation

Climbing Phoenix Terrace at Jinling

Dēng Jīn Líng Fèng Huáng Tái
Li Bai · Tang · Seven-character regulated verse

On Phoenix Terrace phoenixes once used to play;

The phoenixes are gone, the terrace bare; the river flows its way.

The flowers and grasses of the Wu palace bury hidden lanes;

The robes and caps of Jin are ancient mounds today.

The Three Peaks, half-dissolving, lean beyond the blue;

Two waters split around White Egret Isle midway.

It's all because the drifting clouds can hide the sun —

Chang'an is lost to sight, and grief has come to stay.

About this poem

Jinling (Nanjing) had been the capital of six dynasties, all long vanished. Li Bai's poem deliberately echoes Cui Hao's Yellow Crane Tower, which he had admired. The ending turns from history to politics: the "drifting clouds" hiding the sun are the slanderers who cut Li Bai off from the emperor in Chang'an.
