登岳陽樓

〔唐〕杜甫

昔聞洞庭水，今上岳陽樓。

吳楚東南坼，乾坤日夜浮。

親朋無一字，老病有孤舟。

戎馬關山北，憑軒涕泗流。

英译 English translation

Climbing Yueyang Tower

Dēng Yuè Yáng Lóu
Du Fu · Tang · Five-character regulated verse

Long had I heard of Dongting's waters wide;

Today I climb the Yueyang Tower's height.

Here Wu and Chu split east and south apart;

Heaven and earth float on it day and night.

From kin and friends, not one word comes to me;

Old and sick, I have one lonely boat.

North of the passes, war-horses still ride —

I lean on the rail and let my tears run free.

About this poem

768, two years before Du Fu's death. Dongting Lake in Hunan is so vast that the ancient lands of Wu and Chu seem split by it, and the whole universe floats upon it. Against this immensity, Du Fu has nothing — no letters, only a boat, and war still raging in the north.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Yo-yang Tower”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

Long since the fame of Tung-ting Lake I knew.

At last from Yo-yang Tower its truth I view.

To south and east two countries it divides;

While Earth and Sky swim ever on its tides.

No friend and no relation here engage.

This lonely boat my all in sickly age.

The war horse tramps the hills 'twixt home and me.

Yea, o'er this rail my tears fall bitterly.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
