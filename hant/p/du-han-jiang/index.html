渡漢江

〔唐〕宋之問

嶺外音書斷，經冬復歷春。

近鄉情更怯，不敢問來人。

英译 English translation

Crossing the Han River

Dù Hàn Jiāng
Song Zhiwen · Tang · Five-character quatrain

Beyond the Five Ridges, no word, no letter came;

Through winter, then through spring, I waited, all alone.

Now, nearing home, my heart grows timid, full of fear —

I dare not ask the travellers for news of home.

About this poem

Exiled to the far south (beyond the Five Ridges) in 705, Song Zhiwen secretly fled north. Crossing the Han River near home, he is afraid to ask about his family — what if the news is bad? 近乡情更怯, "the nearer home, the more timid the heart", describes every long-delayed homecoming.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Homeward”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

No letters to the frontier come,

The winter softens into spring;

I tremble as I draw near home,

And dare not ask what news you bring.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.
