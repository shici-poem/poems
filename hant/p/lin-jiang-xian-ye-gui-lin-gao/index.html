臨江仙·夜歸臨皋

〔宋〕蘇軾

夜飲東坡醒復醉，歸來彷彿三更。

家童鼻息已雷鳴。敲門都不應，倚杖聽江聲。

長恨此身非我有，何時忘卻營營？

夜闌風靜縠紋平。小舟從此逝，江海寄餘生。

英译 English translation

Immortal by the River: Returning to Lingao at Night

Lín Jiāng Xiān Yè Guī Lín Gāo
Su Shi · Song · Ci lyric

Drinking at Dongpo, sober and drunk again,

I come home near the third watch, I'd say.

The houseboy's snores are thundering by then;

I knock, but no one answers —

So, leaning on my stick, I hear the river on its way.

I've long resented that this body's not my own —

When can I forget this endless strife?

The night is late, the wind is still, the ripples smooth as crepe:

In a little boat I'll vanish from here, alone,

And give the rivers and the seas the rest of my life.

About this poem

Huangzhou, 1082. Locked out by his snoring servant, Su Shi stands listening to the Yangtze and longs to be free of office and its "striving" (营营, a word from Zhuangzi). Dongpo, "East Slope", was the plot he farmed in exile — and the name he took for himself. Legend says a rumour spread the next day that he had sung this song, hung up his official hat and sailed away; the alarmed governor rushed over and found him snoring in bed.
