涼州詞（王翰）

〔唐〕王翰

葡萄美酒夜光杯，欲飲琵琶馬上催。

醉臥沙場君莫笑，古來征戰幾人回？

英译 English translation

Liangzhou Song (Wang Han)

Liáng Zhōu Cí Wáng Hàn
Wang Han · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

Fine wine of grapes in cups of jade that glow at night;

We go to drink — the pipas urge us on from horseback, too.

If I lie drunk upon the sands, don't laugh at the sight:

Since ancient times, of those who went to war, how few came through?

About this poem

Grape wine and "night-shining cups" of white jade were exotic luxuries from Central Asia, and the pipa was the instrument of the steppe. The soldiers drink before battle; the last couplet is both bravado and despair.

Earlier translations 旧译

“A Reason Fair”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

'Tis night: the grape-juice mantles high in cups of gold galore;

We set to drink,—but now the lute calls us to horse once more.

Oh marvel not if drunken we lie strewed about the plain;

How few of all who seek the fight shall e'er come back again!

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain. Lightly adapted: "the bugle sounds" changed to "the lute calls us" in line 2 (the call to horse comes from a pipa played on horseback).
