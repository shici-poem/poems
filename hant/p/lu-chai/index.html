鹿柴

〔唐〕王維

空山不見人，但聞人語響。

返景入深林，復照青苔上。

英译 English translation

Deer Park

Lù Chái
Wang Wei · Tang · Five-character quatrain

On the empty mountain, no one can be seen —

Yet voices echo, somewhere, faintly heard.

The sunset's light comes back into the deep woods,

And shines again upon the moss of green.

About this poem

One of the twenty quatrains of the Wangchuan Collection, written at Wang Wei's country estate. Nothing happens — voices without people, a slant of evening light on moss — yet the poem has been called the purest expression of Chan (Zen) stillness in Chinese verse. Translators love it: Eliot Weinberger wrote a whole book, Nineteen Ways of Looking at Wang Wei, comparing versions of these twenty characters.

Earlier translations 旧译

“The Form of the Deer”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

So lone seem the hills; there is no one in sight there.

But whence is the echo of voices I hear?

The rays of the sunset pierce slanting the forest,

And in their reflection green mosses appear.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
