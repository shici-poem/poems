賈生

〔唐〕李商隱

宣室求賢訪逐臣，賈生才調更無倫。

可憐夜半虛前席，不問蒼生問鬼神。

英译 English translation

Master Jia

Jiǎ Shēng
Li Shangyin · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

Seeking worthies, the emperor summoned back the banished man;

Master Jia's brilliance was beyond compare.

At midnight, sadly, the emperor drew his mat close — in vain:

He asked not of the people, but of ghosts and gods up there.

About this poem

Jia Yi, the brilliant young statesman of the Han, had been exiled; Emperor Wen recalled him and talked with him late into the night in the Xuanshi Hall, leaning closer and closer on his mat — about ghosts and spirits. Li Shangyin uses the story to mock rulers who chased Daoist mysticism (several Tang emperors died of "immortality" elixirs) while neglecting their people.
