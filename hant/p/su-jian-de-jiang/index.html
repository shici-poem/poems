宿建德江

〔唐〕孟浩然

移舟泊煙渚，日暮客愁新。

野曠天低樹，江清月近人。

英译 English translation

Mooring on the Jiande River

Sù Jiàn Dé Jiāng
Meng Haoran · Tang · Five-character quatrain

I steer my boat to moor by a misty isle;

As the sun sets, a traveller's grief comes new.

The plain is vast — the sky stoops below the trees;

The river's clear — the moon comes close to me, too.

About this poem

Mooring at dusk on the Xin'an River in Zhejiang. The last two lines are among the most admired in Chinese: on the open plain the sky seems lower than the trees; in the clear river, the moon's reflection floats right beside the boat — the only companion the lonely traveller has.

Earlier translations 旧译

“At Anchor”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

I steer my boat to anchor by the mist-clad river eyot,

And mourn the dying day that brings me nearer to my fate.

Across the woodland wild I see the sky lean on the trees,

While close to hand the mirrored moon floats on the shining seas.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.
