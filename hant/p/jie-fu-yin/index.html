節婦吟

〔唐〕張籍

君知妾有夫，贈妾雙明珠。

感君纏綿意，系在紅羅襦。

妾家高樓連苑起，良人執戟明光裡。

知君用心如日月，事夫誓擬同生死。

還君明珠雙淚垂，恨不相逢未嫁時。

英译 English translation

Song of a Faithful Wife

Jié Fù Yín
Zhang Ji · Tang · Yuefu ballad

You knew I had a husband — still, you sent

A pair of shining pearls to me.

Moved by the love and tenderness they meant,

I tied them to my red silk robe, to see.

My family's tower rises by the royal park;

My husband bears a halberd in the Bright Hall's guard.

I know your heart is clear as sun and moon,

But I've vowed to share my husband's life and death, however hard.

I give you back your pearls; two tears fall down —

If only we had met before I wore the bridal crown!

About this poem

On the surface, a faithful wife gently refusing a suitor's pearls. In fact it is a political allegory: Zhang Ji was declining an invitation to serve the powerful warlord Li Shidao, who was defying the Tang court — the "husband" is the emperor. 恨不相逢未嫁时, "if only we had met before I married", became the classic line for love that came too late.

Earlier translations 旧译

“The Chaste Wife's Reply”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Knowing, fair sir, my matrimonial thrall,

Two pearls thou sentest me, costly withal.

And I, seeing that Love thy heart possessed,

I wrapped them fondly in my silken vest.

For mine is a household of high degree,

My husband captain in the King's army;

And one with wit like thine should say,

"The troth of wives is for ever and ay."

With thy two pearls I send thee back two tears:

Tears—that we did not meet in earlier years!

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain. Lightly adapted: "coldly" changed to "fondly" in line 4 (in the Chinese she is touched and fastens the pearls on her jacket).

“The Retort Courteous”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

That I am duly married, assuredly you know,

And yet to me you send as gift twin pearls of mystic glow.

For this your kind devotion my heart must grateful be.

I hung within my red silk vest those pearls I might not show.

My dwelling is a lofty one within a stately dome.

My husband is a soldier who guards the Emperor's home.

I recognize your love as bright as shining sun or moon:

Yet swear to serve my husband, and never from him roam.

With your bright pearls I send again twin tears as crystal clear,

Regretting that we had not met ere Fortune placed me here.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
