== 静夜思 | Quiet Night Thoughts
by: 李白
form: 五绝
tags: 思乡 月 夜 羁旅
scene: night moon window frost
床前明月光，疑是地上霜。
举头望明月，低头思故乡。
--
Before my bed there gleams a silver light —
Could it be frost that settles on the ground?
I raise my head: the moon is shining bright;
I bow my head: my thoughts are homeward bound.
--
The best-known poem in the Chinese language; nearly every child can recite it. Far from home, the traveller wakes to moonlight so pale he takes it for frost. The same moon shines over his distant hometown, and the simple movement of raising and lowering his head carries him there. (床 may be a bed, or — some scholars argue — the railing of a well in the courtyard.)

== 望庐山瀑布 | Viewing the Waterfall at Mount Lu
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 山水 山 天空
scene: day sun peaks waterfall mist
日照香炉生紫烟，遥看瀑布挂前川。
飞流直下三千尺，疑是银河落九天。
--
Sunlight on Censer Peak sets violet mist aglow;
Far off, a waterfall hangs on the stream below.
It flies three thousand feet straight down through air —
The Milky Way, it seems, poured from the Ninth Heaven there.
--
Censer Peak (香炉峰) on Mount Lu in Jiangxi is named for its shape and the incense-like mists that rise from it. "Three thousand feet" is Li Bai's typical grand exaggeration. The Chinese call the Milky Way the "Silver River" (银河), and the Ninth Heaven is the highest of the heavens — so the waterfall becomes the river of stars tumbling to earth.

== 早发白帝城 | Leaving Baidi at Dawn
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 江河 山 羁旅 人生
scene: dawn clouds peaks river boat
朝辞白帝彩云间，千里江陵一日还。
两岸猿声啼不住，轻舟已过万重山。
--
At dawn I leave White Emperor town, high in the rosy cloud;
A thousand li to Jiangling — and home within a day.
From both the banks the gibbons cry, unceasing and aloud;
My little boat has slipped ten thousand peaks away.
--
Written in 759. Li Bai, exiled to the far southwest for his part in a prince's rebellion, received a pardon at Baidi, high above the Yangtze. The headlong rush down the Three Gorges mirrors his joy: the boat is "light" (轻舟) in more ways than one, and the gibbons' cries — usually the saddest sound in Chinese poetry — are left behind.

== 黄鹤楼送孟浩然之广陵 | Seeing Meng Haoran Off to Guangling at Yellow Crane Tower
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 离别 友情 江河 春
scene: spring tower river sail willow
故人西辞黄鹤楼，烟花三月下扬州。
孤帆远影碧空尽，唯见长江天际流。
--
My old friend bids the Yellow Crane Tower in the west goodbye;
In the third month's mist of blossom, down to Yangzhou he goes.
His lonely sail, a fading shade, is lost in the blue sky;
Now only the Long River to the edge of heaven flows.
--
Meng Haoran, twelve years older, was a poet Li Bai idolised. The poem names no feeling at all — Li Bai simply stands watching until the sail vanishes, and that long gaze is the friendship. Yangzhou (Guangling) in spring was the most glamorous city of the empire; 烟花 "mist and flowers" became a set phrase for its springtime.

== 赠汪伦 | For Wang Lun
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 友情 离别 江河
scene: spring river boat peach two
李白乘舟将欲行，忽闻岸上踏歌声。
桃花潭水深千尺，不及汪伦送我情。
--
I, Li Bai, am aboard my boat, about to sail away,
When suddenly from shore I hear a stamping, singing throng.
Though Peach Blossom Pool is a thousand feet deep, they say,
It's not so deep as Wang Lun's love that sees me off with song.
--
Wang Lun was a local gentleman of Jingxian (Anhui) who had invited the famous poet to stay. As Li Bai was leaving, Wang came down to the shore with villagers, singing and stamping out the rhythm (踏歌, "stepping song"). Naming himself in the first line is a cheerful informality almost unique in Tang poetry.

== 月下独酌（其一） | Drinking Alone by Moonlight
by: 李白
form: 五古
tags: 饮酒 月 夜 花 人生
scene: night moon flowers person jar cup
花间一壶酒，独酌无相亲。
举杯邀明月，对影成三人。
月既不解饮，影徒随我身。
暂伴月将影，行乐须及春。
我歌月徘徊，我舞影零乱。
醒时同交欢，醉后各分散。
永结无情游，相期邈云汉。
--
Among the flowers, a jug of wine;
I pour alone, with no one nigh.
I lift my cup to invite the moon —
With my shadow, we make three: moon, shade and I.
But the moon has never learned to drink,
And my shadow only does what I do;
Yet for a while, with moon and shadow,
Let's revel while the spring is new.
I sing — the moon lingers and sways;
I dance — my shadow reels and sprawls.
Sober, we share our joy together;
Drunk, we each go where slumber calls.
Let us pledge a friendship free of worldly ties,
To meet again beyond the Milky Way's far skies.
--
The first of Li Bai's four poems "Drinking Alone by Moonlight". Loneliness becomes a party of three, then dissolves back into solitude. The closing pledge of a 无情游 — a companionship beyond human feeling, with moon and shadow who have none — to be renewed in the Milky Way (云汉), makes his two silent friends his companions for eternity.

== 将进酒 | Bring in the Wine
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 饮酒 人生 时光 友情 江河
scene: night moon river mountains cup jar two
君不见黄河之水天上来，奔流到海不复回。
君不见高堂明镜悲白发，朝如青丝暮成雪。
人生得意须尽欢，莫使金樽空对月。
天生我材必有用，千金散尽还复来。
烹羊宰牛且为乐，会须一饮三百杯。
岑夫子，丹丘生，将进酒，杯莫停。
与君歌一曲，请君为我倾耳听。
钟鼓馔玉不足贵，但愿长醉不复醒。
古来圣贤皆寂寞，惟有饮者留其名。
陈王昔时宴平乐，斗酒十千恣欢谑。
主人何为言少钱，径须沽取对君酌。
五花马，千金裘，呼儿将出换美酒，与尔同销万古愁。
--
Have you not seen the Yellow River's waters, sent from heaven's height,
Rush to the sea and never more return?
Have you not seen, in lofty halls, the mirror mourn hair turned to white —
Black silk at dawn, by dusk like snow? Then learn:
When life goes well, enjoy it to the full;
Don't let your golden goblet face the moon in vain.
Heaven made my talents — they will find their use;
Spend a thousand gold — the gold will come again.
Roast the lamb and kill the ox, and let us make merry —
Three hundred cups at a single sitting we must drain!
Master Cen, and Danqiu, my friend,
Bring in the wine — don't let your cups stand still!
Let me sing a song for you —
Lend me your ears, if you will:
Bells and drums and jade-fine feasts — what are they worth?
I only wish to stay drunk, and never wake again.
The sages and the worthies all lie lonely in the earth;
Only the great drinkers' names remain.
The Prince of Chen once feasted in the Pingle Hall,
Ten thousand cash a cask, carousing without end.
Why does our host say he is short of money? Go and buy it all,
And fill our cups, so we can drink, my friend!
My dappled horse,
My fur worth a thousand gold —
Call the boy, take them out, and trade them for fine wine,
And with you I'll drown the sorrows of ten thousand years untold!
--
"Bring in the Wine" (an old Han ballad title) was written around 752 as Li Bai drank with his friends Cen Xun and Yuan Danqiu. It opens with two images of time's unstoppable flow — the Yellow River pouring from heaven to the sea, hair turning white in a single day — and answers them with defiant joy and boundless self-belief. The Prince of Chen is the poet Cao Zhi, famous for his lavish banquets at Pingle Lodge.

== 蜀道难 | The Road to Shu Is Hard
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 山 山水 羁旅 天空
scene: day peaks clouds pine waterfall mist birds
噫吁嚱，危乎高哉！
蜀道之难，难于上青天！
蚕丛及鱼凫，开国何茫然！
尔来四万八千岁，不与秦塞通人烟。
西当太白有鸟道，可以横绝峨眉巅。
地崩山摧壮士死，然后天梯石栈相钩连。
上有六龙回日之高标，下有冲波逆折之回川。
黄鹤之飞尚不得过，猿猱欲度愁攀援。
青泥何盘盘，百步九折萦岩峦。
扪参历井仰胁息，以手抚膺坐长叹。
问君西游何时还？畏途巉岩不可攀。
但见悲鸟号古木，雄飞雌从绕林间。
又闻子规啼夜月，愁空山。
蜀道之难，难于上青天，使人听此凋朱颜！
连峰去天不盈尺，枯松倒挂倚绝壁。
飞湍瀑流争喧豗，砯崖转石万壑雷。
其险也如此，嗟尔远道之人胡为乎来哉！
剑阁峥嵘而崔嵬，一夫当关，万夫莫开。
所守或匪亲，化为狼与豺。
朝避猛虎，夕避长蛇；磨牙吮血，杀人如麻。
锦城虽云乐，不如早还家。
蜀道之难，难于上青天，侧身西望长咨嗟！
--
Ah! Oh! How steep! How high!
The road to Shu is hard — harder than climbing the blue sky!
Cancong and Yufu, who founded the land —
How dim and distant, those days, to understand!
For forty-eight thousand years since then,
No smoke of hearths crossed to the Qin frontier's men.
West, by Mount Taibai, runs a track only birds can trace,
That cuts across to Emei's summit face.
Then the earth split, the mountain fell, the strong men died,
And ladders to heaven, stone plank-roads, were linked along the side.
Above, a peak so high the sun's six dragons turn their car;
Below, the rapids dash and twist where the whirling eddies are.
The yellow crane in flight can never cross that height;
The gibbons long to pass, and dread the climb outright.
How Green Mud Ridge goes coiling, coiling round —
Nine turns in a hundred steps, through crags it's wound!
You touch Orion, pass the Well Star, gasp and look on high;
You sit and clutch your chest, and heave a long, long sigh.
I ask you, traveller to the west, when will you come back home?
The road is fearful, sheer the cliffs — no foot can overcome.
You see only mournful birds that cry in ancient trees,
The male in front, the female after, circling through the leaves;
You hear the cuckoo weep beneath the moon at night,
Grieving the empty hills.
The road to Shu is hard — harder than climbing the blue sky!
Just hearing of it, rosy cheeks would fade and youth would fly.
Peak after peak, not a foot from heaven's floor,
Dead pines hang upside down from cliffs they cling to more and more.
The flying torrents, roaring falls, compete in their uproar;
They pound the cliffs and roll the rocks — ten thousand gorges' thunder-roar.
Such is its peril!
Alas, you traveller from afar, why ever come this way?
Sword Gate Pass looms, jagged and steep:
One man can hold it — ten thousand cannot break in to sweep.
And if its guards are not men you can trust,
They'll turn to wolves and jackals, as such men must.
By day beware the tiger, by night beware the snake —
They grind their teeth and suck men's blood, and kill like reapers' rake.
Though Brocade City may be fair, they say,
Better go home without delay.
The road to Shu is hard — harder than climbing the blue sky!
I turn and gaze toward the west, and heave a long, long sigh.
--
Shu is today's Sichuan, cut off from the central plain by mountain ranges crossed only on plank roads pinned to cliff faces. Li Bai, a son of Shu, mixes legend (the ancient kings Cancong and Yufu; five strongmen killed when a mountain collapsed as they dragged a snake from its cave; the six dragons pulling the sun's chariot) with real geography, in wildly irregular lines that lurch like the road itself. When He Zhizhang read it, he called Li Bai "an immortal banished from heaven".

== 行路难（其一） | Hard Is the Road (I)
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 人生 壮志 失意 饮酒
scene: winter snow mountains river sail person
金樽清酒斗十千，玉盘珍羞直万钱。
停杯投箸不能食，拔剑四顾心茫然。
欲渡黄河冰塞川，将登太行雪满山。
闲来垂钓碧溪上，忽复乘舟梦日边。
行路难，行路难，多歧路，今安在？
长风破浪会有时，直挂云帆济沧海。
--
Clear wine in cups of gold, ten thousand cash a measure;
Rare dishes on a plate of jade, worth ten thousand more —
I set my cup aside, cast down my chopsticks — no pleasure;
I draw my sword and look around, heart lost, unsure.
I'd cross the Yellow River — ice has choked its flow;
I'd climb the Taihang Mountains — snow lies deep on every height.
Then let me fish, as the old sage did, where green brooks go,
Or dream, like Yi Yin, of sailing toward the sun's own light.
Hard is the road! Hard is the road!
So many forks — and where am I now?
A day will come to ride the wind and break the waves:
I'll hoist my cloud-high sail and cross the boundless sea somehow.
--
Written in 744 when Li Bai left the court at Chang'an, his hopes of a political career in ruins. Ice and snow block every way forward. The fisherman is Lü Shang, who fished in obscurity until King Wen of Zhou discovered him; Yi Yin dreamed of sailing past the sun just before King Tang employed him — both sages who waited for their moment. The famous last couplet turns despair into confidence.

== 望天门山 | Gazing at Tianmen Mountain
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 山水 江河 山
scene: day sun mountains river sail
天门中断楚江开，碧水东流至此回。
两岸青山相对出，孤帆一片日边来。
--
The Gate of Heaven splits in two; the Chu River bursts through;
Its blue-green waters, flowing east, here swirl and turn around.
From either bank the green hills stride out, facing, into view —
And from beside the sun there comes a single sail, without a sound.
--
Tianmen ("Heaven's Gate") is a pair of hills facing each other across the Yangtze in Anhui. Sailing downstream, Li Bai sees the hills seem to step forward to meet him as his boat approaches — a moving-camera view, a thousand years before cinema.

== 独坐敬亭山 | Sitting Alone on Jingting Mountain
by: 李白
form: 五绝
tags: 山 隐逸 人生 闲适
scene: day mountains clouds birds person pine
众鸟高飞尽，孤云独去闲。
相看两不厌，只有敬亭山。
--
The flocks of birds fly high and disappear;
A lonely cloud drifts off without a care.
We gaze and gaze, and never tire of it —
Just Jingting Mountain, and I, sitting there.
--
Written in 753 near Xuancheng, when Li Bai was past fifty and long out of favour. Birds and clouds — like friends and fortune — all leave him; only the mountain stays, and the two regard each other like old friends who need no words.

== 夜宿山寺 | Staying the Night at a Mountain Temple
by: 李白
form: 五绝
tags: 夜 天空 禅 童趣
scene: night stars temple mountains crescent
危楼高百尺，手可摘星辰。
不敢高声语，恐惊天上人。
--
The towering pagoda soars a hundred feet in height;
From here my hand could pluck the stars of night.
I dare not raise my voice above a sigh,
For fear of waking those who dwell on high.
--
A temple tower on a mountaintop, so high that the stars are within reach and heaven's residents might overhear. Built entirely from playful exaggeration, it is one of the first poems Chinese children learn.

== 峨眉山月歌 | Song of the Moon over Mount Emei
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 月 秋 江河 思乡 羁旅
scene: night autumn crescent mountains river boat
峨眉山月半轮秋，影入平羌江水流。
夜发清溪向三峡，思君不见下渝州。
--
Over Mount Emei hangs the autumn half-moon's light;
Its image floats along the Pingqiang's flowing stream.
I leave Clear Creek for the Three Gorges in the night;
I miss you — you are lost to sight — and drift to Yuzhou in a dream.
--
Written around 725 when the young Li Bai first left his home province of Shu. In four lines he names five places — Emei, the Pingqiang River, Clear Creek, the Three Gorges, Yuzhou — yet the poem reads as one smooth glide downstream. The "you" he misses may be the moon itself, hidden now behind the gorge cliffs, or a friend left behind.

== 春夜洛城闻笛 | Hearing a Flute on a Spring Night in Luoyang
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 思乡 春 夜 音乐 柳
scene: night spring moon willow tower
谁家玉笛暗飞声，散入春风满洛城。
此夜曲中闻折柳，何人不起故园情。
--
Whose jade flute sends its notes in secret flight,
Scattered by spring winds through Luoyang's every street?
Hearing "Breaking Willows" in the tune tonight,
Whose heart would not for home and garden beat?
--
"Breaking Willows" (折杨柳) was a farewell tune: friends parting gave each other a willow twig, because 柳 (willow) sounds like 留 (stay). Hearing it played by an unseen flautist in the spring night, the poet realises the whole city must be thinking of home.

== 送友人 | Seeing a Friend Off
by: 李白
form: 五律
tags: 离别 友情 山
scene: dusk sun hills river wall rider two
青山横北郭，白水绕东城。
此地一为别，孤蓬万里征。
浮云游子意，落日故人情。
挥手自兹去，萧萧班马鸣。
--
Green hills lie stretched beyond the northern wall;
White water winds around the eastern town.
Once we have parted here, you'll be a lonely weed
Ten thousand li along the road, wind-blown.
The drifting clouds — a wanderer's mind;
The setting sun — an old friend's heart.
We wave our hands, and from this place you go;
Your horse, leaving the herd, neighs as we part.
--
A model regulated poem, its middle couplets perfectly parallel. The drifting cloud is the departing friend, the reluctant setting sun the friend left behind. The last line borrows from the *Book of Songs*: 班马 is a horse separated from its companions, crying out — the animals feel the parting too.

== 渡荆门送别 | Farewell After Crossing Jingmen
by: 李白
form: 五律
tags: 江河 山水 思乡 羁旅 月
scene: night moon clouds river boat hills
渡远荆门外，来从楚国游。
山随平野尽，江入大荒流。
月下飞天镜，云生结海楼。
仍怜故乡水，万里送行舟。
--
I've crossed afar, beyond the Jingmen Gate,
To roam among the lands of ancient Chu.
The hills give way as the broad plains begin;
The River pours into the wilds — a boundless view.
The moon drops down, a mirror flying through the sky;
Clouds rise and build sea-towers out of the blue.
Still I love my native river, that has come
Ten thousand li to see my boat off, as friends do.
--
Li Bai at about twenty-four, leaving Sichuan for the first time. Past the Jingmen gorge the mountains suddenly end and the Yangtze spreads into the plains of Hubei. The moon in the water is a "flying mirror", the clouds a mirage of "sea towers". The "farewell" of the title is the river from home seeing him off.

== 闻王昌龄左迁龙标遥有此寄 | On Hearing That Wang Changling Was Demoted to Longbiao
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 友情 离别 月 春
scene: night moon willow mountains river
杨花落尽子规啼，闻道龙标过五溪。
我寄愁心与明月，随君直到夜郎西。
--
The willow-down has fallen, and the cuckoos weep;
I hear you pass Five Streams, to Longbiao sent away.
I give my grieving heart into the bright moon's keep,
To follow you to the west of Yelang all the way.
--
Wang Changling, a fellow poet and friend, was banished to remote Longbiao (in today's Hunan) around 748. The cuckoo's cry was heard as 不如归去, "better go home". Unable to follow, Li Bai entrusts his heart to the moon, which shines on both of them — one of the best-loved images of friendship in Chinese.

== 清平调（其一） | Qingping Song (I)
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 花 春 女性 爱情
scene: night spring moon palace flowers clouds lady
云想衣裳花想容，春风拂槛露华浓。
若非群玉山头见，会向瑶台月下逢。
--
Clouds bring her robes to mind, and flowers, her face;
The spring wind strokes the rail, the dew lies thick and bright.
If she is not a goddess seen on Jade Mountain's height,
She must be one you'd meet on Jasper Terrace by moonlight.
--
Written to order around 743, when Emperor Xuanzong and his beloved Consort Yang were admiring peonies at the Aloeswood Pavilion. Li Bai, reportedly still drunk, dashed off three songs. In this one the consort and the peony blur into each other, and both into a goddess of the Queen Mother of the West's paradise (Jade Mountain, Jasper Terrace).

== 子夜吴歌·秋歌 | Midnight Song of Wu: Autumn
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 相思 秋 月 战争 闺怨
scene: night autumn moon wall wind lady
长安一片月，万户捣衣声。
秋风吹不尽，总是玉关情。
何日平胡虏，良人罢远征。
--
One sheet of moonlight over Chang'an lies;
From ten thousand homes the fulling-mallets pound.
The autumn wind blows on and never dies —
All of it heartache, for the Jade Gate bound.
When will we crush the raiders and have peace,
So my good man's long campaigns may cease?
--
Women beat cloth on stones to soften it before sewing winter clothes for husbands at the frontier, so the sound of "pounding clothes" (捣衣) was the sound of autumn longing. The Jade Gate Pass (玉门关) was the gateway to the western frontier. Li Bai turns an old southern love-song form into the voice of a whole city's grief.

== 关山月 | The Moon over the Mountain Pass
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 边塞 战争 月 相思
scene: night moon clouds mountains fort wind
明月出天山，苍茫云海间。
长风几万里，吹度玉门关。
汉下白登道，胡窥青海湾。
由来征战地，不见有人还。
戍客望边色，思归多苦颜。
高楼当此夜，叹息未应闲。
--
The bright moon rises over Tianshan's height,
Amid a boundless sea of clouds and haze.
A long wind sweeps ten thousand li of night,
And blows through Jade Gate Pass along its ways.
Han armies marched down Baideng's road;
The Hu still eye the bays of Qinghai's shore.
From these old battlefields, as all men know,
Not one has ever come back home from war.
The soldiers gaze upon the frontier scene,
Their faces bitter, longing to return;
In high towers on this night, their wives, unseen,
Must sigh and sigh, and cannot rest, but yearn.
--
An old ballad title, reworked on an immense scale: the moon over the Tianshan range, a wind blowing ten thousand li to the Jade Gate. Baideng is where the Han founder was besieged by the Xiongnu; Qinghai Lake was a Tang–Tibetan battleground. The last couplet cuts from the frontier to the wives in their towers far away, watching the same moon.

== 玉阶怨 | Jade Stairs Lament
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 闺怨 月 秋 夜 相思
scene: night autumn moon palace window lady
玉阶生白露，夜久侵罗袜。
却下水晶帘，玲珑望秋月。
--
White dew is forming on the jade stair;
The night grows long; it soaks her silken hose.
She lowers the crystal curtain — there,
Through its glinting beads, the autumn moon still glows.
--
A palace lament in which nothing is said of feeling. A woman has waited on the steps so long that the dew soaks her stockings; she goes in and lowers the blind — and still cannot stop gazing at the moon through it. Ezra Pound admired the poem for saying everything by implication.

== 宣州谢朓楼饯别校书叔云 | Farewell to Secretary Shuyun at Xie Tiao's Tower in Xuanzhou
by: 李白
form: 七古
tags: 饮酒 人生 失意 离别 秋
scene: autumn day geese tower river mountains wind
弃我去者，昨日之日不可留；
乱我心者，今日之日多烦忧。
长风万里送秋雁，对此可以酣高楼。
蓬莱文章建安骨，中间小谢又清发。
俱怀逸兴壮思飞，欲上青天揽明月。
抽刀断水水更流，举杯销愁愁更愁。
人生在世不称意，明朝散发弄扁舟。
--
What has abandoned me —
Yesterday — I cannot keep;
What troubles my heart —
Today — its cares are deep.
The long wind speeds the autumn geese ten thousand li away;
Facing this, in the high tower, let us drink our fill today.
Your prose has Penglai's grace, my verse the bones of Jian'an's age,
With Little Xie's clear freshness in between, upon the page.
We both are full of soaring moods and thoughts in lofty flight —
We'd climb the azure sky to pluck the moon so bright.
I draw my sword to cut the stream — the stream flows on the more;
I raise my cup to drown my grief — my grief grows all the more.
If life in this world will not go the way we planned,
Tomorrow, hair unpinned, I'll take a skiff and leave the land.
--
Xie Tiao (464–499), a poet Li Bai revered, built this tower in Xuanzhou. "Penglai writing" alludes to the Han imperial library, "Jian'an bones" to the vigour of Cao Cao's era — praise for his kinsman's prose and his own verse. "Cutting water with a sword" became a proverb for sorrow that cannot be stopped; unpinning the hair meant leaving official life for a free one.

== 梦游天姥吟留别 | A Dream Journey to Mount Tianmu: A Farewell
by: 李白
form: 七古
tags: 山 山水 人生 隐逸 壮志
scene: night moon peaks clouds waterfall lake mist crane
海客谈瀛洲，烟涛微茫信难求；
越人语天姥，云霞明灭或可睹。
天姥连天向天横，势拔五岳掩赤城。
天台四万八千丈，对此欲倒东南倾。
我欲因之梦吴越，一夜飞度镜湖月。
湖月照我影，送我至剡溪。
谢公宿处今尚在，渌水荡漾清猿啼。
脚著谢公屐，身登青云梯。
半壁见海日，空中闻天鸡。
千岩万转路不定，迷花倚石忽已暝。
熊咆龙吟殷岩泉，栗深林兮惊层巅。
云青青兮欲雨，水澹澹兮生烟。
列缺霹雳，丘峦崩摧。
洞天石扉，訇然中开。
青冥浩荡不见底，日月照耀金银台。
霓为衣兮风为马，云之君兮纷纷而来下。
虎鼓瑟兮鸾回车，仙之人兮列如麻。
忽魂悸以魄动，恍惊起而长嗟。
惟觉时之枕席，失向来之烟霞。
世间行乐亦如此，古来万事东流水。
别君去兮何时还？且放白鹿青崖间，须行即骑访名山。
安能摧眉折腰事权贵，使我不得开心颜！
--
Seafarers tell of Yingzhou, isle of the immortals' rest —
Through misty waves so dim and far, it's truly hard to find;
But men of Yue speak of Mount Tianmu, whose crest
In flickering cloud and rosy light may sometimes be divined.
Tianmu joins the sky and lies across the sky's expanse;
It overtops the Five Great Peaks and hides the Red Wall Hill;
Tiantai, forty-eight thousand feet, at a single glance
Seems to topple southeast before it, bowing lower still.
So, longing for Wu and Yue, I dreamed — and in one night
Flew over Mirror Lake beneath the moon.
The lake-moon lit my shadow, and it led me with its light
To Shan Creek, where Lord Xie once lodged; it stands there still,
With clear green waters rippling, and the gibbons' crying shrill.
I put on Lord Xie's climbing clogs
And mounted the ladder of blue cloud.
Halfway up the cliff I saw the sun rise from the sea,
And heard the Heavenly Rooster crow aloud.
A thousand crags, ten thousand turns — the path would twist and stray;
Lost among flowers, leaning on rocks, I found the light had gone away.
Bears roared and dragons chanted, booming through the rocks and springs;
The deep woods shook, the topmost peaks were startled at such things.
The clouds were dark and heavy, close to rain;
The waters rippled, breathing mist again.
Then lightning split the sky, and thunder roared;
The hills and ridges crumbled and collapsed.
The stone gates of the grotto-heaven
With a crash flew open wide.
A blue void, vast and fathomless, lay beyond;
Sun and moon shone on terraces of gold and silver inside.
With rainbows for their robes and winds for horses,
The Lords of the Clouds came streaming down in throngs;
Tigers struck the zithers, phoenixes drew the carriages round;
Immortals stood in ranks, as thick as hemp upon the ground.
Then suddenly my soul was shaken, my spirit stirred —
I started up awake with a long sigh;
There was only the pillow and the mat I woke upon;
The mist and rosy clouds of before were gone.
The pleasures of this world are just the same:
Since ancient times, all things flow east like water, and are gone.
I leave you now — when shall I return?
For now I'll set my white deer free among the green cliffs to roam,
And ride it, when I must, to visit the famous mountains, my true home.
How could I bow my brows and bend my back to serve the great,
And never let my heart be glad, and never let my face be bright?
--
Written in 745 as Li Bai left eastern Shandong for the south, a year after leaving the court. The dream climbs Mount Tianmu in Zhejiang in the footsteps of Xie Lingyun (Lord Xie), the 5th-century landscape poet who invented special clogs for mountain climbing, and bursts into a vision of an immortals' paradise — which vanishes on waking. The moral explodes in the last line: no worldly glory is worth bending the back to the powerful. It became a creed for independent spirits.

== 登金陵凤凰台 | Climbing Phoenix Terrace at Jinling
by: 李白
form: 七律
tags: 怀古 江河 山 失意
scene: day clouds tower river hills sail
凤凰台上凤凰游，凤去台空江自流。
吴宫花草埋幽径，晋代衣冠成古丘。
三山半落青天外，二水中分白鹭洲。
总为浮云能蔽日，长安不见使人愁。
--
On Phoenix Terrace phoenixes once used to play;
The phoenixes are gone, the terrace bare; the river flows its way.
The flowers and grasses of the Wu palace bury hidden lanes;
The robes and caps of Jin are ancient mounds today.
The Three Peaks, half-dissolving, lean beyond the blue;
Two waters split around White Egret Isle midway.
It's all because the drifting clouds can hide the sun —
Chang'an is lost to sight, and grief has come to stay.
--
Jinling (Nanjing) had been the capital of six dynasties, all long vanished. Li Bai's poem deliberately echoes Cui Hao's *Yellow Crane Tower*, which he had admired. The ending turns from history to politics: the "drifting clouds" hiding the sun are the slanderers who cut Li Bai off from the emperor in Chang'an.

== 秋浦歌（其十五） | Autumn River Song (XV)
by: 李白
form: 五绝
tags: 时光 人生 秋 失意
scene: autumn river person mountains window
白发三千丈，缘愁似个长。
不知明镜里，何处得秋霜。
--
My white hair hangs three thousand yards —
So long, because my grief is long as well.
Into the bright mirror I stare:
Where did this autumn frost come from? I cannot tell.
--
From a series of seventeen songs on the Qiupu River in Anhui, written in Li Bai's fifties. "White hair three thousand *zhang*" (about 10 km!) is the most famous exaggeration in Chinese poetry, and it is somehow perfectly true to the feeling of seeing oneself suddenly old.

== 客中作 | Written as a Traveller
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 饮酒 羁旅 思乡
scene: dusk sun cup jar person cottage
兰陵美酒郁金香，玉碗盛来琥珀光。
但使主人能醉客，不知何处是他乡。
--
The fine wine of Lanling breathes of turmeric's scent;
Poured in a bowl of jade, it gleams with amber light.
If only my host can get his guest well drunk tonight,
I'll never know which land is foreign, or where I went.
--
Lanling (in Shandong) was famous for its wine, scented with 郁金 (turmeric or curcuma). Most travel poems are homesick; this one, from Li Bai's carefree younger days, cheerfully proposes that good enough wine makes anywhere home.

== 山中问答 | Question and Answer in the Mountains
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 隐逸 山 闲适 花
scene: spring peaks river petals peach person
问余何意栖碧山，笑而不答心自闲。
桃花流水窅然去，别有天地非人间。
--
You ask me why I dwell among the green hills here;
I smile and don't reply — my heart is calm and free.
Peach blossoms on the water drift away, and disappear:
There is another world, not of men, for me.
--
Peach blossoms floating on a stream recall Tao Yuanming's *Peach Blossom Spring*, a hidden paradise found by following petals upstream. The best answer to "why live in the mountains?" is a smile.

== 山中与幽人对酌 | Drinking with a Recluse in the Mountains
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 饮酒 友情 隐逸 音乐
scene: spring mountains flowers two cup jar
两人对酌山花开，一杯一杯复一杯。
我醉欲眠卿且去，明朝有意抱琴来。
--
We two sit drinking where the mountain flowers bloom —
One cup, another cup, and then another cup.
I'm drunk and want to sleep — be off now, leave the room!
Tomorrow, if you like, bring your zither, and come up.
--
Spontaneity itself: the second line simply repeats "one cup" three times. The third line borrows from Tao Yuanming, who used to tell his guests "I'm drunk and want to sleep — you may go" — the frankness of true friends.

== 古朗月行（节选） | Song of the Bright Moon (excerpt)
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 月 童趣 夜 天空
scene: night moon clouds pine tower
小时不识月，呼作白玉盘。
又疑瑶台镜，飞在青云端。
--
When I was small, I didn't know the moon —
I called it a white jade plate up in the sky,
Or thought it was the Jasper Terrace mirror,
Flying above the blue clouds, way up high.
--
The opening of a longer poem, recited by every Chinese child: a little boy's guesses at what the moon must be. The Jasper Terrace (瑶台) is the dwelling of immortals, so the moon is their mirror.

== 长干行（其一） | A Song of Changgan
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 爱情 相思 女性 离别 秋
scene: autumn river butterfly cottage maple lady
妾发初覆额，折花门前剧。
郎骑竹马来，绕床弄青梅。
同居长干里，两小无嫌猜。
十四为君妇，羞颜未尝开。
低头向暗壁，千唤不一回。
十五始展眉，愿同尘与灰。
常存抱柱信，岂上望夫台。
十六君远行，瞿塘滟滪堆。
五月不可触，猿声天上哀。
门前迟行迹，一一生绿苔。
苔深不能扫，落叶秋风早。
八月蝴蝶黄，双飞西园草。
感此伤妾心，坐愁红颜老。
早晚下三巴，预将书报家。
相迎不道远，直至长风沙。
--
When my hair was cut in bangs above my brow,
I played by the gate, picking flowers in the sun;
You came riding on your bamboo horse, and how
We circled the well-rail, tossing green plums, just for fun.
We lived together in the lane of Changgan,
Two little ones, without suspicion or care.
At fourteen I became your wife, my man —
So shy I never smiled, I'd hardly dare;
I hung my head and faced the shadowed wall;
A thousand times you called — I never turned at all.
At fifteen my brows unknit; I wished to be
With you as dust and ashes, endlessly.
I kept the faith of the man who held the pillar fast —
Why would I climb the Watching-for-Husband Tower at last?
At sixteen you went far away from me,
Past Qutang Gorge, where Yanyu's rocks rear high —
In the fifth month, no boat can touch them safely,
And the gibbons wail up in the sky.
Before the gate, the footprints that you left behind
Have each grown over, one by one, with moss of green;
The moss is deep, too deep to sweep, I find;
Leaves fall early in the autumn wind, I've seen.
In the eighth month, yellow butterflies,
In pairs, fly over the west garden's grass;
It wounds my heart to see them; my youth flies,
And I sit grieving as my looks fade and pass.
Sooner or later, when you come down from the Three Ba,
Send me a letter home ahead — I'll go and wait;
I won't say it's far: I'll come to meet you,
All the way to Long Wind Sands, beyond the gate.
--
A young merchant's wife writes to her husband trading far up the Yangtze. Her childhood with him gave Chinese two idioms for childhood sweethearts — 青梅竹马 "green plums and bamboo horse" and 两小无猜 "two little ones without suspicion". The "man who held the pillar" waited for his love under a bridge until the flood drowned him; the Watching-for-Husband Tower is where wives waited in vain. Ezra Pound's free version, *The River-Merchant's Wife: A Letter*, is one of the most famous translations in English.
