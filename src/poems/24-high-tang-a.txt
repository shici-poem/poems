== 次北固山下 | Mooring Below Beigu Mountain
by: 王湾
form: 五律
tags: 思乡 江河 羁旅 春
scene: dawn sun sea river sail hills geese
客路青山外，行舟绿水前。
潮平两岸阔，风正一帆悬。
海日生残夜，江春入旧年。
乡书何处达？归雁洛阳边。
--
My road runs on beyond the blue-green hills;
My boat sails on across the jade-green stream.
The tide is high; the banks spread far and wide;
The wind is fair; one sail hangs upright in the gleam.
The sea-sun rises while the night still lingers;
The river's spring arrives before the old year's through.
Where can my letter home be sent? —
By the wild geese flying north to Luoyang, carried through.
--
Written near Zhenjiang on the lower Yangtze. The third couplet — the sun born out of the sea while night is not yet over, spring arriving on the river before the old year has ended — so impressed the chief minister Zhang Yue that he hung it in his hall as a model of poetry. It captures time itself moving forward.

== 终南望余雪 | Gazing at the Lingering Snow on Zhongnan
by: 祖咏
form: 五绝
tags: 雪 冬 山
scene: dusk winter snow peaks clouds wall
终南阴岭秀，积雪浮云端。
林表明霁色，城中增暮寒。
--
Zhongnan's northern ridges rise in beauty proud;
The lingering snow floats high above the clouds.
The treetops glow with light as skies clear late;
And in the city, evening's cold grows great.
--
An examination poem: required to write twelve lines, Zu Yong stopped at four, saying "the meaning is complete". From Chang'an, the snow on the shaded northern slopes of the Zhongnan mountains catches the clearing sunset — and makes the city below feel colder.

== 黄鹤楼 | Yellow Crane Tower
by: 崔颢
form: 七律
tags: 思乡 怀古 江河 天空 鸟
scene: dusk tower river clouds crane grass mist
昔人已乘黄鹤去，此地空余黄鹤楼。
黄鹤一去不复返，白云千载空悠悠。
晴川历历汉阳树，芳草萋萋鹦鹉洲。
日暮乡关何处是？烟波江上使人愁。
--
The sage of old has ridden off upon the yellow crane;
Here only Yellow Crane Tower is left, standing alone.
The yellow crane, once gone, has never come again;
For a thousand years white clouds drift on, aimless, on their own.
Across the sunlit river, the trees of Hanyang are clear;
On Parrot Isle the fragrant grasses grow so thick and green.
The sun goes down — where is my home? It isn't here;
The mist upon the river's waves brings sorrow to the scene.
--
Legend says an immortal flew away from this spot in Wuhan on a yellow crane. Cui Hao's poem breaks the rules of regulated verse — "yellow crane" appears three times in four lines — yet it was hailed as the greatest seven-character regulated poem of the Tang. Li Bai, visiting the tower, is said to have sighed: "Before my eyes is a scene I cannot describe, for Cui Hao's poem is already above it."

== 长干行（崔颢） | Changgan Song (Cui Hao)
by: 崔颢
form: 五绝
tags: 爱情 女性 江河 思乡
scene: day river boat two
君家何处住？妾住在横塘。
停船暂借问，或恐是同乡。
--
"Where is your home, sir? Tell me, do —
I live at Hengtang, down by the shore.
I stopped my boat to ask you this:
Perhaps we're from the same town — I'm not sure."
--
A tiny drama in twenty characters: a girl on one boat calls out to a stranger on another, using the excuse of a shared hometown to start a conversation — shy, bold and charming at once.

== 题破山寺后禅院 | The Meditation Hall Behind Poshan Temple
by: 常建
form: 五律
tags: 禅 山 山水 鸟
scene: dawn sun temple pine flowers lake birds
清晨入古寺，初日照高林。
曲径通幽处，禅房花木深。
山光悦鸟性，潭影空人心。
万籁此都寂，但余钟磬音。
--
At early dawn I enter the old temple's gate;
The first rays of the sun light up the forest tall.
A winding path leads to a secret, hidden place,
Where flowers and trees grow deep round the meditation hall.
The mountain light delights the nature of the birds;
The pool's reflections empty out the hearts of men.
Here all the sounds of the world fall silent,
Save for the bell and the stone chime, now and then.
--
Poshan Temple is in Changshu, Jiangsu. 曲径通幽处, "a winding path leads to a secluded place", became a proverb and a principle of Chinese garden design. The poem moves from the outer landscape to inner stillness — the reflections in the pool empty the mind of desire — and ends with nothing but the temple bells.

== 逢雪宿芙蓉山主人 | Lodging with the Master of Lotus Mountain in Snow
by: 刘长卿
form: 五绝
tags: 雪 冬 夜 羁旅
scene: night winter snow mountains cottage wind person
日暮苍山远，天寒白屋贫。
柴门闻犬吠，风雪夜归人。
--
At sunset, the grey-blue mountains far away;
The sky is cold; the thatched hut, white and poor.
A dog barks at the brushwood gate — someone, late,
Comes home through wind and snow, at night, to the door.
--
Twenty characters, a complete short film: a traveller finds shelter at dusk in a poor mountain hut; in the night a dog barks — the master is coming home through the storm. 风雪夜归人, "a man coming home on a night of wind and snow", is one of the best-loved images in Chinese.

== 枫桥夜泊 | Night Mooring at Maple Bridge
by: 张继
form: 七绝
tags: 夜 秋 羁旅 月 江河
scene: night autumn crescent frost maple river boat temple crows
月落乌啼霜满天，江枫渔火对愁眠。
姑苏城外寒山寺，夜半钟声到客船。
--
The moon has set; crows caw; frost fills the sky;
By river maples and fishing fires, I lie in grief, half asleep.
Outside Gusu's walls, the Cold Mountain Temple stands nearby;
Its midnight bell rings out to the traveller's boat, slow and deep.
--
Written around 756 near Suzhou (Gusu) by a traveller fleeing the An Lushan rebellion. Sight, sound and feeling blend in the dark: setting moon, cawing crows, frost, red maples and fishing lights — and finally the bell. The poem made Hanshan ("Cold Mountain") Temple famous; crowds still gather there on New Year's Eve to hear its bell.

== 寒食 | Cold Food Festival
by: 韩翃
form: 七绝
tags: 节日 春 花 柳 怀古
scene: dusk spring palace willow petals smoke
春城无处不飞花，寒食东风御柳斜。
日暮汉宫传蜡烛，轻烟散入五侯家。
--
In the spring city, petals fly in every lane;
At Cold Food, the east wind bends the palace willows down.
At dusk the Han court sends out candles, lit again;
Their light smoke drifts into the Five Lords' mansions in the town.
--
At the Cold Food Festival, just before Qingming, no fires could be lit for three days; at the end, the palace sent new fire to favoured households. Only the "Five Lords" — the emperor's eunuch favourites — receive it first: a delicate satire hidden in a lovely spring picture. Emperor Dezong loved the poem so much that he appointed its author by quoting the first line.

== 渔歌子 | Fisherman's Song
by: 张志和
form: 词
tags: 隐逸 春 雨 江河 闲适
scene: spring rain mountains river peach fisher crane
西塞山前白鹭飞，桃花流水鳜鱼肥。
青箬笠，绿蓑衣，斜风细雨不须归。
--
Before Xisai Mountain, the white egrets wing;
Peach petals on the stream; the perch grow fat in spring.
A hat of green bamboo, a cape of green straw:
In slanting wind and drizzle — why go home at all?
--
An early *ci* lyric by "the Old Fisherman of the Misty Waves". The fisherman, free and content in any weather, is the ideal of the recluse; the emperor himself sent for paintings of the scene. 鳜鱼 is the mandarin fish, at its best in peach-blossom season.

== 金缕衣 | The Gold-Threaded Robe
by: 杜秋娘
form: 七绝
tags: 时光 人生 花 哲理
scene: spring flowers peach person
劝君莫惜金缕衣，劝君惜取少年时。
花开堪折直须折，莫待无花空折枝。
--
Don't treasure, I beg you, your robe of golden thread;
Treasure your youth instead, while it is here.
When flowers are ripe for picking, pick them, then —
Don't wait to snap bare branches when the flowers disappear.
--
Sung by Du Qiuniang, a singing girl of Jinling who became a consort in the Tang palace (the song is sometimes listed as anonymous). China's *carpe diem* — "gather ye rosebuds while ye may".
