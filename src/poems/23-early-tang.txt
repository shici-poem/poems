== 蝉 | The Cicada
by: 虞世南
form: 五绝
tags: 咏物 哲理 秋
scene: autumn day leaves pine
垂緌饮清露，流响出疏桐。
居高声自远，非是藉秋风。
--
With tasselled mouth it sips the clear, pure dew;
Its flowing song pours from the wutong, sparse and tall.
Its voice goes far because it dwells up high —
And not because the autumn wind has carried it at all.
--
The cicada's mouthparts look like the tassels of an official's cap, and it was believed to live on dew — so it is an upright official. A person of real worth is heard far and wide by his own merit, without needing powerful backing. One of the "three great cicada poems" of the Tang, with Luo Binwang's and Li Shangyin's.

== 咏鹅 | Ode to the Goose
by: 骆宾王
form: 五古
tags: 童趣 鸟 夏
scene: day summer lake birds willow
鹅，鹅，鹅，曲项向天歌。
白毛浮绿水，红掌拨清波。
--
Goose, goose, goose!
It curves its neck and sings up to the sky.
White feathers float on water green;
Red feet paddle the clear waves by.
--
Said to have been written by Luo Binwang at the age of seven, and the first poem many Chinese children learn: sound, shape and colour — white, green, red, clear — in eighteen characters.

== 送杜少府之任蜀州 | Seeing Off Assistant Magistrate Du to His Post in Shu
by: 王勃
form: 五律
tags: 离别 友情 哲理
scene: day wall mist river two rider
城阙辅三秦，风烟望五津。
与君离别意，同是宦游人。
海内存知己，天涯若比邻。
无为在歧路，儿女共沾巾。
--
The walls of Chang'an guard the lands of Qin;
Through wind and mist I see the Five Fords far away.
The pain of parting we both feel within —
Both officials, far from home, we wander and we stray.
As long as somewhere in the world a true friend lives,
The world's end is as near as the house next door.
So here, where the roads divide, let's not, like girls and boys,
Wet our handkerchiefs with tears, and weep the more.
--
Wang Bo's farewell to a friend posted to Sichuan (the "Five Fords" of the Min River). Instead of tears, comfort: 海内存知己，天涯若比邻 — "with a true friend anywhere in the world, the ends of the earth are like next door" — is one of the most quoted lines on friendship ever written.

== 风 | Wind
by: 李峤
form: 五绝
tags: 风 咏物 童趣 秋 春
scene: wind bamboo river leaves
解落三秋叶，能开二月花。
过江千尺浪，入竹万竿斜。
--
It shakes down leaves when autumn's late,
And opens flowers when spring's begun.
Over the river, it raises thousand-foot waves;
Into the bamboo — it bends ten thousand, every one.
--
A riddle-poem: the wind is never named, only shown through what it does. A favourite with Chinese children.

== 渡汉江 | Crossing the Han River
by: 宋之问
form: 五绝
tags: 思乡 羁旅 江河
scene: spring river boat person hills
岭外音书断，经冬复历春。
近乡情更怯，不敢问来人。
--
Beyond the Five Ridges, no word, no letter came;
Through winter, then through spring, I waited, all alone.
Now, nearing home, my heart grows timid, full of fear —
I dare not ask the travellers for news of home.
--
Exiled to the far south (beyond the Five Ridges) in 705, Song Zhiwen secretly fled north. Crossing the Han River near home, he is afraid to ask about his family — what if the news is bad? 近乡情更怯, "the nearer home, the more timid the heart", describes every long-delayed homecoming.

== 登幽州台歌 | Song on Climbing Youzhou Terrace
by: 陈子昂
form: 杂言
tags: 怀古 人生 失意 天空 时光
scene: dusk tower hills clouds person
前不见古人，后不见来者。
念天地之悠悠，独怆然而涕下。
--
Before me, I cannot see the ancients gone;
Behind me, I cannot see those yet to be.
I think of heaven and earth, which go on and on,
And alone, in sorrow, my tears fall free.
--
Youzhou Terrace (near Beijing) was said to be the Golden Terrace built by King Zhao of Yan in the 4th century BC to welcome men of talent. Chen Zi'ang, a frustrated adviser on a military campaign in 697, climbs it: the wise kings are gone, the future cannot be seen, and he stands alone in infinite time and space. One of the loneliest poems ever written.

== 回乡偶书（其一） | Coming Home (I)
by: 贺知章
form: 七绝
tags: 思乡 时光 人生 童趣
scene: spring cottage willow elder child
少小离家老大回，乡音无改鬓毛衰。
儿童相见不相识，笑问客从何处来。
--
I left home young, and come back old;
My accent's the same, but my hair's grown thin.
The children see me, a stranger to them all —
"Where are you from, sir?" they ask, with a grin.
--
He Zhizhang came home to Yuezhou (Zhejiang) in 744, aged eighty-five, after more than fifty years at court. The children's innocent question — asking a native where he comes from — holds all the gentle comedy and sadness of the poem.

== 咏柳 | Ode to the Willow
by: 贺知章
form: 七绝
tags: 春 柳 咏物 风
scene: spring willow wind river
碧玉妆成一树高，万条垂下绿丝绦。
不知细叶谁裁出，二月春风似剪刀。
--
A tree dressed up in green jade, standing tall,
Lets down ten thousand ribbons of green silk.
Who cut these slender leaves, so fine and small?
The spring wind of the second month — like scissors, cut them all.
--
A child's favourite: the willow as a young woman dressed in green jade with silken ribbons, and the surprising last line — the spring wind is a tailor's scissors.

== 春江花月夜 | Spring River in the Flower Moon Night
by: 张若虚
form: 七古
tags: 月 春 江河 相思 哲理 夜 花
scene: night spring moon river sea tower boat flowers mist
春江潮水连海平，海上明月共潮生。
滟滟随波千万里，何处春江无月明？
江流宛转遶芳甸，月照花林皆似霰。
空里流霜不觉飞，汀上白沙看不见。
江天一色无纤尘，皎皎空中孤月轮。
江畔何人初见月，江月何年初照人？
人生代代无穷已，江月年年秖相似。
不知江月待何人？
但见长江送流水。
白云一片去悠悠，青枫浦上不胜愁。
谁家今夜扁舟子，何处相思明月楼？
可怜楼上月裴回，应照离人妆镜台。
玉户帘中卷不去，捣衣砧上拂还来。
此时相望不相闻，愿逐月华流照君。
鸿雁长飞光不度，鱼龙潜跃水成文。
昨夜闲潭梦落花，可怜春半不还家。
江水流春去欲尽，江潭落月复西斜。
斜月沈沈藏海雾，碣石潇湘无限路。
不知乘月几人归，落月摇情满江树。
--
The spring river's tide flows level with the sea;
Upon the sea the bright moon rises with the tide.
It glitters on the waves ten thousand li or more —
Where on the spring river is the moonlight not spread wide?
The river winds and curls round fragrant meadowland;
On flowering woods the moonlight falls like sleet.
The flowing frost of light falls through the air unseen;
The white sand on the shoals has melted into it, complete.
River and sky are one colour, without a speck of dust;
Bright, bright in the empty air, the moon's lone wheel.
Who was the first by the river to see the moon?
And when did the river moon first shine on men?
Generation after generation, men live on without end;
The river moon looks the same, year after year, again.
I wonder whom the river moon has waited for so long —
I see the Long River speeding its waters on and on.
A single white cloud drifts slowly far away;
At Green Maple Shore, the sorrow is too much to bear.
Whose family is on a little boat tonight?
Where is the moonlit tower, where someone longs for him there?
How sadly the moon lingers above the tower,
Shining on the mirror-stand of the lonely one inside;
Within her jade door's curtain, it can't be rolled away;
On the fulling-stone, she brushes it off — it comes back to abide.
Now we gaze at it together, but cannot hear each other;
I wish I could follow the moonlight, to shine on you.
The wild geese fly far, but cannot carry the light;
The fish and dragons leap, and only ripples come through.
Last night by the quiet pool I dreamed of falling flowers;
Alas, spring is half gone, and still I'm not home.
The river carries spring away, now nearly gone;
Over the river pool, the moon sinks west alone.
The slanting moon sinks deep, hidden in the sea mist;
From Jieshi to Xiaoxiang, the roads are without end.
How many, I wonder, ride the moonlight home tonight?
The setting moon stirs feeling in the river trees that bend.
--
Only two of Zhang Ruoxu's poems survive, and this one was nearly forgotten until the Ming; the modern scholar Wen Yiduo called it "the poem above all poems, the peak above all peaks". It follows a single spring night from moonrise to moonset, moving from the beauty of the river to questions about eternity — who first saw the moon? when did it first shine on man? — and to the longing of separated lovers, a husband on a boat and a wife in her tower, whom the same moon joins and divides. Geese and fish were the legendary carriers of letters; even they cannot bring word.

== 望月怀远 | Gazing at the Moon, Longing for One Far Away
by: 张九龄
form: 五律
tags: 月 相思 夜 海
scene: night moon sea window person
海上生明月，天涯共此时。
情人怨遥夜，竟夕起相思。
灭烛怜光满，披衣觉露滋。
不堪盈手赠，还寝梦佳期。
--
Over the sea the bright moon rises high;
At the ends of the earth, we share this hour.
Lovers resent the long, long night;
All night their longing grows in power.
I snuff the candle — the moonlight fills the room;
I throw a robe on, feeling the dew grow deep.
I cannot give you handfuls of its gleam,
So back to bed — to meet you in my sleep.
--
海上生明月，天涯共此时 — "the bright moon rises over the sea; at the ends of the earth we share this moment" — is the line every Chinese person thinks of at the Mid-Autumn Festival when loved ones are far away. Written by Zhang Jiuling around 737, after his dismissal as chief minister.

== 感遇（其一） | Moved by What I Encounter (I)
by: 张九龄
form: 五古
tags: 咏物 哲理 花 春 秋
scene: spring flowers pine mountains
兰叶春葳蕤，桂华秋皎洁。
欣欣此生意，自尔为佳节。
谁知林栖者，闻风坐相悦。
草木有本心，何求美人折？
--
In spring the orchid leaves grow lush and green;
In autumn cassia blossoms shine so pure and bright.
They flourish, full of life, serene,
Each in its own season, as is right.
Who'd think the hermits living in the woods
Would catch their fragrance on the wind and love them so?
But plants have their own nature — why should they
Seek to be picked by a beauty, just for show?
--
The first of twelve poems written after Zhang Jiuling lost power in 737. Orchid and cassia are fragrant by their own nature, not to win admiration — like a person of virtue, who does not need to be "picked" by a ruler (the "beauty" of the old allegory). Integrity does not depend on recognition.
