== 青玉案·元夕 | Green Jade Table: Lantern Festival
by: 辛弃疾
form: 词
tags: 节日 爱情 夜 春
scene: night spring lantern stars flowers palace
东风夜放花千树，更吹落、星如雨。
宝马雕车香满路。凤箫声动，玉壶光转，一夜鱼龙舞。

蛾儿雪柳黄金缕，笑语盈盈暗香去。
众里寻他千百度，蓦然回首，那人却在，灯火阑珊处。
--
The east wind blows by night, and a thousand trees burst into flower,
And blows down showers of stars like rain.
Fine horses, carved carriages — the road is filled with scent.
The phoenix flutes are playing,
The jade-pot moon is turning,
All night the fish and dragon lanterns dance again.

In moth-wing ornaments and gold-threaded willow sprays,
The ladies pass, laughing, a faint perfume behind.
A thousand times I searched the crowd for her —
Then suddenly I turned my head,
And there she was,
Where the lantern light was dim, where few would find.
--
The Lantern Festival in Hangzhou: fireworks like flowering trees and falling stars, lanterns shaped like fish and dragons, women in their finery. Amid it all the poet seeks one person — and finds her apart from the crowd, where the lights are fading. Wang Guowei cited the last lines as the third and highest of the "three stages" of any great achievement: after long searching, sudden discovery. Many read "her" as the poet's own lonely integrity.

== 破阵子·为陈同甫赋壮词以寄之 | Breaking Through the Ranks: A Heroic Lyric for Chen Tongfu
by: 辛弃疾
form: 词
tags: 壮志 爱国 战争 边塞 时光
scene: night candle fort flag horse rider
醉里挑灯看剑，梦回吹角连营。
八百里分麾下炙，五十弦翻塞外声。沙场秋点兵。

马作的卢飞快，弓如霹雳弦惊。
了却君王天下事，赢得生前身后名。可怜白发生！
--
Drunk, I trim the lamp to gaze upon my sword;
I wake from dreams to bugles sounding through the camps.
The roast of the great ox is shared among the men;
The fifty-stringed zithers play the frontier's songs —
On autumn fields of battle, the troops are mustered, ranks on ranks.

The horses fly as swift as Dilu;
The bowstrings twang like thunder in the night.
To finish for my king the task of winning back the world,
And win a name in life, and after death —
But alas — my hair's gone white!
--
Written for his friend Chen Liang (Tongfu), another patriot who longed to recover the north. Xin Qiji, in his late forties and long kept from command, dreams of leading an army again — a whole battle in nine lines — and the last line drops him back into reality: an old man who will never get the chance. Dilu was the horse that leapt a river to save Liu Bei.

== 永遇乐·京口北固亭怀古 | Joy of Eternal Union: Remembering the Past at Beigu Pavilion, Jingkou
by: 辛弃疾
form: 词
tags: 怀古 爱国 壮志 战争 时光
scene: dusk sun river tower mountains flag
千古江山，英雄无觅，孙仲谋处。
舞榭歌台，风流总被，雨打风吹去。
斜阳草树，寻常巷陌，人道寄奴曾住。
想当年，金戈铁马，气吞万里如虎。

元嘉草草，封狼居胥，赢得仓皇北顾。
四十三年，望中犹记，烽火扬州路。
可堪回首，佛狸祠下，一片神鸦社鼓。
凭谁问：廉颇老矣，尚能饭否？
--
These rivers and these hills endure a thousand years,
But heroes are no longer found —
No Sun Quan, who once ruled here.
The dancing halls and singing towers, all their style,
Are beaten down by rain, and blown away by wind.
In the slanting sunlight, grass and trees,
An ordinary lane —
Where Jinu lived, they say.
I think of those years:
Gilded spears and armoured steeds,
His spirit swallowing ten thousand li like a tiger in his day.

In Yuanjia's reign, in reckless haste,
They tried to carve their name on far Langjuxu,
And won only a panicked glance back north, in fear.
Forty-three years —
I gaze, and still recall
The beacon fires along the Yangzhou road, so near.
How can I bear to look back now?
Below the shrine of the Tartar khan,
Sacred crows and village drums are all I hear.
Who will come and ask of me:
"Lian Po is old —
Can he still eat his fill, this year?"
--
1205: Xin Qiji, sixty-five, is given a post at last at Jingkou (Zhenjiang) on the Yangtze, as the court rashly prepares to attack the Jin. He recalls Sun Quan, who held the south against the north, and Liu Yu ("Jinu"), who marched north to retake the old capitals — and warns, through the disastrous rush north of the Yuanjia era (450), against an ill-prepared war. Forty-three years before, he had fled south through war-torn Yangzhou. Now the locals worship at a shrine to Foli, the northern khan who invaded — they have forgotten. And no one asks whether old Xin, like the aged general Lian Po, who ate a huge meal to prove he could still fight, is fit to serve.

== 西江月·夜行黄沙道中 | West River Moon: Walking at Night on the Yellow Sand Road
by: 辛弃疾
form: 词
tags: 田园 夏 夜 月 雨
scene: night summer moon stars fields cottage bridge birds
明月别枝惊鹊，清风半夜鸣蝉。
稻花香里说丰年，听取蛙声一片。

七八个星天外，两三点雨山前。
旧时茅店社林边，路转溪桥忽见。
--
The bright moon on a far branch startles a magpie;
At midnight, in the cool breeze, the cicadas sing.
In the scent of rice flowers, they talk of a good harvest's yield —
Listen: a chorus of frogs from everything.

Seven or eight stars beyond the sky;
Two or three drops of rain before the hill.
The old thatched inn by the shrine's woods —
The road turns at the creek bridge — and there it is still!
--
A night walk through the countryside near Shangrao, where Xin Qiji lived in enforced retirement. Magpie, cicadas, rice flowers and frogs make a summer night's music; a sudden shower sends him looking for shelter — and there, round the bend, is the old inn he had forgotten. Pure joy in small things.

== 清平乐·村居 | Pure Serene Music: Village Life
by: 辛弃疾
form: 词
tags: 田园 童趣 夏 闲适
scene: summer cottage river grass lotus elder lady child
茅檐低小，溪上青青草。
醉里吴音相媚好，白发谁家翁媪？

大儿锄豆溪东，中儿正织鸡笼。
最喜小儿亡赖，溪头卧剥莲蓬。
--
The thatched eaves are low and small;
Along the creek the grass grows green.
Tipsy, in soft Wu dialect, so sweet to hear —
Whose white-haired couple is this I've seen?

The eldest boy is hoeing beans east of the stream;
The middle one is weaving a coop for the hens.
But best of all, the youngest, the little rascal,
Sprawled by the creek, peeling lotus pods.
--
A happy farm family seen by the retired general: an old couple chatting tipsily in their soft local dialect, and three sons — two hard at work, and the youngest, a lazy scamp, lying by the creek snacking on lotus seeds. Every Chinese child learns it.

== 丑奴儿·书博山道中壁 | Ugly Slave: Written on a Wall on the Road to Mount Bo
by: 辛弃疾
form: 词
tags: 人生 秋 时光 失意
scene: autumn tower mountains person wind
少年不识愁滋味，爱上层楼。爱上层楼，为赋新词强说愁。

而今识尽愁滋味，欲说还休。欲说还休，却道天凉好个秋！
--
When young, I didn't know what sorrow tasted like, back then;
I loved to climb up high towers.
I loved to climb up high towers,
To write new songs, and fake a sorrow with my pen.

Now I have tasted every sorrow on the way,
I want to speak, but stop.
I want to speak, but stop —
And only say: "How cool it is — a fine autumn day!"
--
Written on a wall along the road to Mount Bo during his long retirement. The young poet, knowing nothing of grief, invented it to sound profound; the old man, who knows it all too well, can only talk about the weather. One of the most quoted lyrics in Chinese.

== 菩萨蛮·书江西造口壁 | Buddhist Barbarian: Written on a Wall at Zaokou, Jiangxi
by: 辛弃疾
form: 词
tags: 爱国 江河 山 失意 鸟
scene: dusk river mountains tower birds
郁孤台下清江水，中间多少行人泪。
西北望长安，可怜无数山。

青山遮不住，毕竟东流去。
江晚正愁余，山深闻鹧鸪。
--
Below Yugu Terrace runs the clear river's flow —
How many refugees' tears are mingled in its stream!
I gaze north-west toward Chang'an — but no:
Alas, the countless mountains block the scene.

The green hills cannot hold it back —
At last, it flows away east.
At evening on the river, my heart is sad;
Deep in the hills, the partridge cries, "You cannot go!"
--
Zaokou in Jiangxi, where in 1129 Jin cavalry pursued the Song empress dowager and refugees fled south. "Chang'an" stands for the lost northern capital. The mountains cannot stop the river — as obstacles cannot stop the patriot's will — but the partridge's cry, heard as "you cannot go on, brother", ends the poem in doubt.

== 南乡子·登京口北固亭有怀 | Southern Village: Thoughts on Climbing Beigu Pavilion at Jingkou
by: 辛弃疾
form: 词
tags: 怀古 爱国 江河 壮志
scene: day tower river mountains flag
何处望神州？满眼风光北固楼。
千古兴亡多少事？悠悠。不尽长江滚滚流。

年少万兜鍪，坐断东南战未休。
天下英雄谁敌手？曹刘。生子当如孙仲谋。
--
Where can I gaze upon the Central Land?
All I see is the view from Beigu's tower.
How many rises and falls through the ages, past understanding? —
On and on,
Like the endless Yangtze, rolling, hour after hour.

Young, he led ten thousand men in helmets,
And held the south-east, fighting on without rest.
Who among the heroes of the world could match him?
Cao and Liu alone.
"If I have a son," said Cao, "let him be like Sun Quan — the best."
--
Jingkou again, 1204. The "Divine Land" (神州) is the lost north. Sun Quan (Zhongmou), as a young man, held the south against Cao Cao and Liu Bei; Cao Cao himself said, "A son should be like Sun Zhongmou." The implied contrast with the feeble Song court is plain.

== 水龙吟·登建康赏心亭 | Water Dragon Chant: Climbing the Pavilion of the Joyful Heart at Jiankang
by: 辛弃疾
form: 词
tags: 爱国 壮志 失意 秋 江河
scene: dusk autumn sun tower river mountains geese
楚天千里清秋，水随天去秋无际。
遥岑远目，献愁供恨，玉簪螺髻。
落日楼头，断鸿声里，江南游子。
把吴钩看了，栏杆拍遍，无人会，登临意。

休说鲈鱼堪脍，尽西风，季鹰归未？
求田问舍，怕应羞见，刘郎才气。
可惜流年，忧愁风雨，树犹如此！
倩何人唤取，红巾翠袖，揾英雄泪！
--
A thousand li of southern sky in clear autumn's light;
The water runs with the sky; autumn has no end.
I gaze at the distant hills —
They offer only sorrow, bring only regret,
Like jade hairpins, like conch-shell knots of hair.
At sunset on the tower,
Among the cries of a lone wild goose,
A wanderer in the south am I.
I've stared at my Wu sword,
And slapped the railings all along —
But no one understands
Why I have climbed up here.

Don't tell me the perch is fine for slicing —
The west wind blows; yet has Zhang Han gone home?
To seek out fields and houses for myself
Would make me blush to face
Liu Bei and his great heart.
Alas, the years flow by,
In worry, wind and rain —
Even the trees grow old like this!
Whom can I ask to call
A girl in red scarf and green sleeves,
To wipe away a hero's tears?
--
Nanjing, 1174. Xin Qiji, a northerner who came south to fight, climbs the Pavilion of the Joyful Heart. The distant hills, like a woman's hairpins and topknots, offer only sorrow; he slaps every railing in frustration. He will not go home like Zhang Han, who resigned to eat the perch of his native river, nor buy land like the petty men Liu Bei despised. As the general Huan Wen said, seeing willows he had planted grown huge: "If even trees age like this, how can men bear it?" — and there is no one to wipe his tears.

== 贺新郎（甚矣吾衰矣） | Congratulating the Bridegroom (How Old I've Grown)
by: 辛弃疾
form: 词
tags: 人生 隐逸 饮酒 山 时光 壮志
scene: day mountains clouds window cup person wind
甚矣吾衰矣。
怅平生、交游零落，只今余几！
白发空垂三千丈，一笑人间万事。
问何物、能令公喜。
我见青山多妩媚，料青山、见我应如是。
情与貌，略相似。

一尊搔首东窗里。
想渊明、停云诗就，此时风味。
江左沉酣求名者，岂识浊醪妙理。
回首叫、云飞风起。
不恨古人吾不见，恨古人、不见吾狂耳。
知我者，二三子。
--
How old I've grown, how frail!
I grieve my lifelong friends have scattered far —
How many now remain?
My white hair hangs for nothing, three thousand yards long;
With one laugh I dismiss the world's affairs.
What could still make me glad, you ask?
I find the green mountains so charming —
And I'd guess they find me charming too.
In heart and looks,
We're much alike, we two.

A cup of wine; I scratch my head by the east window;
I think of Tao Yuanming, his "Lingering Clouds" just done —
He must have felt like this.
Those south of the River, drunk on fame —
What do they know of the deep wisdom of cloudy wine?
I turn and shout — the clouds fly, the wind rises!
I don't regret I never saw the ancients;
I regret the ancients never saw my wild delight.
Those who know me
Are just two or three.
--
Written in retirement around 1200, in a pavilion named after Tao Yuanming's poem "Lingering Clouds", which mourns absent friends. 我见青山多妩媚，料青山见我应如是 — "I find the green mountains charming; I expect they find me so too" — is among the most quoted lines Xin Qiji wrote: a lonely man finding his only equal in the mountains. "The clouds fly, the wind rises" echoes Liu Bang's *Song of the Great Wind*.
