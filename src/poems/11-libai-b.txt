== 把酒问月 | Holding My Cup, I Ask the Moon
by: 李白
form: 七古
tags: 月 饮酒 时光 人生 哲理
scene: night moon clouds sea person cup
青天有月来几时？我今停杯一问之。
人攀明月不可得，月行却与人相随。
皎如飞镜临丹阙，绿烟灭尽清辉发。
但见宵从海上来，宁知晓向云间没。
白兔捣药秋复春，嫦娥孤栖与谁邻？
今人不见古时月，今月曾经照古人。
古人今人若流水，共看明月皆如此。
唯愿当歌对酒时，月光长照金樽里。
--
How long has the moon been in the blue sky? Here,
I put my cup down for a while to ask it so.
Men try to climb up to the moon — it's never near;
Yet as they walk, the moon walks with them where they go.
Bright as a flying mirror over the crimson gate,
When the green mists all have melted, it pours out its clear light.
We see it rising from the sea each night, but late,
Who knows where it sinks among the clouds at dawn's first light?
The white hare pounds its herbs from autumn into spring;
Chang'e lives alone — who is her neighbour there?
People today never saw the ancient moon a-shining,
Yet this moon once shone on the ancients, just as fair.
The ancients and the moderns flow on like a stream,
And all have watched the bright moon, just as we.
I only hope, when I sing over wine, its beam
Will shine into my golden cup eternally.
--
Written at a friend's request. Li Bai muses on the moon's eternity against human transience: the moon follows us yet cannot be reached; it watched the ancients as it watches us. The white hare and the goddess Chang'e are the moon's legendary inhabitants. Su Shi's famous "When will the bright moon appear?" owes much to this poem.

== 南陵别儿童入京 | Leaving My Children at Nanling for the Capital
by: 李白
form: 七古
tags: 壮志 饮酒 人生 秋
scene: autumn dusk sun cottage fields person rider
白酒新熟山中归，黄鸡啄黍秋正肥。
呼童烹鸡酌白酒，儿女嬉笑牵人衣。
高歌取醉欲自慰，起舞落日争光辉。
游说万乘苦不早，著鞭跨马涉远道。
会稽愚妇轻买臣，余亦辞家西入秦。
仰天大笑出门去，我辈岂是蓬蒿人。
--
The white wine has just ripened as I come back to the hills;
The yellow hens peck millet, in the autumn fat and fine.
I call the boy to cook a chicken and to pour the wine;
My children laugh and tug my clothes with giggles and with trills.
I sing aloud and drink my fill to soothe my soul;
I rise to dance, and vie in brilliance with the setting sun.
Too late, I've come to counsel the Lord of Ten Thousand Chariots — the goal!
I whip my horse and ride the long road till it's done.
The foolish wife of Kuaiji scorned Zhu Maichen once, they say;
I, too, now leave my home and go west, to Qin, away.
I throw back my head and laugh aloud, and out the gate I stride —
Are men like me to live and die in brambles, cast aside?
--
In 742, aged 41, Li Bai was summoned to court by Emperor Xuanzong. Zhu Maichen was a Han woodcutter whose wife left him for his poverty — and who later rose to high office. The last couplet, a shout of vindication, is one of the most quoted lines Li Bai wrote. (The court career lasted less than two years.)

== 上李邕 | To Li Yong
by: 李白
form: 七古
tags: 壮志 人生 天空
scene: day clouds sea wind birds
大鹏一日同风起，扶摇直上九万里。
假令风歇时下来，犹能簸却沧溟水。
世人见我恒殊调，闻余大言皆冷笑。
宣父犹能畏后生，丈夫未可轻年少。
--
The great Peng bird, one day, will rise up with the wind,
And spiral straight ninety thousand li into the sky;
And even if the wind should drop, and it come down,
It still could toss the waters of the vast sea high.
The people of the world find all my tunes odd,
And when they hear my boasts they sneer and scoff;
But Confucius said the young deserve respect —
A real man shouldn't brush a young man off.
--
Written by the young Li Bai to Li Yong, a famous senior official who had treated him dismissively. The Peng is the colossal bird in *Zhuangzi* that rises ninety thousand li on a whirlwind. Confucius had said "the young are to be held in awe" — so Li Bai turns the sage's words against his elder.

== 长相思（其一） | Endless Longing (I)
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 相思 爱情 秋 月 夜
scene: night autumn moon window clouds candle
长相思，在长安。
络纬秋啼金井阑，微霜凄凄簟色寒。
孤灯不明思欲绝，卷帷望月空长叹。
美人如花隔云端！
上有青冥之长天，下有渌水之波澜。
天长路远魂飞苦，梦魂不到关山难。
长相思，摧心肝！
--
Endless longing —
In Chang'an town.
The crickets cry in autumn by the golden well-rail's crown;
A light frost, chill and bleak; my bamboo mat is cold.
My lonely lamp burns dim; my longing's near to death;
I roll the curtain, watch the moon, and sigh with empty breath.
My love, like a flower, is far beyond the clouds!
Above, the blue and boundless height of heaven;
Below, the clear and rippling waves.
The sky is wide, the road is long; my soul's flight is hard;
Even in dreams my spirit can't cross the mountain passes barred.
Endless longing —
It breaks my heart!
--
An old ballad title. The beloved "like a flower beyond the clouds" may be a woman — or, in a long tradition of Chinese allegory, the emperor whom the exiled poet longs to serve. Either reading makes the soul that cannot even reach its goal in dreams heartbreaking.
