== 清平调（其二） | Qingping Song (II)
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 花 春 女性 怀古
scene: spring palace flowers clouds mist
一枝红艳露凝香，云雨巫山枉断肠。
借问汉宫谁得似，可怜飞燕倚新妆。
--
A spray of crimson glory, fragrance gathered in the dew —
The goddess of Wu's cloud and rain broke hearts, but all in vain.
Who in the Han palace could have matched her? Tell me, who?
Sweet Flying Swallow, maybe — after making up again.
--
The second of the three peony songs for Consort Yang. The goddess of Mount Wu, who visited a king "as cloud at dawn and rain at dusk", is no match for her; only Zhao Feiyan ("Flying Swallow"), the Han emperor's slender favourite, comes close — and only after doing her make-up. Later enemies claimed the comparison was a veiled insult, since Feiyan was notorious; it may have helped get Li Bai sent away from court.

== 清平调（其三） | Qingping Song (III)
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 花 春 女性 爱情
scene: spring palace flowers pavilion
名花倾国两相欢，长得君王带笑看。
解释春风无限恨，沉香亭北倚阑干。
--
The famous flower and the beauty who could topple states delight each other so,
And win the sovereign's smiling gaze forever, as they stand.
They melt away the endless sorrows that the spring winds blow —
North of the Aloeswood Pavilion, leaning on the rail, hand in hand.
--
The last of the peony songs: flower and woman are now one pair, both 倾国 — beauty that "topples a kingdom", a phrase from a Han song. Leaning on the balustrade of the Aloeswood Pavilion, they dispel all of spring's melancholy. Within a dozen years the An Lushan rebellion would topple the kingdom in earnest, and Consort Yang would be put to death.

== 秋风词 | Autumn Wind Song
by: 李白
form: 杂言
tags: 相思 秋 月 爱情
scene: night autumn moon leaves crows
秋风清，秋月明，
落叶聚还散，寒鸦栖复惊。
相思相见知何日？此时此夜难为情！
入我相思门，知我相思苦，
长相思兮长相忆，短相思兮无穷极。
早知如此绊人心，何如当初莫相识。
--
Autumn wind clear,
Autumn moon bright;
Fallen leaves gather, then scatter in flight;
Cold crows settle, then start up in fright.
When shall we, who long for each other, meet? Who knows?
This hour, this night, how hard to bear these woes!
Once you have entered love's gate,
You know how bitter longing can be:
Long longing — long remembering;
Short longing — no end that I see.
Had I known how it would bind my heart so,
Better we'd never met, long ago.
--
Called the "three-five-seven-character poem" for its lines that grow from three to five to seven characters, like a rising breath. The leaves that gather and scatter, the crows that settle and are startled, mirror the restless heart. The famous last couplet — "had I known it would bind my heart like this, better we had never met" — is still quoted by lovers today. (The last six lines are found only in later editions; the earliest texts end with "this hour, this night".)

== 劳劳亭 | Laolao Pavilion
by: 李白
form: 五绝
tags: 离别 春 柳 风
scene: spring pavilion willow wind two
天下伤心处，劳劳送客亭。
春风知别苦，不遣柳条青。
--
Of all the places under heaven where hearts break,
None like Laolao Pavilion, where travellers are sent.
The spring wind knows how bitter partings make —
It will not let the willows green, for that's what partings meant.
--
Laolao Pavilion, south of Nanjing, was the traditional place to see travellers off. Because parting friends broke off willow twigs as keepsakes, the poet imagines the spring wind kindly holding back the willows' green — no twigs, no partings.

== 秋登宣城谢朓北楼 | Climbing Xie Tiao's North Tower at Xuancheng in Autumn
by: 李白
form: 五律
tags: 秋 山水 怀古 江河
scene: autumn dusk tower river bridge hills mist
江城如画里，山晚望晴空。
两水夹明镜，双桥落彩虹。
人烟寒橘柚，秋色老梧桐。
谁念北楼上，临风怀谢公。
--
The river town lies as if painted in a scroll;
At evening, from the hills, I watch the cloudless sky.
Two streams enclose a mirror, bright and whole;
Twin bridges fall like rainbows from on high.
Chimney smoke chills the oranges and pomelos;
Autumn's colour ages the wutong trees.
Who'd think that here, upon the North Tower, someone knows
And thinks of Lord Xie, facing the breeze?
--
Xuancheng (Anhui), between two rivers, was governed in the 5th century by the poet Xie Tiao, whom Li Bai revered. The middle couplets are among the most painterly in Tang verse — the bridges reflected as rainbows, the cold blue smoke among the fruit trees, autumn "aging" the wutongs. Li Bai, alone at the top, feels a kinship across three centuries.

== 听蜀僧濬弹琴 | Listening to the Monk Jun of Shu Play the Qin
by: 李白
form: 五律
tags: 音乐 禅 秋 友情
scene: autumn dusk pine mountains temple person clouds
蜀僧抱绿绮，西下峨眉峰。
为我一挥手，如听万壑松。
客心洗流水，余响入霜钟。
不觉碧山暮，秋云暗几重。
--
A monk from Shu, his Green Silk qin in hand,
Came west from the peaks of Emei's height.
He swept his hand across the strings for me —
I heard ten thousand valleys' pines take flight.
My traveller's heart was washed as by a stream;
The echoes merged with frosty temple bells.
I did not notice the green hills grow dark,
Till autumn clouds had dimmed them, swells on swells.
--
The qin (seven-stringed zither) was the scholar's instrument. Green Silk (绿绮) was the name of a famous ancient qin. "Flowing water" recalls the legendary friendship of Boya, who played, and Ziqi, who alone understood him — so the music both cleanses the poet and makes the two men soul-friends. The bell rung in frost was said to resonate of itself.

== 赠孟浩然 | For Meng Haoran
by: 李白
form: 五律
tags: 友情 隐逸 饮酒 花
scene: day pine clouds mountains person cottage
吾爱孟夫子，风流天下闻。
红颜弃轩冕，白首卧松云。
醉月频中圣，迷花不事君。
高山安可仰，徒此揖清芬。
--
I love you, Master Meng, my friend —
Your grace and style the whole world knows.
In youth you spurned the carriage and the crown;
Now white-haired, you lie where pine and cloud repose.
Drunk by the moon, you often "hit the sage";
Lost in the flowers, you will serve no king.
A mountain so high — how can I look up to it?
I can only bow to your pure fragrance as I sing.
--
Li Bai's tribute to Meng Haoran, the older poet who never held office. "Hitting the sage" (中圣) was Wei-dynasty slang for getting drunk — "sage" meant clear wine. The last couplet quotes the *Book of Songs*: "the high mountain I look up to" — a phrase for supreme admiration.

== 夜泊牛渚怀古 | Mooring at Niuzhu at Night, Thinking of the Past
by: 李白
form: 五律
tags: 怀古 失意 秋 月 江河
scene: night autumn moon river boat maple leaves
牛渚西江夜，青天无片云。
登舟望秋月，空忆谢将军。
余亦能高咏，斯人不可闻。
明朝挂帆席，枫叶落纷纷。
--
At Niuzhu, on the Western River, night;
Not one cloud flecks the blue sky's face.
I climb the deck to watch the autumn moon,
And think in vain of General Xie's grace.
I, too, can chant my poems loud and high —
But such a man will never hear my song.
Tomorrow I will hoist my mat-sail and go,
While maple leaves fall, scattering along.
--
In the 4th century General Xie Shang, mooring here on a moonlit night, overheard a poor boatman named Yuan Hong chanting his own poems, and was so impressed he made him his protégé. Li Bai moors in the same spot — and no patron comes. The falling maple leaves carry the disappointment.

== 金陵酒肆留别 | Farewell at a Wine Shop in Jinling
by: 李白
form: 七古
tags: 离别 友情 饮酒 春 柳
scene: spring willow cottage river jar cup two
风吹柳花满店香，吴姬压酒劝客尝。
金陵子弟来相送，欲行不行各尽觞。
请君试问东流水，别意与之谁短长？
--
The wind blows willow-down; the whole shop smells of spring;
A girl of Wu presses the wine and bids the guests to taste.
The young men of Jinling have come to see me off — we cling:
I go, yet don't; and each drains cup on cup in haste.
I ask you, friends, go ask the river flowing east —
Which is longer: its flow, or this parting's feast?
--
Li Bai, about 25, leaving Nanjing for Yangzhou. "Pressing the wine" means straining fresh rice wine from the mash. The image of the endless eastward-flowing river as a measure of feeling became a favourite device of later poets.

== 把酒问月 | Holding My Cup, I Ask the Moon
by: 李白
form: 七古
tags: 月 饮酒 时光 人生 哲理
scene: night moon clouds sea person cup
青天有月来几时？我今停杯一问之。
人攀明月不可得，月行却与人相随。
皎如飞镜临丹阙，绿烟灭尽清辉发。
但见宵从海上来，宁知晓向云间没。
白兔捣药秋复春，嫦娥孤栖与谁邻？
今人不见古时月，今月曾经照古人。
古人今人若流水，共看明月皆如此。
唯愿当歌对酒时，月光长照金樽里。
--
How long has the moon been in the blue sky? Here,
I put my cup down for a while to ask it so.
Men try to climb up to the moon — it's never near;
Yet as they walk, the moon walks with them where they go.
Bright as a flying mirror over the crimson gate,
When the green mists all have melted, it pours out its clear light.
We see it rising from the sea each night, but late,
Who knows where it sinks among the clouds at dawn's first light?
The white hare pounds its herbs from autumn into spring;
Chang'e lives alone — who is her neighbour there?
People today never saw the ancient moon a-shining,
Yet this moon once shone on the ancients, just as fair.
The ancients and the moderns flow on like a stream,
And all have watched the bright moon, just as we.
I only hope, when I sing over wine, its beam
Will shine into my golden cup eternally.
--
Written at a friend's request. Li Bai muses on the moon's eternity against human transience: the moon follows us yet cannot be reached; it watched the ancients as it watches us. The white hare and the goddess Chang'e are the moon's legendary inhabitants. Su Shi's famous "When will the bright moon appear?" owes much to this poem.

== 南陵别儿童入京 | Leaving My Children at Nanling for the Capital
by: 李白
form: 七古
tags: 壮志 饮酒 人生 秋
scene: autumn dusk sun cottage fields person rider
白酒新熟山中归，黄鸡啄黍秋正肥。
呼童烹鸡酌白酒，儿女嬉笑牵人衣。
高歌取醉欲自慰，起舞落日争光辉。
游说万乘苦不早，著鞭跨马涉远道。
会稽愚妇轻买臣，余亦辞家西入秦。
仰天大笑出门去，我辈岂是蓬蒿人。
--
The white wine has just ripened as I come back to the hills;
The yellow hens peck millet, in the autumn fat and fine.
I call the boy to cook a chicken and to pour the wine;
My children laugh and tug my clothes with giggles and with trills.
I sing aloud and drink my fill to soothe my soul;
I rise to dance, and vie in brilliance with the setting sun.
Too late, I've come to counsel the Lord of Ten Thousand Chariots — the goal!
I whip my horse and ride the long road till it's done.
The foolish wife of Kuaiji scorned Zhu Maichen once, they say;
I, too, now leave my home and go west, to Qin, away.
I throw back my head and laugh aloud, and out the gate I stride —
Are men like me to live and die in brambles, cast aside?
--
In 742, aged 41, Li Bai was summoned to court by Emperor Xuanzong. Zhu Maichen was a Han woodcutter whose wife left him for his poverty — and who later rose to high office. The last couplet, a shout of vindication, is one of the most quoted lines Li Bai wrote. (The court career lasted less than two years.)

== 上李邕 | To Li Yong
by: 李白
form: 七古
tags: 壮志 人生 天空
scene: day clouds sea wind birds
大鹏一日同风起，扶摇直上九万里。
假令风歇时下来，犹能簸却沧溟水。
世人见我恒殊调，闻余大言皆冷笑。
宣父犹能畏后生，丈夫未可轻年少。
--
The great Peng bird, one day, will rise up with the wind,
And spiral straight ninety thousand li into the sky;
And even if the wind should drop, and it come down,
It still could toss the waters of the vast sea high.
The people of the world find all my tunes odd,
And when they hear my boasts they sneer and scoff;
But Confucius said the young deserve respect —
A real man shouldn't brush a young man off.
--
Written by the young Li Bai to Li Yong, a famous senior official who had treated him dismissively. The Peng is the colossal bird in *Zhuangzi* that rises ninety thousand li on a whirlwind. Confucius had said "the young are to be held in awe" — so Li Bai turns the sage's words against his elder.

== 下终南山过斛斯山人宿置酒 | Coming Down Zhongnan Mountain, I Stay with the Recluse Husi, Who Sets Out Wine
by: 李白
form: 五古
tags: 隐逸 饮酒 友情 月 山
scene: dusk moon mountains bamboo cottage two pine
暮从碧山下，山月随人归。
却顾所来径，苍苍横翠微。
相携及田家，童稚开荆扉。
绿竹入幽径，青萝拂行衣。
欢言得所憩，美酒聊共挥。
长歌吟松风，曲尽河星稀。
我醉君复乐，陶然共忘机。
--
At dusk I came down from the jade-green hill;
The mountain moon came home with me.
Looking back on the path I'd come along,
I saw blue haze across the green slopes lie, so free.
Hand in hand, we came to his farmhouse;
The children opened up the thorn-wood gate.
Green bamboo lined the hidden path,
Green vines brushed my clothes as I walked straight.
We talked and laughed, so glad to rest,
And shared the fine wine, cup by cup.
We sang long songs to the wind in the pines,
Till the songs were done, and few stars still were up.
I was drunk, and you were merry too —
Content, we both forgot the world's deceit and its ado.
--
A pastoral evening in the manner of Tao Yuanming. Zhongnan Mountain, south of Chang'an, was home to many hermits. 忘机 — "forgetting scheming" — is the Daoist ideal of a mind free of calculation: a man who has forgotten scheming, the story goes, can play among the seagulls without frightening them.

== 哭晁卿衡 | Mourning Lord Chao Heng
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 悼亡 友情 海 月
scene: night moon sea clouds sail
日本晁卿辞帝都，征帆一片绕蓬壶。
明月不归沉碧海，白云愁色满苍梧。
--
Lord Chao of Japan has left the imperial capital;
His single sail went circling round the Isles of Penglai.
The bright moon has not returned — it sank beneath the blue sea;
White clouds of sorrow fill the Cangwu sky.
--
Chao Heng was Abe no Nakamaro, a Japanese scholar who passed the Tang examinations and served at court for decades, a friend of Li Bai and Wang Wei. In 753 he set sail for Japan and was reported drowned. In fact he was shipwrecked on the coast of Vietnam and made his way back to Chang'an — but the poem, written in grief, stayed. He is the "bright moon" lost at sea.

== 春思 | Spring Thoughts
by: 李白
form: 五古
tags: 相思 春 闺怨 女性 风
scene: spring window grass willow wind
燕草如碧丝，秦桑低绿枝。
当君怀归日，是妾断肠时。
春风不相识，何事入罗帏？
--
In Yan, the grass is like fine jade-green thread;
In Qin, the mulberry bows its green boughs low.
The day you long for home at last, I dread,
Will be the day my heart breaks — long ago.
Spring wind, you are a stranger here to me —
Why do you slip into my silken bed-curtains so?
--
A wife in Qin (around Chang'an) thinks of her husband in Yan (the far north-east). Spring comes late in Yan: when his grass is only just sprouting, her mulberries are already heavy — her heart has been breaking for a long time. The last couplet's prim rebuke to the spring wind is a declaration of fidelity.

== 塞下曲（其一） | Frontier Song (I)
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 边塞 战争 壮志 雪
scene: snow mountains fort flag rider
五月天山雪，无花只有寒。
笛中闻折柳，春色未曾看。
晓战随金鼓，宵眠抱玉鞍。
愿将腰下剑，直为斩楼兰。
--
In the fifth month there's still snow on the Tianshan peaks:
No flowers bloom; there's nothing here but cold.
Only in the flute's "Breaking Willows" spring still speaks;
The spring itself, no eye here can behold.
At dawn we fight, to the gongs' and war-drums' beat;
At night we sleep with jade saddles in our arms.
I only wish the sword here at my waist
Could cut down Loulan's king and end these harms.
--
The Tianshan range in Central Asia, where even midsummer is winter. The soldiers know spring only from the tune "Breaking Willows" played on a flute. In 77 BC the Han envoy Fu Jiezi assassinated the treacherous king of Loulan, a desert oasis kingdom; "beheading Loulan" became the soldier's dream of decisive victory.

== 苏台览古 | Viewing the Past at Gusu Terrace
by: 李白
form: 七绝
tags: 怀古 月 时光 春
scene: night moon willow palace lake
旧苑荒台杨柳新，菱歌清唱不胜春。
只今惟有西江月，曾照吴王宫里人。
--
Old parks, a ruined terrace — yet the willows are new;
The water-chestnut songs ring clear, too full of spring to bear.
Now only the moon above the Western River, true,
Once shone on the ladies of the King of Wu's palace there.
--
Gusu Terrace in Suzhou was built by Fuchai, King of Wu, for the legendary beauty Xi Shi, before his kingdom fell to Yue. The new willows and the girls' songs make the ruin unbearable; the moon is the only witness left from those days. Companion piece to *Viewing the Past at Yue*.

== 长相思（其一） | Endless Longing (I)
by: 李白
form: 乐府
tags: 相思 爱情 秋 月 夜
scene: night autumn moon window clouds candle
长相思，在长安。
络纬秋啼金井阑，微霜凄凄簟色寒。
孤灯不明思欲绝，卷帷望月空长叹。
美人如花隔云端！
上有青冥之长天，下有渌水之波澜。
天长路远魂飞苦，梦魂不到关山难。
长相思，摧心肝！
--
Endless longing —
In Chang'an town.
The crickets cry in autumn by the golden well-rail's crown;
A light frost, chill and bleak; my bamboo mat is cold.
My lonely lamp burns dim; my longing's near to death;
I roll the curtain, watch the moon, and sigh with empty breath.
My love, like a flower, is far beyond the clouds!
Above, the blue and boundless height of heaven;
Below, the clear and rippling waves.
The sky is wide, the road is long; my soul's flight is hard;
Even in dreams my spirit can't cross the mountain passes barred.
Endless longing —
It breaks my heart!
--
An old ballad title. The beloved "like a flower beyond the clouds" may be a woman — or, in a long tradition of Chinese allegory, the emperor whom the exiled poet longs to serve. Either reading makes the soul that cannot even reach its goal in dreams heartbreaking.

== 送友人入蜀 | Seeing a Friend Off to Shu
by: 李白
form: 五律
tags: 离别 友情 山 羁旅 春
scene: spring peaks clouds rider river wall
见说蚕丛路，崎岖不易行。
山从人面起，云傍马头生。
芳树笼秦栈，春流绕蜀城。
升沉应已定，不必问君平。
--
They say the road to Cancong's land of Shu
Is steep and rough, and hard to travel through:
Mountains rise right up before your face;
Clouds are born beside your horse's head.
Yet fragrant trees will shade the plank roads of Qin,
And spring streams circle Shu's walled town, instead.
Your rise or fall in life is fixed already —
No need to ask Junping to have it read.
--
Gentler than *The Road to Shu Is Hard*: the dizzying middle lines — mountains rising from in front of your face, clouds born beside the horse's head — give way to spring trees and streams. Yan Junping was a famous Han fortune-teller of Chengdu. Li Bai, recently out of favour himself, offers a friend philosophical calm.

== 宿五松山下荀媪家 | Staying with Old Mother Xun Below Five Pines Mountain
by: 李白
form: 五古
tags: 民生 田园 秋 月
scene: night autumn moon cottage pine fields
我宿五松下，寂寥无所欢。
田家秋作苦，邻女夜舂寒。
跪进雕胡饭，月光明素盘。
令人惭漂母，三谢不能餐。
--
I stayed the night beneath Five Pines,
So lonely, with no pleasure there to find.
Farm folk toil bitterly in autumn;
Next door, girls pound grain in the cold at night.
She knelt to offer me wild-rice dinner —
The moonlight lit the plain white plate.
I felt ashamed, as before the washer-woman,
And thanked her thrice, but could not eat.
--
A rare glimpse of Li Bai humbled. A poor old farm woman serves him precious wild rice (雕胡, the grain of *Zizania*). The washer-woman (漂母) fed the starving young Han Xin, later a great general, who rewarded her with a thousand gold; Li Bai knows he can never repay such kindness.

== 自遣 | Diverting Myself
by: 李白
form: 五绝
tags: 饮酒 闲适 月 花
scene: night moon petals river person birds
对酒不觉暝，落花盈我衣。
醉起步溪月，鸟还人亦稀。
--
Drinking, I didn't notice dusk come on;
Fallen blossoms filled the folds of my gown.
Drunk, I rise and walk along the moonlit stream;
The birds are home; few people are around.
--
Twenty characters of pure contentment. Time slips by unnoticed, petals pile up on his clothes, and he wanders tipsily by a stream full of moonlight as the world goes quiet.
