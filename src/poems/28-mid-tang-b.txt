== 离思（其四） | Thoughts of Parting (IV)
by: 元稹
form: 七绝
tags: 爱情 悼亡 相思
scene: day sea clouds mountains flowers
曾经沧海难为水，除却巫山不是云。
取次花丛懒回顾，半缘修道半缘君。
--
Once you have seen the sea, no other water is the same;
Beside Wu Mountain's clouds, no other clouds are true.
I pass through clumps of flowers, and never turn my head —
Half for the Way I follow; half because of you.
--
One of five poems mourning Yuan Zhen's wife Wei Cong, who died in 809 at twenty-seven. Mount Wu's clouds are the goddess who visited a king in dreams "as morning cloud and evening rain". 曾经沧海难为水 — "once you have seen the ocean, no other water will do" — is quoted for any incomparable experience, especially a first love. (Yuan Zhen remarried within two years; readers have long noted the irony.)

== 行宫 | The Travelling Palace
by: 元稹
form: 五绝
tags: 怀古 女性 花 时光
scene: day palace flowers person two
寥落古行宫，宫花寂寞红。
白头宫女在，闲坐说玄宗。
--
The old travelling palace, lonely and decayed;
Its flowers bloom red, with no one there to see.
White-haired palace maids are all that stayed —
They sit and talk of Xuanzong's days, idly.
--
Twenty characters holding half a century. The women were chosen as girls to serve Emperor Xuanzong, who never came back after the rebellion; now white-haired, they reminisce about him among the red flowers. Critics have called it the most concise poem of the Tang.

== 遣悲怀（其二） | Venting My Grief (II)
by: 元稹
form: 七律
tags: 悼亡 爱情 人生
scene: night candle cottage window person
昔日戏言身后意，今朝都到眼前来。
衣裳已施行看尽，针线犹存未忍开。
尚想旧情怜婢仆，也曾因梦送钱财。
诚知此恨人人有，贫贱夫妻百事哀。
--
We used to joke of what would come when one of us was dead;
Today it's all come true, before my eyes, as if we'd known.
Your clothes I've given away — they're almost gone, as you said;
Your sewing box I keep, but can't bear to open it alone.
Remembering your love, I'm kind to the maids you knew;
Once, after a dream, I burned paper money for you.
I know this sorrow is a thing that everyone must share —
But for a couple who were poor, each memory brings despair.
--
The second of three elegies for Yuan Zhen's wife, who had shared his years of poverty before he rose in office. The details — her clothes given away, her sewing unopened, money burned for her after a dream — made these some of the most moving elegies in Chinese. 贫贱夫妻百事哀 does not mean poverty spoils marriage; it means that for a couple who suffered together, every memory now hurts.

== 菊花 | Chrysanthemums
by: 元稹
form: 七绝
tags: 花 秋 咏物
scene: dusk autumn chrysanthemum cottage person sun
秋丛绕舍似陶家，遍绕篱边日渐斜。
不是花中偏爱菊，此花开尽更无花。
--
Autumn clusters ring my house, as at Tao Qian's home;
Around the hedge I wander, till the sun is low.
It isn't that, of all the flowers, I prefer the chrysanthemum —
But when this one has faded, there are no more flowers to show.
--
Chrysanthemums have been linked with Tao Yuanming ever since he wrote of "picking chrysanthemums by the eastern hedge". Yuan Zhen gives a simple, touching reason for loving them: they are the last flowers of the year.

== 雁门太守行 | Song of the Governor of Yanmen
by: 李贺
form: 乐府
tags: 边塞 战争 壮志 秋
scene: dusk autumn clouds wall fort flag rider frost
黑云压城城欲摧，甲光向日金鳞开。
角声满天秋色里，塞上燕脂凝夜紫。
半卷红旗临易水，霜重鼓寒声不起。
报君黄金台上意，提携玉龙为君死。
--
Black clouds press on the walls — the city's close to breaking;
Our armour flashes in the sun like golden scales unfolding.
The bugles fill the sky amid the colours of the fall;
On the frontier, rouge-red earth congeals to purple as night is falling.
Half-furled, the red flags reach the river Yi;
The frost is thick; the drums are cold; their beat won't rise at all.
To repay my lord, who raised me to his Golden Terrace high,
I'll grasp my jade-dragon sword, and for him I'll die.
--
Li He, about eighteen, brought this poem to Han Yu, who — so the story goes — was so struck by the first line that he refastened his belt and invited the young man in. A battle painted in colours: black clouds, golden armour, rouge-red earth turning purple with blood, red flags, white frost. The Golden Terrace was where King Zhao of Yan welcomed men of talent; the "jade dragon" is a sword.

== 马诗（其五） | Horse Poem (V)
by: 李贺
form: 五绝
tags: 壮志 边塞 秋 月
scene: night autumn crescent desert horse mountains
大漠沙如雪，燕山月似钩。
何当金络脑，快走踏清秋。
--
The desert's sand is white as snow;
Over Mount Yan, the moon hangs like a hook.
When will I wear a golden bridle, and go
Galloping through clear autumn? — Now I can only look.
--
One of twenty-three "Horse Poems", in which a fine horse waiting for a worthy master is the talented young Li He waiting for recognition — which never came. The golden bridle is an honoured place in the emperor's service.

== 李凭箜篌引 | Li Ping Plays the Konghou
by: 李贺
form: 乐府
tags: 音乐 秋 月 天空
scene: night autumn moon clouds mountains palace
吴丝蜀桐张高秋，空白凝云颓不流。
江娥啼竹素女愁，李凭中国弹箜篌。
昆山玉碎凤皇呌，芙蓉泣露香兰笑。
十二门前融冷光，二十三丝动紫皇。
女娲炼石补天处，石破天惊逗秋雨。
梦入坤山教神妪，老鱼跳波瘦蛟舞。
吴质不眠倚桂树，露脚斜飞湿寒兔。
--
Wu silk on Shu wood, strung in high autumn's air;
In empty hills the clouds congeal and halt, as if to hear.
The river goddess weeps on bamboo; the White Maiden's in despair —
In the capital, Li Ping plays the konghou harp, so clear.
Like Kunlun jade that shatters, like the phoenix crying high;
Like lotus weeping dew, and fragrant orchids laughing bright.
Before the twelve gates of the city, it melts the cold light;
Its twenty-three strings move the Purple Emperor in the sky.
Where Nüwa smelted stones to mend the heavens long ago,
The stones break — heaven shudders — autumn rain comes pouring through.
In dreams it climbs the holy mountain, teaching the goddess who would know;
Old fishes leap the waves, and lean dragons dance anew.
Wu Gang, sleepless, leans against the cassia tree;
The slanting dew falls down and wets the cold moon-hare, I see.
--
Li Ping was the most famous player of the konghou, a vertical harp, at court. Li He describes the music not by its sound but by its effects across the universe: clouds stop, goddesses weep, the Purple Emperor of heaven is moved, the stone that the goddess Nüwa used to patch the sky cracks and lets the rain through, and in the moon Wu Gang and the jade hare are drenched with dew. With Bai Juyi's *Pipa Player*, one of the great Tang poems on music.

== 金铜仙人辞汉歌 | The Bronze Immortal Takes Leave of Han
by: 李贺
form: 乐府
tags: 怀古 悼亡 月 秋 时光
scene: night autumn moon palace wall wind
茂陵刘郎秋风客，夜闻马嘶晓无迹。
画栏桂树悬秋香，三十六宫土花碧。
魏官牵车指千里，东关酸风射眸子。
空将汉月出宫门，忆君清泪如铅水。
衰兰送客咸阳道，天若有情天亦老。
携盘独出月荒凉，渭城已远波声小。
--
Lord Liu of Maoling, guest of the autumn wind's lament —
At night his horses neighed; by dawn they'd left no trace.
By painted rails the cassia trees still hang their autumn scent;
In the thirty-six palaces, green moss covers every place.
The officers of Wei drag off the cart a thousand li;
At the eastern gate, a bitter wind stings the eyes.
Only the Han moon goes with him through the palace gate, to see;
Remembering his lord, his tears fall like molten lead, in sighs.
Withered orchids see the traveller off along the Xianyang road —
If heaven had feelings, heaven, too, would age and grow old.
Holding his plate, he goes out alone in the bleak moon's glow;
Weicheng is far behind; the river's murmur fades to low.
--
In 233 the Wei emperor had the great bronze statue of an immortal — holding a plate to catch heavenly dew for Emperor Wu of Han's elixirs — carted from Chang'an to Luoyang; legend said the statue wept as it left. Li He, a distant descendant of the Tang royal house, mourns the passing of dynasties through the bronze man's tears. 天若有情天亦老, "if heaven had feelings, heaven too would grow old", is one of the most famous lines in Chinese.

== 悯农（其一） | Pity the Farmers (I)
by: 李绅
form: 五古
tags: 民生 田园 秋
scene: autumn fields person sun
春种一粒粟，秋收万颗子。
四海无闲田，农夫犹饿死。
--
In spring they sow a single grain;
In autumn reap ten thousand more.
No field lies idle anywhere —
Yet still the farmers starve, as before.
--
Every Chinese child learns these two poems. The first asks a terrible question: with so much harvested, why do the people who grow it starve? (The answer, left unsaid, is rents and taxes.)

== 悯农（其二） | Pity the Farmers (II)
by: 李绅
form: 五古
tags: 民生 田园 夏
scene: summer sun fields person
锄禾日当午，汗滴禾下土。
谁知盘中餐，粒粒皆辛苦。
--
Hoeing the crops beneath the noonday sun,
Their sweat drips on the soil where seedlings grow.
Who thinks, of the rice upon his plate, that every one —
Each single grain — was won with toil and woe?
--
The poem Chinese parents quote to children who leave rice in their bowls: every grain is someone's sweat.
--
烈日当午，农人仍在田间锄禾，汗水一滴滴落进泥土。“谁知盘中餐，粒粒皆辛苦”，是家长劝孩子珍惜粮食时最常念的两句。

== 十五夜望月 | Gazing at the Moon on the Fifteenth Night
by: 王建
form: 七绝
tags: 节日 月 秋 相思 夜
scene: night autumn moon crows cottage
中庭地白树栖鸦，冷露无声湿桂花。
今夜月明人尽望，不知秋思落谁家。
--
The courtyard ground is white; crows roost upon the trees;
Cold dew, in silence, wets the cassia's blooms.
Tonight the moon is bright, and all look up to see —
I wonder in whose house the autumn longing looms?
--
The Mid-Autumn Festival, when families gather under the full moon. The ground white with moonlight, crows silent in the trees, dew falling on the cassia (the tree that grows in the moon) — and a gentle question: everyone is looking at the moon, but whose heart is missing someone most?
