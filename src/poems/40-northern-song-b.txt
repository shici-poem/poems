== 生查子·元夕 | Sheng Zha Zi: Lantern Night
by: 欧阳修
form: 词
tags: 节日 爱情 月 相思 柳
scene: night moon willow lantern person
去年元夜时，花市灯如昼。
月上柳梢头，人约黄昏后。

今年元夜时，月与灯依旧。
不见去年人，泪湿春衫袖。
--
Last year, on Lantern Night,
The flower market's lamps shone bright as day.
The moon climbed to the willow tips;
We met when dusk was turning grey.

This year, on Lantern Night,
The moon and lanterns shine as then.
But I can't find last year's love —
Tears wet my spring sleeves once again.
--
The Lantern Festival was the one night when young people could meet freely in the crowds. 月上柳梢头，人约黄昏后 — "the moon over the willow tips; a tryst after dusk" — is the most romantic couplet in Chinese. The lyric is traditionally given to Ouyang Xiu, though some editions credit the woman poet Zhu Shuzhen.

== 蝶恋花（庭院深深深几许） | Butterflies Love Flowers (Deep, Deep the Courtyard)
by: 欧阳修
form: 词
tags: 闺怨 春 花 雨 风 女性
scene: dusk spring rain wind willow petals tower
庭院深深深几许？杨柳堆烟，帘幕无重数。
玉勒雕鞍游冶处，楼高不见章台路。

雨横风狂三月暮，门掩黄昏，无计留春住。
泪眼问花花不语，乱红飞过秋千去。
--
Deep, deep the courtyard — how deep can it be?
Willows heaped with mist,
And curtains past all counting, endlessly.
With jade bridle and carved saddle, he roams the pleasure lanes;
The tower's high — the road to Zhangtai is lost to me.

Rain lashing, wind raging, the third month's end;
I close the gate at dusk —
There's no way I can make the spring stay.
With tearful eyes I ask the flowers — the flowers do not speak;
The scattered red flies past the swing, and away.
--
A wife shut deep in a great house while her husband rides off to the pleasure quarters (Zhangtai Lane). Li Qingzhao loved the opening line so much that she began several lyrics with it — and it is her testimony that settles the authorship, since some collections give the lyric to Feng Yansi. The last couplet — the flowers, asked, say nothing, and fly away past the swing where she once played — is one of the most admired in the *ci*.

== 浪淘沙（把酒祝东风） | Waves Washing Sand (I Raise My Cup to the East Wind)
by: 欧阳修
form: 词
tags: 友情 离别 春 花 饮酒 时光
scene: spring flowers willow wall two cup
把酒祝东风，且共从容。
垂杨紫陌洛城东。总是当时携手处，游遍芳丛。

聚散苦匆匆，此恨无穷。
今年花胜去年红。可惜明年花更好，知与谁同？
--
I raise my cup and pray the east wind:
Linger with us, take your ease.
The willows hang along Luoyang's purple lanes —
Where we walked hand in hand in days like these,
Through all the flowers, under the trees.

Meetings and partings come too fast;
This sorrow has no end.
This year the flowers are redder than last year's;
Next year they'll be lovelier still, my friend —
But who will see them with me then?
--
Luoyang, 1032, at a reunion with his friend Mei Yaochen. The flowers get better every year — but the friends who share them scatter. The last line makes the beauty of next spring a cause of sorrow.

== 画眉鸟 | The Thrush
by: 欧阳修
form: 七绝
tags: 鸟 咏物 哲理 闲适 山
scene: spring mountains flowers birds pine
百啭千声随意移，山花红紫树高低。
始知锁向金笼听，不及林间自在啼。
--
A hundred trills, a thousand notes, it flits where it may please,
Among the red and purple mountain blossoms, high and low.
Now I know a bird in a golden cage, for all its ease,
Can never sing as sweetly as one free in the woods below.
--
Written while Ouyang Xiu was demoted to Chuzhou. A thrush in a golden cage — like an official at court — sings less sweetly than one free in the forest: a gentle hymn to freedom, and to his exile.

== 踏莎行（候馆梅残） | Treading the Grass (At the Inn, the Plum Is Fading)
by: 欧阳修
form: 词
tags: 离别 相思 春 柳 花
scene: spring willow plum bridge rider hills tower
候馆梅残，溪桥柳细，草薰风暖摇征辔。
离愁渐远渐无穷，迢迢不断如春水。

寸寸柔肠，盈盈粉泪，楼高莫近危阑倚。
平芜尽处是春山，行人更在春山外。
--
At the inn, the plum is fading;
By the stream bridge, the willows slight;
Sweet grass, warm wind — I shake the reins and ride.
The sorrow of parting grows endless as I go,
On and on, unbroken, like a spring river's tide.

Inch by inch, her tender heart;
Brimming, her rouged tears flow.
The tower is high — don't lean against the rail.
Where the level meadows end, there are the spring hills —
And the traveller is further still, beyond them, far away.
--
The first stanza is the traveller riding away, his sorrow growing with every step like a spring river; the second is the woman left behind in her tower. In the famous last couplet she gazes to the edge of the plain, to the spring hills — and he is beyond even them.

== 采桑子（群芳过后西湖好） | Picking Mulberries (When the Flowers Have Passed, West Lake Is Fair)
by: 欧阳修
form: 词
tags: 春 闲适 柳 鸟 雨 山水
scene: spring rain lake willow swallows petals pavilion
群芳过后西湖好，狼籍残红。飞絮濛濛，垂柳阑干尽日风。

笙歌散尽游人去，始觉春空。垂下帘栊，双燕归来细雨中。
--
When all the flowers have passed, West Lake is lovely still:
The fallen red lies strewn,
The willow-down drifts free,
The willows by the railings sway all day in the breeze.

The songs and pipes are done; the crowds have gone away;
And now I feel how empty spring can be.
I let the curtain down —
And through the fine rain, two swallows come home to me.
--
Ouyang Xiu retired in 1071 to the West Lake of Yingzhou (Fuyang, Anhui — not the lake at Hangzhou) and wrote ten lyrics about it to this tune. Here he finds beauty after the crowds and flowers have gone: quiet, emptiness, and a pair of swallows in the rain.

== 玉楼春（尊前拟把归期说） | Spring in the Jade Tower (Over the Wine, I Meant to Say)
by: 欧阳修
form: 词
tags: 离别 爱情 春 花 饮酒 人生
scene: spring flowers wall two cup
尊前拟把归期说，未语春容先惨咽。
人生自是有情痴，此恨不关风与月。

离歌且莫翻新阕，一曲能教肠寸结。
直须看尽洛城花，始共春风容易别。
--
Over the wine I meant to tell her when I would return;
Before I'd said a word, her spring-fair face dissolved in tears.
We humans are born fools for love, I've come to learn —
This grief is none of the wind's or moon's affairs.

Don't play the parting song again in a new key —
One tune's enough to tie the heart in knots, inch by inch.
First let us see all Luoyang's flowers, every tree —
Then from the spring wind we can part without a flinch.
--
Leaving Luoyang in 1034. 人生自是有情痴，此恨不关风与月 — "people are simply born fools for love; this grief has nothing to do with wind or moon" — is often quoted: our sorrows come from our own hearts, not the scenery. The last couplet is typical Ouyang Xiu bravado: let us enjoy everything first, and parting will be easy.

== 泊船瓜洲 | Mooring at Guazhou
by: 王安石
form: 七绝
tags: 思乡 春 月 江河
scene: night spring moon river boat hills
京口瓜洲一水间，钟山只隔数重山。
春风又绿江南岸，明月何时照我还？
--
Jingkou and Guazhou — only a river runs between;
Bell Mountain lies beyond just a few hills more.
Once again the spring wind turns the south bank green —
Bright moon, when will you light me home to my own door?
--
Wang Anshi, mooring at Guazhou on the Yangtze in 1075 on his way back to office as chief minister, looks toward his retirement home at Bell Mountain (Zhongshan) in Nanjing. The verb 绿, "to green", is the most famous word choice in Chinese poetry: his draft is said to have tried "arrives", "passes", "enters", "fills" and a dozen others before he settled on "greens".

== 元日 | New Year's Day
by: 王安石
form: 七绝
tags: 节日 春 冬
scene: dawn winter sun cottage smoke wall
爆竹声中一岁除，春风送暖入屠苏。
千门万户曈曈日，总把新桃换旧符。
--
Amid the firecrackers, the old year's chased away;
The spring wind brings its warmth into the New Year wine.
On a thousand gates, ten thousand homes, the sun shines on the day
They take the old peach charms down, and put up new ones, fine.
--
Firecrackers drove away evil spirits; *tusu* wine was drunk for health; peach-wood charms bearing door gods were hung on gates — the ancestors of today's red New Year couplets. Written in 1069 as Wang Anshi launched his great reforms, replacing old charms with new became a symbol of renewal.

== 梅花（王安石） | Plum Blossom
by: 王安石
form: 五绝
tags: 花 冬 咏物 雪
scene: winter plum snow cottage
墙角数枝梅，凌寒独自开。
遥知不是雪，为有暗香来。
--
In the corner by the wall, a few plum sprays
Have bloomed alone, defying the bitter cold.
From far away I know it isn't snow —
A hidden fragrance on the air has told.
--
Written in retirement after his reforms had failed. White plum blossom looks like snow — but snow has no scent. Like the plum, the person of integrity stands alone in hard times, and is known by a "hidden fragrance".

== 登飞来峰 | Climbing Feilai Peak
by: 王安石
form: 七绝
tags: 哲理 壮志 山 天空
scene: dawn sun peaks temple clouds
飞来山上千寻塔，闻说鸡鸣见日升。
不畏浮云遮望眼，自缘身在最高层。
--
On Flying Peak there stands a pagoda, a thousand fathoms high;
They say at cockcrow you can see the sun come up from there.
I do not fear the floating clouds that block the eye,
Because I'm standing on the highest stair.
--
Written in 1050 by the young Wang Anshi, full of confidence. "Floating clouds" were the standard image for slanderers who obscure the truth; stand high enough, and you will not be deceived. A favourite poem of vision and ambition.

== 桂枝香·金陵怀古 | Fragrance of the Cassia Branch: Remembering the Past at Jinling
by: 王安石
form: 词
tags: 怀古 秋 江河 山水
scene: dusk autumn sun river peaks sail flag crane
登临送目，正故国晚秋，天气初肃。
千里澄江似练，翠峰如簇。
归帆去棹残阳里，背西风、酒旗斜矗。
彩舟云淡，星河鹭起，画图难足。

念往昔、繁华竞逐，叹门外楼头，悲恨相续。
千古凭高，对此漫嗟荣辱。
六朝旧事随流水，但寒烟衰草凝绿。
至今商女，时时犹唱，后庭遗曲。
--
I climb up high and send my gaze afar:
It's late autumn in the ancient capital,
The air just turning crisp and clear.
A thousand li of river, bright as a sash of silk;
Green peaks clustered like arrows, far and near.
Homeward sails and parting oars in the setting sun;
Their backs to the west wind, wine flags stand askew.
Painted boats in pale clouds,
Egrets rising from the river of stars —
No painting could capture the view.

I think of times long past, when men vied for splendour, and
"Outside the gate, upon the tower" —
Grief followed grief, without an end.
Through the ages men have climbed to face this view,
And sighed in vain at glory and disgrace again.
The Six Dynasties are gone with the flowing water;
Only cold mist and withered grass still hold their green.
And to this day the singing girls,
Now and then, still sing
The song of the Rear Court, as it's been.
--
Nanjing (Jinling), capital of six fallen southern dynasties. "Outside the gate, upon the tower" recalls the fall of Chen in 589, when a Sui general was already at the gate while the last emperor was upstairs with his favourite; that ruler's song "Flowers of the Rear Courtyard" became the emblem of decadence. Of more than thirty lyrics written on this theme in Wang Anshi's day, his was judged the best.

== 书湖阴先生壁（其一） | Written on the Wall of Master Huyin (I)
by: 王安石
form: 七绝
tags: 田园 山水 闲适 夏
scene: summer cottage river fields hills flowers
茅檐长扫净无苔，花木成畦手自栽。
一水护田将绿绕，两山排闼送青来。
--
His thatched eaves are swept so often there's no moss at all;
The flowers and trees are set in beds he planted by hand.
A stream protects his fields, wrapping them round in green;
Two hills push open his door and send in their blue-green band.
--
Written on the wall of his neighbour Yang Dezheng ("Master Huyin") near Bell Mountain. The last couplet personifies the landscape — the stream "guards" the fields like a soldier, the hills "push open the door" (排闼) like eager visitors — with phrases borrowed from the *History of the Han*, yet perfectly natural.
