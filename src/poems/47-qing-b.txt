== 论诗（其二） | On Poetry (II)
by: 赵翼
form: 七绝
tags: 哲理 时光 人生
scene: day mountains river person
李杜诗篇万口传，至今已觉不新鲜。
江山代有才人出，各领风骚数百年。
--
Li Bai's and Du Fu's poems are on ten thousand tongues;
By now they've come to feel a little stale.
In every age the land brings forth new talents,
Each to lead poetry a few centuries — that's the tale.
--
A bold claim for originality: even the greatest models grow stale through imitation, and every age must find its own voice. 江山代有才人出，各领风骚数百年 — "every age brings forth its own talents, each to lead for a few hundred years" — is quoted whenever a new generation rises.

== 杂感 | Random Feelings
by: 黄景仁
form: 七律
tags: 失意 人生 夜
scene: night candle person cottage
仙佛茫茫两未成，只知独夜不平鸣。
风蓬飘尽悲歌气，泥絮沾来薄幸名。
十有九人堪白眼，百无一用是书生。
莫因诗卷愁成谶，春鸟秋虫自作声。
--
To be an immortal or a Buddha — I've failed at both, it seems;
I only know, alone at night, to cry out at the world's wrong.
Like windblown tumbleweed, my mournful songs have spent their force;
Like catkins in the mud, I've earned a heartless name, so long.
Nine people out of ten deserve the whites of my eyes;
Of all the useless things, a scholar is the worst.
Don't fear my book of poems will prove a sad prophecy —
The spring birds and autumn insects simply sing because they must.
--
Huang Jingren, a gifted, impoverished poet who died at thirty-four. 十有九人堪白眼，百无一用是书生 — "nine in ten deserve the whites of my eyes; of all things, a scholar is the most useless" — is still the classic bitter joke of frustrated intellectuals. The "whites of the eyes" are Ruan Ji's look of contempt.

== 绮怀（其十五） | Tender Memories (XV)
by: 黄景仁
form: 七律
tags: 爱情 相思 夜 音乐 时光
scene: night stars flowers wall person
几回花下坐吹箫，银汉红墙入望遥。
似此星辰非昨夜，为谁风露立中宵。
缠绵思尽抽残茧，宛转心伤剥后蕉。
三五年时三五月，可怜杯酒不曾消。
--
How many times beneath the flowers I sat and played the flute,
Gazing at the Milky Way, the red wall far away.
Like stars tonight that are not last night's stars —
For whom do I stand in wind and dew at midnight, till the day?
My lingering love is drawn out, like the last silk from a cocoon;
My heart is wounded like a plantain stripped, layer by layer, in decay.
At fifteen, in the fifteenth night's full moon —
Alas, no cup of wine has ever washed that pain away.
--
The fifteenth of sixteen poems remembering a first love — a girl he met at fifteen and lost. 似此星辰非昨夜，为谁风露立中宵 — "these stars are not last night's stars; for whom do I stand at midnight in the wind and dew?" — answers Li Shangyin's "last night's stars, last night's wind", and is one of the most quoted lines of longing in Chinese.

== 己亥杂诗（其五） | Poems of the Year Jihai (V)
by: 龚自珍
form: 七绝
tags: 离别 哲理 花 春 人生
scene: dusk spring sun petals rider flowers
浩荡离愁白日斜，吟鞭东指即天涯。
落红不是无情物，化作春泥更护花。
--
Vast as the sea, my sorrow at parting; the sun is slanting low;
I point my whip east, chanting — and the world's end lies ahead.
But fallen petals are not heartless things, you know:
They turn to mud in spring, to feed the flowers in their bed.
--
1839: Gong Zizhen resigns from office in Beijing and heads home to the south. He compares himself to a fallen petal — no longer on the branch, but still devoted. 落红不是无情物，化作春泥更护花 is now quoted in praise of teachers and anyone who quietly serves the next generation.

== 己亥杂诗（其二百二十） | Poems of the Year Jihai (CCXX)
by: 龚自珍
form: 七绝
tags: 爱国 壮志 哲理 天空
scene: day clouds wind horse mountains
九州生气恃风雷，万马齐喑究可哀。
我劝天公重抖擞，不拘一格降人才。
--
The Nine Provinces need wind and thunder to come alive;
Ten thousand horses standing mute — how sad to see!
I urge the Lord of Heaven to rouse himself once more,
And send down talents of every kind, unbound and free.
--
Written as the Qing empire stagnated on the eve of the Opium War. "Ten thousand horses all mute" is a silenced, conformist officialdom; China needs "wind and thunder" — great change — and talents chosen "not in one mould" (不拘一格). The last line is now a slogan for open-minded recruitment.

== 赴戍登程口占示家人（其二） | Setting Out for Exile: Lines for My Family (II)
by: 林则徐
form: 七律
tags: 爱国 人生 壮志 离别
scene: day rider horse mountains desert
力微任重久神疲，再竭衰庸定不支。
苟利国家生死以，岂因祸福避趋之？
谪居正是君恩厚，养拙刚于戍卒宜。
戏与山妻谈故事，试吟断送老头皮。
--
My strength is small, my task was great; I'm long worn out and spent;
To strain my poor old talents more would surely see me fall.
If it will serve my country, I'll give my life or death to it —
Should I chase luck or flee misfortune? Not at all!
My banishment is surely proof of my lord's great grace;
To nurse my clumsiness, a soldier's post is just my place.
In jest I tell my wife the tale of long ago,
And chant the line, "There goes the old man's head!" — with a smile on my face.
--
In 1842 Lin Zexu, who had destroyed the British opium at Humen, was made the scapegoat for the Opium War and exiled to Xinjiang. Taking leave of his family, he wrote 苟利国家生死以，岂因祸福避趋之 — "if it serves the country, I will give my life or death; how could I seek fortune or flee disaster?" — the most quoted line of public duty in modern China. The ending is a joke: a Song recluse summoned to court was sent off by his wife with a verse, "this time there goes the old man's head".

== 村居（高鼎） | Village Life
by: 高鼎
form: 七绝
tags: 春 童趣 柳 鸟 田园
scene: spring willow oriole grass fields person two
草长莺飞二月天，拂堤杨柳醉春烟。
儿童散学归来早，忙趁东风放纸鸢。
--
The grass grows tall, the orioles fly, in the second month of spring;
The willows brush the dyke, and, drunk with spring's soft mist, they sway.
The children have come home from school early today,
To fly their paper kites in the east wind while they may.
--
The happiest poem of the late Qing: early spring in the countryside, willows "drunk" in the misty air, and children running home to fly kites. Every Chinese child learns it — often while flying a kite.

== 狱中题壁 | Written on the Prison Wall
by: 谭嗣同
form: 七绝
tags: 爱国 壮志 人生
scene: day wall person sun clouds
望门投止思张俭，忍死须臾待杜根。
我自横刀向天笑，去留肝胆两昆仑。
--
Like Zhang Jian, may my friends find shelter at any door;
Like Du Gen, may they bear death a while, until the time is right.
As for me, I'll hold my sword and laugh at heaven —
The ones who flee, the one who stays: two Kunlun peaks of might.
--
Written on his prison wall in 1898, after the Empress Dowager Cixi crushed the Hundred Days' Reform. Tan Sitong refused to flee: "No country has ever been reformed without bloodshed; let it begin with me." Zhang Jian and Du Gen were Han loyalists who survived persecution — he wishes his escaping comrades the same luck. Those who flee to carry on the struggle and the one who stays to die are "two Kunlun mountains" of equal courage. He was executed days later, aged thirty-three.

== 对酒 | Facing Wine
by: 秋瑾
form: 七绝
tags: 爱国 壮志 饮酒 女性
scene: day sea person cup
不惜千金买宝刀，貂裘换酒也堪豪。
一腔热血勤珍重，洒去犹能化碧涛。
--
I'll spend a thousand gold without a care to buy a sword;
To trade my sable coat for wine is gallant, too.
This chestful of hot blood — I'll guard it, treasure it;
For spilled, it still can turn to jade-green waves anew.
--
Qiu Jin, revolutionary and feminist, left an arranged marriage to study in Japan and plot against the Qing. Blood "turning to green jade" recalls Chang Hong, a loyal Zhou official whose spilled blood became green jade three years after his unjust death. She was beheaded in 1907, aged thirty-one.

== 蝶恋花（阅尽天涯离别苦） | Butterflies Love Flowers (I've Seen All the Pain of Parting)
by: 王国维
form: 词
tags: 时光 爱情 离别 春 花
scene: dusk spring petals window candle two
阅尽天涯离别苦，不道归来，零落花如许。
花底相看无一语，绿窗春与天俱莫。

待把相思灯下诉，一缕新欢，旧恨千千缕。
最是人间留不住，朱颜辞镜花辞树。
--
I've seen all the pain of parting at the ends of the earth —
But never thought, on coming home,
To find the flowers fallen, all this way.
Beneath the flowers we gaze at each other, wordless;
At the green window, spring and the light both fade away.

I'd tell her all my longing by the lamp —
A single thread of new joy,
A thousand threads of old pain, grey.
What this world can least keep, most of all:
Young faces leaving the mirror; flowers leaving the spray.
--
Wang Guowei — whose "three stages" of achievement appear in several notes on this site — comes home after long travels to find his wife aged and the spring gone. 最是人间留不住，朱颜辞镜花辞树 — "what the world can least keep: rosy youth leaving the mirror, blossoms leaving the tree" — is the most quoted line of his own lyrics.
