== 锦瑟 | The Inlaid Zither
by: 李商隐
form: 七律
tags: 爱情 时光 音乐 人生 相思
scene: night moon sea butterfly mist
锦瑟无端五十弦，一弦一柱思华年。
庄生晓梦迷蝴蝶，望帝春心托杜鹃。
沧海月明珠有泪，蓝田日暖玉生烟。
此情可待成追忆？只是当时已惘然。
--
The patterned zither, for no reason, has fifty strings;
Each string, each bridge, brings back the years of bloom.
Zhuangzi at dawn dreamed he was a butterfly, lost in its wings;
Emperor Wang left his spring heart to the cuckoo's gloom.
On the blue sea, under the moon, the pearls are tears;
At Lantian, in the warm sun, the jade gives off a haze.
Must such a feeling wait for memory, across the years?
Even then, as it was happening, I was lost in a daze.
--
Perhaps the most debated poem in Chinese — a love poem, an elegy for his wife, a lament for his career, or a poem about poetry itself. The legendary fifty-stringed zither was so sad that the god Fuxi split it into twenty-five. Zhuangzi dreamed he was a butterfly and woke unsure which was the dreamer; Emperor Wang of Shu became a cuckoo that cried till it bled; mermaids wept pearls; the jade buried at Lantian gave off a glow like smoke in the sun. Every image is lovely and out of reach — like the past.

== 无题（相见时难别亦难） | Untitled (Hard It Is to Meet)
by: 李商隐
form: 七律
tags: 爱情 相思 春 夜 花
scene: night spring candle window petals crescent
相见时难别亦难，东风无力百花残。
春蚕到死丝方尽，蜡炬成灰泪始干。
晓镜但愁云鬓改，夜吟应觉月光寒。
蓬山此去无多路，青鸟殷勤为探看。
--
It's hard for us to meet, and just as hard to part;
The east wind has no strength; a hundred flowers fall.
The spring silkworm spins its silk until it dies;
The candle weeps till it is ash, its tears dried after all.
At dawn, before her mirror, she grieves her hair turning grey;
Chanting at night, she must feel the moonlight's cold.
From here to Penglai Mountain is not so far a way —
Bluebird, go faithfully, and see her for me, as of old.
--
The most famous of Li Shangyin's "Untitled" poems. The silkworm and the candle are the supreme images of devotion until death — and 丝 "silk" puns on 思 "longing". The bluebird was the messenger of the Queen Mother of the West, and Penglai is the island of immortals: the beloved is as unreachable as a goddess. Teachers today quote the couplet to honour selfless dedication.

== 无题（昨夜星辰昨夜风） | Untitled (Last Night's Stars)
by: 李商隐
form: 七律
tags: 爱情 相思 夜 饮酒
scene: night stars tower candle cup
昨夜星辰昨夜风，画楼西畔桂堂东。
身无彩凤双飞翼，心有灵犀一点通。
隔座送钩春酒暖，分曹射覆蜡灯红。
嗟余听鼓应官去，走马兰台类转蓬。
--
Last night's stars, and last night's breeze,
West of the painted tower, east of the cassia hall.
Though I lack the bright phoenix's wings to fly to you with ease,
Our hearts, like the magic horn, are linked through all.
Across the tables we passed the hook; the spring wine glowed warm;
In teams we guessed at hidden things, by candles burning red.
Alas — the drums call me to my desk; I must away from you,
And ride to the Orchid Terrace, a tumbleweed blown ahead.
--
A night of party games — passing a hidden hook, guessing concealed objects — where the poet exchanged glances with someone he could not openly approach. 心有灵犀一点通: the rhinoceros horn was believed to have a white thread running from end to end, so the phrase means two hearts that understand each other instantly. The Orchid Terrace was the imperial library, where Li Shangyin worked as a minor clerk.

== 夜雨寄北 | Written on a Rainy Night, to the North
by: 李商隐
form: 七绝
tags: 相思 雨 夜 秋 爱情 羁旅
scene: night autumn rain mountains window candle
君问归期未有期，巴山夜雨涨秋池。
何当共剪西窗烛，却话巴山夜雨时。
--
You ask me when I'm coming home — I cannot say just when;
Tonight in the hills of Ba, the rain is filling autumn's pool.
When shall we trim the candle by the west window again,
And talk of tonight, when the rain fell on Ba, so cool?
--
Written in Sichuan (Ba) around 851 to his wife — or a friend — in the north. The present lonely, rainy night is imagined as a future memory shared by candlelight, so the poem folds time back on itself; "Ba hills, night rain" appears twice, once in sorrow and once in comfort. "Trimming the candle at the west window" (西窗剪烛) now means a heart-to-heart talk with someone dear.

== 登乐游原 | Climbing Leyou Plain
by: 李商隐
form: 五绝
tags: 人生 时光 天空 失意
scene: dusk sun hills wall rider
向晚意不适，驱车登古原。
夕阳无限好，只是近黄昏。
--
Toward evening, my heart was ill at ease;
I drove my carriage up the ancient height.
The setting sun is boundlessly lovely —
It's just that it's so close to night.
--
Leyou Plain was the highest spot in Chang'an, with a view over the whole city. 夕阳无限好，只是近黄昏 — beauty at its height is already ending — is quoted of old age, of the late Tang dynasty itself, and of every last golden moment.

== 嫦娥 | Chang'e
by: 李商隐
form: 七绝
tags: 月 夜 相思 女性
scene: night moon stars window candle palace
云母屏风烛影深，长河渐落晓星沉。
嫦娥应悔偷灵药，碧海青天夜夜心。
--
Behind the mica screen, the candle's shadows grow;
The Milky Way sinks slowly, and the morning stars sink low.
Chang'e must regret she stole the elixir away:
Blue sea, blue sky — her lonely heart, night after night, till day.
--
Chang'e stole the elixir of immortality from her husband, the archer Hou Yi, floated up to the moon, and lives there forever, alone. The sleepless speaker — a lonely woman, a Daoist nun, or the poet himself — understands her: immortality without companionship is an endless night.

== 贾生 | Master Jia
by: 李商隐
form: 七绝
tags: 怀古 失意 民生
scene: night palace candle two
宣室求贤访逐臣，贾生才调更无伦。
可怜夜半虚前席，不问苍生问鬼神。
--
Seeking worthies, the emperor summoned back the banished man;
Master Jia's brilliance was beyond compare.
At midnight, sadly, the emperor drew his mat close — in vain:
He asked not of the people, but of ghosts and gods up there.
--
Jia Yi, the brilliant young statesman of the Han, had been exiled; Emperor Wen recalled him and talked with him late into the night in the Xuanshi Hall, leaning closer and closer on his mat — about ghosts and spirits. Li Shangyin uses the story to mock rulers who chased Daoist mysticism (several Tang emperors died of "immortality" elixirs) while neglecting their people.

== 无题（来是空言去绝踪） | Untitled (Your Coming Was an Empty Word)
by: 李商隐
form: 七律
tags: 爱情 相思 夜
scene: night crescent tower candle window
来是空言去绝踪，月斜楼上五更钟。
梦为远别啼难唤，书被催成墨未浓。
蜡照半笼金翡翠，麝熏微度绣芙蓉。
刘郎已恨蓬山远，更隔蓬山一万重。
--
Your coming was an empty word; you've gone without a trace;
The moon slants on the tower; the fifth-watch bell is tolled.
In dreams we parted far; I wept, and could not call or hold;
I rushed this letter — the ink's too pale, not thick nor bold.
The candlelight half-veils the gold kingfisher quilt;
The musk scent faintly drifts through the embroidered lotus' fold.
Young Liu already grieved that Penglai Mountain was so far —
And you're beyond Penglai by ten thousand mountains' fold.
--
In a Han legend, Liu Chen met two fairies in the Tiantai Mountains, stayed half a year, and returned to find seven generations had passed — he could never find them again. The lover is now even further than that fairyland. The candle half-lighting the kingfisher-embroidered quilt and the drifting musk are the traces of a night together.

== 隋宫（紫泉宫殿） | The Sui Palace
by: 李商隐
form: 七律
tags: 怀古 柳 鸟
scene: dusk palace willow crows river sail
紫泉宫殿锁烟霞，欲取芜城作帝家。
玉玺不缘归日角，锦帆应是到天涯。
于今腐草无萤火，终古垂杨有暮鸦。
地下若逢陈后主，岂宜重问后庭花。
--
The halls of Purple Spring lay locked in mist and rosy haze;
He wanted to make Wucheng his imperial home.
Had the jade seal not gone to the man with the sun-horned brow,
His brocade sails would have reached the world's end, to roam.
Today, in the rotting grass, there are no fireflies;
Forever, on the drooping willows, evening crows remain.
If underground he meets the Last Lord of Chen,
How could he ask for "Flowers of the Rear Court" again?
--
Emperor Yang of Sui (r. 604–618) left his capital for pleasure cruises to Yangzhou (Wucheng) in dragon boats with brocade sails, bankrupting the state; he was murdered, and the empire passed to Li Yuan, founder of the Tang, whose "sun-horned" forehead was a sign of a destined emperor. Yang had fireflies gathered by the bushel to light his night outings, and planted the willows along the Grand Canal. The Last Lord of Chen lost his kingdom — to the Sui — while composing the song "Flowers of the Rear Courtyard". The two decadent rulers would have much to discuss in the underworld.

== 蝉 | The Cicada
by: 李商隐
form: 五律
tags: 咏物 失意 夏 人生
scene: summer dusk willow cottage
本以高难饱，徒劳恨费声。
五更疏欲断，一树碧无情。
薄宦梗犹泛，故园芜已平。
烦君最相警，我亦举家清。
--
Living up high, you can hardly eat your fill;
In vain you cry, resenting the voice you spend.
By the fifth watch your cries grow faint, near still;
The whole green tree is heartless to the end.
A petty official, I drift like a broken spray;
My home garden's overgrown with weeds by now.
I thank you for your stern warning, cicada — I'd say
My whole household is as pure and poor as you, I vow.
--
The cicada, believed to live on dew alone, was a symbol of the incorruptible scholar. Li Shangyin, trapped in minor posts by court factions, sees himself in the insect that sings itself hoarse on a tree that does not care.

== 落花 | Falling Flowers
by: 李商隐
form: 五律
tags: 花 春 失意 时光
scene: dusk spring petals pavilion sun
高阁客竟去，小园花乱飞。
参差连曲陌，迢递送斜晖。
肠断未忍扫，眼穿仍欲归。
芳心向春尽，所得是沾衣。
--
The guests have left the high pavilion at last;
In the little garden, petals whirl and fly.
In tangled drifts they line the winding paths;
Far off, they see the slanting sun go by.
Heartbroken, I can't bring myself to sweep;
I gaze till my eyes ache, and long for their return.
Their fragrant hearts have spent themselves on spring —
And all they get is tears that wet my clothes in turn.
--
Written around 846, when Li Shangyin was idle at home after his mother's death, his career stalled. The petals that give their "fragrant heart" to spring and receive only tears in return are his own devotion, unrewarded.
