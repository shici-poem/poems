== 木兰花·拟古决绝词柬友 | Magnolia: A Farewell in the Old Style, for a Friend
by: 纳兰性德
form: 词
tags: 爱情 离别 秋 怀古
scene: night autumn moon palace window
人生若只如初见，何事秋风悲画扇。
等闲变却故人心，却道故人心易变。

骊山语罢清宵半，泪雨霖铃终不怨。
何如薄幸锦衣郎，比翼连枝当日愿。
--
If life could always be as when we first met,
Why would the autumn wind make the painted fan grieve?
You changed your heart so easily, without regret,
And say it's hearts of old friends that are quick to leave.

At midnight on Mount Li their whispered vows were made;
Though tears fell like the rain and bells, she did not blame.
You can't compare with that faithless lord in brocade —
At least he swore "wing to wing, branches joined", all the same.
--
Nalan Xingde's most famous line opens the lyric: 人生若只如初见 — "if only life could always be like our first meeting". The autumn fan recalls Lady Ban, discarded like a fan when summer ends; the second stanza recalls Emperor Xuanzong and Consort Yang, who vowed eternal love on Mount Li, and the "bells in the rain" the emperor heard after her death. Written "in the style of an old poem of final farewell", it may be about a lover — or a friend.

== 长相思（山一程） | Endless Longing (A Stretch of Hills)
by: 纳兰性德
form: 词
tags: 思乡 边塞 冬 雪 夜 羁旅
scene: night winter snow wind mountains fort lantern
山一程，水一程，身向榆关那畔行，夜深千帐灯。

风一更，雪一更，聒碎乡心梦不成，故园无此声。
--
A stretch of hills, a stretch of streams;
We march toward the far side of Yuguan Pass;
Deep in the night, a thousand tents' lamps gleam.

A watch of wind, a watch of snow;
Their din breaks my homesick heart — I cannot dream;
In my old garden, sounds like these I'd never know.
--
1682: Nalan Xingde, an imperial bodyguard, accompanied the Kangxi Emperor beyond Shanhai Pass to the ancestral tombs in Manchuria. The plain repetitions march like the journey itself; "late at night, the lamps of a thousand tents" is one of the grandest images in the *ci* — and the homesick young man inside one of those tents cannot sleep.

== 浣溪沙（谁念西风独自凉） | Washing Silk in the Stream (Who Cares That I'm Cold in the West Wind?)
by: 纳兰性德
form: 词
tags: 悼亡 爱情 秋 时光
scene: dusk autumn sun leaves window wind person
谁念西风独自凉，萧萧黄叶闭疏窗，沉思往事立残阳。

被酒莫惊春睡重，赌书消得泼茶香，当时只道是寻常。
--
Who cares that I'm alone and cold in the west wind?
The yellow leaves rustle; I close the lattice tight;
Lost in memories, I stand there in the fading light.

When I was drunk, she let me sleep in spring, and didn't wake me;
We'd bet on books, and laugh, and spill the fragrant tea —
At the time, it all just seemed so ordinary.
--
An elegy for Nalan Xingde's wife, Lu Shi, who died in 1677 after three years of marriage. The memories are tiny: she let him sleep off his wine; they played the game Li Qingzhao and her husband once played — betting on which page of which book a quotation came from — and laughed till they spilled the tea. The last line, 当时只道是寻常, "at the time, we thought it all so ordinary", is one of the most heartbreaking in Chinese.

== 蝶恋花（辛苦最怜天上月） | Butterflies Love Flowers (How I Pity the Toiling Moon)
by: 纳兰性德
form: 词
tags: 悼亡 爱情 月 春 秋
scene: night moon window swallows butterfly flowers
辛苦最怜天上月，一昔如环，昔昔都成玦。
若似月轮终皎洁，不辞冰雪为卿热。

无那尘缘容易绝，燕子依然，软踏帘钩说。
唱罢秋坟愁未歇，春丛认取双栖蝶。
--
How I pity the toiling moon up in the sky:
Full as a ring for just one night,
And every other night a broken jade.
If only you'd stay round like the moon, forever bright,
I'd gladly brave the ice and snow to keep you warm, unafraid.

But earthly bonds are broken all too soon;
The swallows, as they always do,
Tread lightly on the curtain hook and chatter.
I sing the autumn graveside songs, but grief has not passed;
Among spring's flowers I'll find two butterflies — and know they're us at last.
--
Another elegy for his wife. The moon is full only one night a month — like their brief happiness. "I'd warm you through ice and snow" recalls Xun Can, who, when his wife had a fever in winter, stood out in the cold and then lay against her to cool her. The ending recalls the Butterfly Lovers, Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai, who became a pair of butterflies after death.

== 画堂春（一生一代一双人） | Spring in the Painted Hall (One Life, One Pair)
by: 纳兰性德
form: 词
tags: 爱情 相思 春
scene: spring river bridge moon person
一生一代一双人，争教两处销魂。
相思相望不相亲，天为谁春？

浆向蓝桥易乞，药成碧海难奔。
若容相访饮牛津，相对忘贫。
--
One life, one lifetime, one pair made to be —
Why must we break our hearts, apart, in two places?
We long for each other, we gaze, but can't be close —
For whom does heaven send its springtime graces?

Begging a drink at Blue Bridge would be easy;
But even with the elixir, I can't fly to the moon above.
If I could cross to the Ford of the Drinking Ox,
We'd face each other, poor, and forget it all for love.
--
A love made for a lifetime but kept apart — perhaps Nalan's first love, said to have been taken into the palace. Pei Hang won a fairy bride at Blue Bridge by begging a drink of water; Chang'e flew to the moon with the elixir and could never return; the Herdboy and the Weaver Girl meet only once a year at the Ford of the Drinking Ox. If only he could reach her, poverty would mean nothing.

== 竹石 | Bamboo and Rock
by: 郑燮
form: 七绝
tags: 咏物 哲理 壮志 风
scene: day bamboo mountains wind
咬定青山不放松，立根原在破岩中。
千磨万击还坚劲，任尔东西南北风。
--
It bites into the green hill, and won't let go;
Its roots are planted in a crack of broken stone.
Ground down a thousand times, struck ten thousand, it stays strong —
Blow, winds, from east or west, from south or north — blow on!
--
Inscribed by Zheng Banqiao on a painting of bamboo growing out of rock. The bamboo, rooted in a crevice and unbowed by any wind, is a character no pressure can bend — one of the most quoted poems of integrity in Chinese.

== 潍县署中画竹呈年伯包大中丞括 | Painting Bamboo at the Weixian Yamen, for Governor Bao
by: 郑燮
form: 七绝
tags: 民生 咏物 夜
scene: night bamboo window cottage person wind
衙斋卧听萧萧竹，疑是民间疾苦声。
些小吾曹州县吏，一枝一叶总关情。
--
Lying in my study, I hear the bamboo's whispering sound,
And wonder if it's the people's suffering crying out.
We're only small officials of a county or a town —
But every branch and every leaf is what we care about.
--
Written in 1746, when Zheng Banqiao was magistrate of Weixian (Shandong) during a famine. He opened the granaries without waiting for permission, and was later dismissed. He painted bamboo and sent it with this poem to the provincial governor: even a minor official must feel every leaf of the people's suffering.

== 题石头记 | On The Story of the Stone
by: 曹雪芹
form: 五绝
tags: 人生 失意 哲理
scene: night candle window person
满纸荒唐言，一把辛酸泪。
都云作者痴，谁解其中味？
--
Pages and pages of absurd words,
A handful of bitter tears.
They all say the author is a fool —
But who has tasted what's within, through all these years?
--
The verse Cao Xueqin set at the opening of *Dream of the Red Chamber* (also called *The Story of the Stone*), the greatest Chinese novel, written in poverty over ten years after the ruin of his family. The "absurd words" are the novel's dreamlike fiction; the "bitter tears", its truth.

== 所见 | What I Saw
by: 袁枚
form: 五绝
tags: 童趣 夏 田园 音乐
scene: summer pine fields person
牧童骑黄牛，歌声振林樾。
意欲捕鸣蝉，忽然闭口立。
--
A herd-boy rides upon a yellow ox;
His song rings out and shakes the woodland shade.
He wants to catch a singing cicada —
He suddenly shuts his mouth and stands, not a sound made.
--
A child in a split second: loud singing, then frozen silence as he spots a cicada. Yuan Mei championed poetry of "natural spirit" (性灵) — fresh, spontaneous, true to life — and this is a perfect example.

== 苔 | Moss
by: 袁枚
form: 五绝
tags: 咏物 哲理 春 花
scene: spring grass flowers cottage
白日不到处，青春恰自来。
苔花如米小，也学牡丹开。
--
Where the bright sun never reaches, in the shade,
The green of spring arrives all on its own.
Moss flowers, tiny as grains of rice,
Learn to blossom too, like peonies full-grown.
--
Even the humblest life, in places the sun never reaches, has its own spring and its own flowering. Neglected for two centuries, the poem became famous in 2018 when a village teacher and his pupils sang it on Chinese television, moving the whole country.
