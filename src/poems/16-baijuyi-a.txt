== 赋得古原草送别 | Grass on the Ancient Plain: A Farewell
by: 白居易
form: 五律
tags: 离别 哲理 春 友情
scene: spring grass hills wall two rider
离离原上草，一岁一枯荣。
野火烧不尽，春风吹又生。
远芳侵古道，晴翠接荒城。
又送王孙去，萋萋满别情。
--
How lush the grass upon the plain!
Each year it withers, then grows green again.
The wildfires cannot burn it all away;
The spring wind blows, and it springs back to stay.
Its distant fragrance creeps across the ancient road;
Its sunlit green runs to the ruined town's abode.
Once more I see a friend go on his way —
The grass grows thick with all I cannot say.
--
Written at sixteen as an exercise on a set theme ("Composed on the Theme…", 赋得). When the young Bai Juyi brought his poems to the capital, the writer Gu Kuang joked about his name — "Juyi" means "living easily", and "rice is dear in Chang'an; living here is not easy" — until he read the second couplet and exclaimed, "With lines like these, living here will be easy!" "Wildfire cannot burn it out; the spring wind blows and it grows again" became a proverb for indomitable life.

== 忆江南（其一） | Remembering the South (I)
by: 白居易
form: 词
tags: 春 江河 思乡 花
scene: dawn spring sun river flowers willow boat
江南好，风景旧曾谙。
日出江花红胜火，春来江水绿如蓝。
能不忆江南？
--
The South is fair —
Its scenery I knew so well:
At sunrise, riverside flowers redder than fire;
In spring, the river's water green as indigo's spell.
How could I not remember the South?
--
Written in old age in Luoyang, remembering his years as governor of Hangzhou and Suzhou. One of the earliest *ci* lyrics by a major poet, set to the tune "Remembering the South". The colours — fire-red and indigo-green — are pure and intense, like memory itself.

== 钱塘湖春行 | A Spring Walk by Qiantang Lake
by: 白居易
form: 七律
tags: 春 山水 鸟 花 柳
scene: spring lake willow swallows oriole flowers horse temple
孤山寺北贾亭西，水面初平云脚低。
几处早莺争暖树，谁家新燕啄春泥。
乱花渐欲迷人眼，浅草才能没马蹄。
最爱湖东行不足，绿杨阴里白沙堤。
--
North of Gushan Temple, west of Lord Jia's pavilion,
The lake has just come level; the clouds hang low and near.
Here and there, early orioles vie for sunny trees;
Whose new swallows peck the spring mud here?
A riot of flowers begins to dazzle the eye;
The tender grass just covers the horses' hooves.
Best I love the lake's east side — I can't walk it enough:
The white-sand causeway in the green willows' shade it moves.
--
Hangzhou's West Lake (then called Qiantang Lake) in early spring, when Bai Juyi was its governor (822–824). Every detail is "just beginning" — early orioles, new swallows, grass just tall enough to hide a hoof. The Bai Causeway, the white-sand dyke he loved, still carries his name.

== 暮江吟 | Song of the River at Dusk
by: 白居易
form: 七绝
tags: 秋 江河 月 夜
scene: dusk autumn sun river crescent
一道残阳铺水中，半江瑟瑟半江红。
可怜九月初三夜，露似真珠月似弓。
--
A ray of the setting sun lies spread across the stream:
Half the river emerald-cold, and half aglow with red.
How lovely is this night, the third of the ninth moon:
Dew like real pearls; the moon a bow overhead.
--
Two pictures in one quatrain: sunset with the river half in shadow (瑟瑟, the blue-green of a precious stone) and half aflame; then night, with dewdrops like pearls and a slender crescent moon like a drawn bow.

== 大林寺桃花 | Peach Blossoms at Dalin Temple
by: 白居易
form: 七绝
tags: 春 花 禅 山 哲理
scene: spring peach temple mountains clouds
人间四月芳菲尽，山寺桃花始盛开。
长恨春归无觅处，不知转入此中来。
--
In the world below, the April flowers are gone;
At this mountain temple, peach blossoms just begin.
I always grieved that spring went off and left no trace —
Not knowing it had come up here, and settled in.
--
Mount Lu, 817. High on the mountain the season lags behind the plain, so the poet finds spring still in full bloom. The small scientific observation becomes a happy surprise: spring has not vanished — it simply climbed the mountain.

== 问刘十九 | A Question for Liu the Nineteenth
by: 白居易
form: 五绝
tags: 饮酒 友情 冬 雪
scene: dusk winter snow cottage jar cup window
绿蚁新醅酒，红泥小火炉。
晚来天欲雪，能饮一杯无？
--
New-brewed wine with green froth like ants;
A little stove of red clay, burning low.
Evening falls, and the sky threatens snow —
Will you come and drink a cup or no?
--
A tiny invitation that every Chinese reader treasures: the "green ants" are the bubbles floating on fresh unfiltered rice wine; the red stove glows; snow is coming. Who could refuse?

== 卖炭翁 | The Old Charcoal Seller
by: 白居易
form: 乐府
tags: 民生 冬 雪
scene: winter snow wall ox elder rider smoke
卖炭翁，伐薪烧炭南山中。
满面尘灰烟火色，两鬓苍苍十指黑。
卖炭得钱何所营？身上衣裳口中食。
可怜身上衣正单，心忧炭贱愿天寒。
夜来城外一尺雪，晓驾炭车辗冰辙。
牛困人饥日已高，市南门外泥中歇。
翩翩两骑来是谁？黄衣使者白衫儿。
手把文书口称敕，回车叱牛牵向北。
一车炭，千余斤，宫使驱将惜不得。
半匹红纱一丈绫，系向牛头充炭直。
--
The old charcoal seller
Cuts wood and burns charcoal in the Southern Hills.
His face is grimed with dust and ash, the colour of smoke and fire;
His temples are grey, his ten fingers black as the coal he fills.
What does he want with the money he gets for his charcoal?
Clothes on his back and food in his mouth — no more.
Pitiful — his clothes are thin, and yet
He hopes for cold, afraid the price will be poor.
Last night a foot of snow fell outside the town;
At dawn he drives his cart through ruts of ice.
The ox is weary, he is hungry, the sun is high;
He rests in the mud outside the south market gate, to wait a price.
Who are those two riders, prancing on their way?
A eunuch in yellow, and his boy in white.
Document in hand, they shout, "Imperial decree!"
And turn the cart, and drive the ox north, out of sight.
A cartload of charcoal — over a thousand pounds —
The palace envoys drive it off; he can't hold on.
Half a bolt of red gauze and ten feet of silk
They tie to the ox's head as payment — and are gone.
--
One of Bai Juyi's *New Yuefu*, written to expose abuses. Palace eunuchs would "buy" goods in the markets of Chang'an at whatever price they liked. The old man's wish — cold weather, though he is freezing — is one of the most poignant lines in Chinese. A worthless scrap of silk pays for his whole winter's labour.

== 池上 | On the Pond
by: 白居易
form: 五绝
tags: 童趣 夏 花
scene: summer lotus lake boat person
小娃撑小艇，偷采白莲回。
不解藏踪迹，浮萍一道开。
--
A little child poles a little boat,
Sneaking home with white lotus she has picked.
She hasn't learned to hide her tracks —
Through the duckweed, a clear path has been flicked.
--
A comic snapshot: the child thinks the theft is secret, but the open lane through the floating duckweed gives her away.

== 夜雪 | Night Snow
by: 白居易
form: 五绝
tags: 冬 雪 夜
scene: night winter snow bamboo window cottage
已讶衾枕冷，复见窗户明。
夜深知雪重，时闻折竹声。
--
I'm surprised my quilt and pillow are so cold,
Then see the window has grown bright.
Deep in the night, I know the snow is heavy:
Now and then, I hear bamboo snap in the night.
--
Snow is never seen directly: it is felt as cold, seen as a glow on the window-paper, and heard as bamboo stems cracking under its weight.

== 花非花 | A Flower That Is Not a Flower
by: 白居易
form: 杂言
tags: 爱情 相思 夜 人生
scene: night mist clouds flowers moon
花非花，雾非雾。
夜半来，天明去。
来如春梦几多时？去似朝云无觅处。
--
A flower, yet not a flower;
A mist, yet not a mist.
She comes at midnight,
She leaves at dawn's first tryst.
She comes like a spring dream — how long can it last?
She goes like morning cloud, gone without trace, gone past.
--
A riddling love song with no subject named — a lover's secret visits, perhaps, or a dream, or life itself. The "morning cloud" recalls the goddess of Mount Wu, who visited a king in his dream and was "morning cloud and evening rain".

== 观刈麦 | Watching the Wheat Harvest
by: 白居易
form: 五古
tags: 民生 夏 田园
scene: summer sun fields person lady child
田家少闲月，五月人倍忙。
夜来南风起，小麦覆陇黄。
妇姑荷箪食，童稚携壶浆，
相随饷田去，丁壮在南冈。
足蒸暑土气，背灼炎天光，
力尽不知热，但惜夏日长。
复有贫妇人，抱子在其旁，
右手秉遗穗，左臂悬敝筐。
听其相顾言，闻者为悲伤。
家田输税尽，拾此充饥肠。
今我何功德？曾不事农桑。
吏禄三百石，岁晏有余粮。
念此私自愧，尽日不能忘。
--
Farm families have few idle months;
In the fifth month they're twice as busy as before.
Last night the south wind rose, and now
The ripe wheat covers the ridges, yellow to the core.
Wives and mothers carry baskets of food;
The children bring jugs of gruel along;
Together they take the meal to the fields,
Where the men toil on the southern ridge, strong.
Their feet are steamed by the hot earth;
Their backs are scorched by the blazing sun;
Worn out, they don't even feel the heat,
And only wish the long summer day weren't done.
And there's a poor woman, too,
With a baby in her arms, beside the rest;
In her right hand, the ears of grain she's gleaned;
On her left arm, a broken basket pressed.
I hear her talking with the others —
Whoever hears her grieves:
"Our own fields all went to pay the tax;
I glean these to fill our stomachs with the sheaves."
And what merit have I, I ask myself?
I've never farmed or tended silk.
My salary is three hundred bushels;
At year's end, I've grain to spare, and milk.
Thinking of this, I'm secretly ashamed,
And all day long I can't forget the shame I bear.
--
806, when Bai Juyi was a county magistrate near Chang'an. The farmers want the day to be longer so they can harvest more, though it is killing them; the gleaning widow has lost her land to taxes. The poem's last lines — an official confessing he has done nothing to deserve his salary — are rare and remarkable in any literature.
