== 安定城楼 | The Tower at Anding
by: 李商隐
form: 七律
tags: 壮志 失意 怀古 柳
scene: day tower wall willow river
迢递高城百尺楼，绿杨枝外尽汀洲。
贾生年少虚垂涕，王粲春来更远游。
永忆江湖归白发，欲回天地入扁舟。
不知腐鼠成滋味，猜意鹓雏竟未休。
--
The high wall winds afar; the tower stands a hundred feet;
Beyond the green willow boughs, there's nothing but isles and shore.
Young Jia Yi shed tears for the state, in vain;
Wang Can in spring went wandering farther than before.
I've always dreamed of going home to rivers and lakes, white-haired —
But first to turn the world around, then sail off in a skiff, set free.
I never dreamed a rotting rat could be such a delicacy,
That they'd suspect the phoenix chick, and never let it be.
--
838: the young Li Shangyin, having failed a placement exam because of factional politics, climbs a tower at Anding. Jia Yi wept over the empire's troubles as a young man; Wang Can, an exile, wrote a famous poem from a tower. In *Zhuangzi*, an owl clutching a rotten rat screeched at a passing phoenix — which eats only bamboo seed — fearing it wanted the rat. His rivals are the owls; office is the rat.

== 霜月 | Frost and Moon
by: 李商隐
form: 七绝
tags: 秋 月 夜 天空
scene: night autumn moon frost tower geese river
初闻征雁已无蝉，百尺楼高水接天。
青女素娥俱耐冷，月中霜里斗婵娟。
--
When first I hear the migrating geese, the cicadas are gone;
From the hundred-foot tower, the water joins the sky.
The Frost Maid and the Moon Goddess both can bear the cold —
In moonlight and in frost they vie, to see whose grace is high.
--
Qingnü (青女) was the goddess of frost; Sue (素娥) is Chang'e, the moon goddess. On a cold, clear autumn night, the white frost and the white moonlight become two goddesses competing in pure, cold beauty.

== 宿骆氏亭寄怀崔雍崔衮 | Staying at the Luo Pavilion, Thinking of Cui Yong and Cui Gun
by: 李商隐
form: 七绝
tags: 秋 雨 友情 相思 花
scene: autumn rain lotus lake pavilion bamboo
竹坞无尘水槛清，相思迢递隔重城。
秋阴不散霜飞晚，留得枯荷听雨声。
--
The bamboo dock is free of dust, the railings by the water clear;
My longing reaches far to you, past city walls on walls.
The autumn clouds won't lift, and frost is late this year —
The withered lotus leaves are left to hear the rain that falls.
--
Staying at a friend's pavilion, Li Shangyin thinks of two cousins far away. The last line — keep the withered lotus to listen to the rain — is among the most loved lines in Chinese: even dead lotus leaves have their beauty, as an instrument for the sound of rain. In *Dream of the Red Chamber*, Lin Daiyu says it is the one line of Li Shangyin's she likes.

== 为有 | Because
by: 李商隐
form: 七绝
tags: 爱情 闺怨 女性 春
scene: dawn spring palace window
为有云屏无限娇，凤城寒尽怕春宵。
无端嫁得金龟婿，辜负香衾事早朝。
--
Behind the mica screen, with all her endless charm,
In Phoenix City, now the cold has gone, she fears the nights of spring:
For no good reason she married a man with a golden tortoise —
He leaves their fragrant quilt for the dawn court of the king.
--
A young wife complains that her husband, a high official — the golden tortoise pouch was a badge of the third rank — leaves her bed before dawn for the morning audience. Light and witty, with a hint that ambition costs love.

== 瑶池 | The Jasper Pool
by: 李商隐
form: 七绝
tags: 怀古 哲理
scene: day palace lake horse clouds
瑶池阿母绮窗开，黄竹歌声动地哀。
八骏日行三万里，穆王何事不重来？
--
At Jasper Pool the Queen Mother opens her latticed window wide;
The "Yellow Bamboo" song rings out, so sad it shakes the ground.
His eight steeds ran thirty thousand li a day, with pride —
So why has King Mu never come back round?
--
King Mu of Zhou, in legend, drove his eight divine horses to the Jasper Pool in the far west to feast with the Queen Mother of the West, who offered immortality and asked him to return. He never did — he died. The poem mocks Tang emperors who sought elixirs of eternal life: even the fastest horses cannot outrun death.

== 马嵬（其二） | Mawei (II)
by: 李商隐
form: 七律
tags: 怀古 爱情 悼亡
scene: night stars palace horse flag
海外徒闻更九州，他生未卜此生休。
空闻虎旅传宵柝，无复鸡人报晓筹。
此日六军同驻马，当时七夕笑牵牛。
如何四纪为天子，不及卢家有莫愁。
--
In vain they told of nine more continents across the sea —
The next life is uncertain; this one's done.
He hears the tiger guards beat out the watches, emptily;
No cockman calls the palace dawn — not one.
That day the Six Armies all reined in their horses there;
That Seventh Night, they'd laughed at the Herdboy's plight.
How could he, four decades the Son of Heaven, fare
Worse than the Lu clan, who kept Mochou home each night?
--
Li Shangyin's sharp reply to the romance of *The Song of Everlasting Sorrow*. The tale that Consort Yang lives on in an immortals' island beyond the sea is empty talk. On the Seventh Night, when the Herdboy and the Weaver Girl meet just once a year, the imperial lovers pitied the stars — yet after nearly fifty years on the throne, Xuanzong could not protect his beloved as well as a commoner of the folk song, whose wife Mochou lived happily at home.

== 春雨 | Spring Rain
by: 李商隐
form: 七律
tags: 爱情 相思 春 雨 夜
scene: night spring rain tower lantern geese
怅卧新春白袷衣，白门寥落意多违。
红楼隔雨相望冷，珠箔飘灯独自归。
远路应悲春晼晚，残宵犹得梦依稀。
玉珰缄札何由达，万里云罗一雁飞。
--
Downcast, I lie in my white robe, the spring just new;
At the White Gate, all is bleak — nothing has come true.
Through the rain, the red tower looks cold, far off;
Past bead curtains and drifting lamps, I walk home alone, too.
On your long road, you must grieve as spring grows late;
In the night's last hours I still dream — faint, but of you.
How can the jade earring and sealed letter reach you? —
Ten thousand li of cloud-net, and one wild goose flying through.
--
The poet returns to the red tower where he once met his love and finds her gone, far away. The "White Gate" is Nanjing, a stock place for lovers' meetings. A jade earring was a love-token; the lone goose, a letter-carrier, is lost in a net of clouds.

== 晚晴 | Clearing at Evening
by: 李商隐
form: 五律
tags: 夏 雨 哲理 鸟 闲适
scene: dusk summer sun tower window birds grass
深居俯夹城，春去夏犹清。
天意怜幽草，人间重晚晴。
并添高阁迥，微注小窗明。
越鸟巢干后，归飞体更轻。
--
Living secluded, I look down on the walled lane's way;
Spring has gone, but summer still is clear and bright.
Heaven pities the shy grass in the shade;
The world treasures the clearing at evening light.
It lengthens the view from the high pavilion;
It gently pours brightness through my small window's frame.
The southern birds' nests are dry at last —
Flying home, they're lighter than when they came.
--
Guilin, 847, as a long rain clears at evening. 天意怜幽草，人间重晚晴 — "Heaven pities the hidden grass; the world treasures the late clearing" — is quoted for kindness to the overlooked and for the beauty of a good old age. Li Shangyin had just found a post after years of frustration; the light, dry birds are his relief.

== 花下醉 | Drunk Among the Flowers
by: 李商隐
form: 七绝
tags: 花 饮酒 夜 春
scene: night spring flowers candle person
寻芳不觉醉流霞，倚树沉眠日已斜。
客散酒醒深夜后，更持红烛赏残花。
--
Seeking flowers, I got drunk on rosy wine before I knew;
I leaned against a tree and slept; the sun had slanted low.
The guests gone, I woke sober, deep into the night —
And took a red candle out to see the last blooms glow.
--
"Flowing Rosy Cloud" (流霞) was the wine of the immortals. Sober at midnight, the poet cannot bear to waste the flowers' last hours. Su Shi later borrowed the idea: "afraid the flowers will fall asleep in the night, I light a tall candle to shine on their red faces."

== 北青萝 | North Green Vine
by: 李商隐
form: 五律
tags: 禅 山 隐逸 秋
scene: dusk autumn mountains temple leaves clouds person
残阳西入崦，茅屋访孤僧。
落叶人何在，寒云路几层。
独敲初夜磬，闲倚一枝藤。
世界微尘里，吾宁爱与憎。
--
The setting sun sinks behind the western peak;
I come to a thatched hut, a lone monk to seek.
Among the falling leaves — where can he be?
Through cold clouds, how many tiers of path to see?
Alone, he strikes the stone chime as night begins;
At ease, he leans upon a single staff of vine.
The whole wide world lies in a mote of dust —
Why should I love or hate? Why should I pine?
--
A visit to a hermit monk on a mountain called Green Vine. The ending is pure Buddhism: if the whole universe can lie within a speck of dust, the poet's own loves and hatreds are as nothing.

== 无题（飒飒东风细雨来） | Untitled (Soughing, the East Wind)
by: 李商隐
form: 七律
tags: 爱情 相思 春 雨
scene: spring rain lotus lake palace wind
飒飒东风细雨来，芙蓉塘外有轻雷。
金蟾啮锁烧香入，玉虎牵丝汲井回。
贾氏窥帘韩掾少，宓妃留枕魏王才。
春心莫共花争发，一寸相思一寸灰。
--
Soughing, the east wind comes with fine rain;
Beyond the lotus pond, a soft thunder rolls.
The gold-toad lock is fast — yet incense smoke slips in;
The jade-tiger winch draws silk rope, and water climbs the well.
Lady Jia peeked through the curtain, for young Han was fair;
Princess Fu gave her pillow to the Prince of Wei, for his flair.
O spring heart, don't race the flowers to burst into bloom:
For every inch of longing, an inch of ash lies there.
--
Thunder, rain, lotus, a locked door, a well: hints of a secret love. Even a locked door lets in incense, and even a deep well yields its water — 丝 "silk" again punning on 思 "longing". Lady Jia glimpsed the handsome Han Shou through a screen and secretly married him; the goddess of the Luo River (Princess Fu) was said to have left a pillow for the poet Cao Zhi. The last line, "an inch of longing, an inch of ash", is the bitterest image of love's cost in Chinese — like incense that burns itself away.
