== 摸鱼儿·雁丘词 | Groping for Fish: The Goose Mound
by: 元好问
form: 词
tags: 爱情 悼亡 鸟 雪
scene: dusk winter snow geese clouds mountains river
问世间，情是何物，直教生死相许？
天南地北双飞客，老翅几回寒暑。
欢乐趣，离别苦，就中更有痴儿女。
君应有语，渺万里层云，千山暮雪，只影向谁去？

横汾路，寂寞当年箫鼓，荒烟依旧平楚。
招魂楚些何嗟及，山鬼暗啼风雨。
天也妒，未信与，莺儿燕子俱黄土。
千秋万古，为留待骚人，狂歌痛饮，来访雁丘处。
--
I ask the world: what is this thing called love,
That makes two souls vow to live and die as one?
North and south, through all the sky, two wanderers on the wing —
How many winters and summers have their old wings flown?
The joy of being together,
The pain of being apart —
And among them, one more lovesick than us all.
You must have something to say:
Across ten thousand li of clouds,
A thousand peaks of evening snow —
To whom will your lone shadow go, alone?

Along the road by the Fen River,
The flutes and drums of long ago are silent;
The wild mist lies as ever on the plain.
To call your soul with songs of Chu — what good would sighing do?
The mountain spirits weep in secret in the wind and rain.
Heaven itself is jealous —
I don't believe that you,
Like orioles and swallows, will just turn to yellow dust in vain.
For a thousand autumns, ten thousand years,
You'll wait here for the poets,
Who'll sing wild songs and drink deep,
And come to visit the Goose Mound again.
--
In 1205 the sixteen-year-old Yuan Haowen, on his way to the examinations, met a hunter who had caught a wild goose; its mate, which had escaped, circled crying and then dashed itself to death on the ground. Yuan bought both birds, buried them by the Fen River, and called the grave "Goose Mound". 问世间，情是何物，直教生死相许 — "I ask the world: what is love, that it makes two vow to live and die together?" — became the most famous line about love in Chinese, quoted endlessly (not least in Jin Yong's martial-arts novels).

== 天净沙·秋思 | Sky-Clear Sand: Autumn Thoughts
by: 马致远
form: 散曲
tags: 秋 思乡 羁旅 鸟
scene: dusk autumn sun crows bridge river cottage horse rider
枯藤老树昏鸦，小桥流水人家，古道西风瘦马。
夕阳西下，断肠人在天涯。
--
Withered vines, old trees, crows at dusk;
A little bridge, a flowing stream, a home;
An ancient road, the west wind, a bony horse.
The setting sun goes down in the west —
A heartbroken man at the world's end, alone.
--
The most famous *sanqu* song of all: twenty-eight characters, mostly bare nouns — nine images in the first three lines, and hardly a verb in the whole poem. Wang Guowei said it equalled the best Tang quatrains. The cosy home by the little bridge makes the homeless traveller lonelier still.

== 天净沙·秋 | Sky-Clear Sand: Autumn
by: 白朴
form: 散曲
tags: 秋 山水 鸟 花
scene: dusk autumn sun cottage crows geese hills river leaves chrysanthemum
孤村落日残霞，轻烟老树寒鸦，一点飞鸿影下。
青山绿水，白草红叶黄花。
--
A lonely village, setting sun, the last of the rosy cloud;
Light mist, an old tree, cold crows;
A single wild goose's shadow, flying low.
Green hills, green water;
White grass, red leaves, and yellow flowers aglow.
--
Written to the same tune as Ma Zhiyuan's song, but with the opposite mood: the first half is the usual bleak autumn; the second bursts into five colours — green, green, white, red, yellow — and autumn becomes beautiful.

== 山坡羊·潼关怀古 | Sheep on the Hillside: Thinking of the Past at Tong Pass
by: 张养浩
form: 散曲
tags: 怀古 民生 山 江河
scene: dusk mountains peaks river fort
峰峦如聚，波涛如怒，山河表里潼关路。
望西都，意踌躇。
伤心秦汉经行处，宫阙万间都做了土。
兴，百姓苦；亡，百姓苦。
--
The peaks and ridges crowd as if they'd gathered here;
The waves and torrents roar as if in rage;
Mountain and river, fold on fold, guard the Tong Pass road.
I gaze toward the western capital,
Lost in thought, I stand and wonder.
It grieves me, where the Qin and Han once passed:
Ten thousand palace halls have turned to dust.
Kingdoms rise — the people suffer;
Kingdoms fall — the people suffer.
--
In 1329 the sixty-year-old Zhang Yanghao came out of retirement to bring famine relief to Shaanxi, and died there of exhaustion within months. Passing Tong Pass, the gateway to the old capitals of Qin and Han, he wrote songs on history. The ending — 兴，百姓苦；亡，百姓苦, "rise: the people suffer; fall: the people suffer" — is the most famous verdict on Chinese history ever written.

== 墨梅 | Ink Plum Blossom
by: 王冕
form: 七绝
tags: 咏物 花 哲理 冬
scene: winter plum lake cottage
吾家洗砚池头树，个个花开淡墨痕。
不要人夸好颜色，只留清气满乾坤。
--
The tree that grows beside my ink-stained pond
Has every blossom tinged with ink's pale trace.
It doesn't need the world to praise its colour —
It only means to fill the earth and sky with purity and grace.
--
Wang Mian, the self-taught master of ink plum painting, inscribed this on one of his paintings. The ink-washing pond recalls the calligrapher Wang Xizhi, whose pond turned black from rinsing brushes. Plum blossom painted in ink has no bright colour to win praise — only the "pure spirit" (清气) of integrity, like the painter who refused every offer of office.

== 四块玉·别情 | Four Pieces of Jade: Parting
by: 关汉卿
form: 散曲
tags: 离别 相思 柳 女性
scene: day willow river hills tower person
自送别，心难舍，一点相思几时绝？
凭阑袖拂杨花雪。溪又斜，山又遮，人去也。
--
Since I saw you off,
My heart can't let you go;
This thread of longing — when will it ever end?
I lean on the rail and brush away the willow-down's snow.
The stream curves round,
The hills cut off the view —
And you're gone, out of sight, I know.
--
A woman watches her beloved leave until the winding stream and the hills hide him from view. Plain, colloquial and piercing — the spirit of the Yuan *sanqu*.

== 正宫·端正好（碧云天） | Duanzheng Hao (Blue Clouds Above) — from The Western Chamber
by: 王实甫
form: 散曲
tags: 离别 秋 爱情 鸟
scene: dawn autumn maple geese clouds chrysanthemum pavilion two
碧云天，黄花地，西风紧，北雁南飞。
晓来谁染霜林醉？总是离人泪。
--
Blue clouds above, yellow flowers on the ground,
The west wind blowing hard, the wild geese flying south.
Who dyed the frosted woods so drunken-red at dawn?
It's all the tears of those who part, I've found.
--
From *The Romance of the Western Chamber*: Cui Yingying sees off her lover Zhang Junrui as he leaves for the capital examinations. It borrows Fan Zhongyan's "blue clouds in the sky, yellow leaves on the ground", and its conceit — that the red autumn maples were dyed by the lovers' tears — is the most famous line in Chinese drama.

== 折桂令·春情 | Plucking the Cassia: Spring Feelings
by: 徐再思
form: 散曲
tags: 爱情 相思 春 女性 月
scene: night spring moon candle window person
平生不会相思，才会相思，便害相思。
身似浮云，心如飞絮，气若游丝。
空一缕余香在此，盼千金游子何之。
证候来时，正是何时？灯半昏时，月半明时。
--
All my life I never knew what lovesickness could be;
Just learning lovesickness,
I'm sick with lovesickness, you see.
My body's like a floating cloud,
My heart like drifting down,
My breath as thin as gossamer, and free.
A wisp of lingering fragrance still is here;
The one I long for, far away — where can he be?
When does the sickness strike?
When the lamp is half dim,
When the moon's half bright, and shines on me.
--
A girl's first love, in a song that plays on 相思, "lovesickness", three times in a row. Every symptom is described with comic precision — body like a cloud, heart like a catkin, breath like a silk thread — and the attack comes late at night, when lamp and moon are both half-bright.

== 石灰吟 | Ode to Lime
by: 于谦
form: 七绝
tags: 咏物 壮志 哲理 爱国
scene: day mountains smoke cottage
千锤万凿出深山，烈火焚烧若等闲。
粉骨碎身浑不怕，要留清白在人间。
--
A thousand hammers, ten thousand chisels dig it from the mountain deep;
It treats the raging fires of the kiln as nothing much at all.
Though its bones are crushed and its body ground to dust, it does not fear,
If only it can leave its spotless whiteness in the world for all.
--
Said to have been written by Yu Qian at seventeen, watching lime being made: quarried, burned in a kiln, slaked to powder — and finally used to whitewash walls. It became his creed. He saved Beijing from the Mongols in 1449, and was executed on false charges in 1457, leaving his "pure whiteness" behind.

== 明日歌 | Song of Tomorrow
by: 钱福
form: 七古
tags: 时光 哲理 人生
scene: dusk sun river person
明日复明日，明日何其多。
我生待明日，万事成蹉跎。
世人若被明日累，春去秋来老将至。
朝看水东流，暮看日西坠。
百年明日能几何？请君听我明日歌。
--
Tomorrow and tomorrow — so many tomorrows ahead!
If I keep waiting for tomorrow, nothing will be done.
If the world's people are tied to tomorrow's thread,
Spring goes, autumn comes, and old age has begun.
At dawn I watch the water flowing east;
At dusk I watch the sun sink in the west.
A hundred years — how many tomorrows, at the least?
I beg you, hear my Song of Tomorrow, and do your best.
--
The Ming scholar Qian Fu's warning against putting things off — "tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow" — is taught to every Chinese schoolchild. Several versions exist; this is the common one.

== 桃花庵歌 | Song of the Peach Blossom Hut
by: 唐寅
form: 七古
tags: 隐逸 饮酒 花 人生 哲理
scene: spring peach cottage petals person jar
桃花坞里桃花庵，桃花庵里桃花仙。
桃花仙人种桃树，又摘桃花换酒钱。
酒醒只在花前坐，酒醉还来花下眠。
半醒半醉日复日，花落花开年复年。
但愿老死花酒间，不愿鞠躬车马前。
车尘马足富者趣，酒盏花枝贫者缘。
若将富贵比贫贱，一在平地一在天。
若将贫贱比车马，他得驱驰我得闲。
别人笑我太疯癫，我笑他人看不穿。
不见五陵豪杰墓，无花无酒锄作田。
--
In Peach Blossom Dock there stands a Peach Blossom Hut;
In the Peach Blossom Hut lives a Peach Blossom Immortal, no less.
The Peach Blossom Immortal plants peach trees, and then
Picks peach blossoms to sell, to pay for his wine, I confess.
When I'm sober, I sit in front of the flowers;
When I'm drunk, I come back and sleep beneath them, too.
Half sober, half drunk, day after day;
The flowers fall and bloom, year after year, anew.
I'd rather grow old and die among flowers and wine
Than bow before the carriages and horses of the great.
Carriage dust and horses' hooves — that's the rich man's fun;
A wine cup and a flower spray — that's the poor man's fate.
If you compare the rich and noble with the poor,
One's on the ground, the other's in the sky;
But if you compare the poor with those in carriages,
They get the rushing about — the leisure's mine, say I.
Others laugh at me for being far too mad;
I laugh at them for never seeing through.
Don't you see the tombs of the great lords of the Five Tombs? —
No flowers, no wine: they've all been ploughed to fields, too.
--
Tang Yin (Tang Bohu), the legendary Suzhou painter, barred from office for life by an examination scandal, built a "Peach Blossom Hut" in Suzhou and lived on his paintings and his wine. Repeating "peach blossom" like a folk song, he mocks the rich and powerful, whose grand tombs end up ploughed into farmland. "Others laugh at me for being mad; I laugh at them for not seeing through" is still quoted.

== 蔽月山房 | The Mountain Study Where the Moon Is Hidden
by: 王守仁
form: 七绝
tags: 哲理 月 山 童趣
scene: night moon mountains cottage
山近月远觉月小，便道此山大于月。
若人有眼大如天，当见山高月更阔。
--
The hill is near, the moon is far — so the moon looks small,
And people say the hill is bigger than the moon.
But one whose eyes were wide as the whole sky
Would see the hill is high — but the moon is wider than them all.
--
Written by Wang Yangming at eleven, in a study where a hill hid the moon. A child's observation of perspective becomes a philosopher's lesson: our judgements depend on where we stand, and a wider view changes everything.

== 临江仙（滚滚长江东逝水） | Immortal by the River (On and On the Long River Flows)
by: 杨慎
form: 词
tags: 怀古 人生 江河 饮酒 哲理
scene: dusk sun river mountains fisher boat two
滚滚长江东逝水，浪花淘尽英雄。
是非成败转头空。青山依旧在，几度夕阳红。

白发渔樵江渚上，惯看秋月春风。
一壶浊酒喜相逢。古今多少事，都付笑谈中。
--
On and on, the Long River rolls away to the east;
Its waves have washed away the heroes, every one.
Right and wrong, success and failure — turn your head, all gone;
The green hills still stand as they did;
How many times has the sunset blazed red, and done?

White-haired fishermen and woodsmen on the river isles
Have watched the autumn moons and spring winds come and go.
Over a jug of cloudy wine, they're glad to meet;
Of all the things of past and present, high and low —
They leave them all to laughter and to talk, you know.
--
Written by Yang Shen in exile in Yunnan, as part of a long ballad of Chinese history. Three centuries later it was chosen to open the novel *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*, and as the theme song of the 1994 television series it became known to every Chinese person. All the heroes' rivalries end as stories over wine for old fishermen.

== 韬钤深处 | Deep Among the Military Books
by: 戚继光
form: 五律
tags: 爱国 壮志 海
scene: night stars sea cottage person
小筑暂高枕，忧时旧有盟。
呼樽来揖客，挥麈坐谈兵。
云护牙签满，星含宝剑横。
封侯非我意，但愿海波平。
--
In my small house I rest a while, my pillow high;
To share the nation's cares is an oath I swore long ago.
I call for wine to greet my guests,
And, waving my whisk, sit discussing war and foe.
The clouds guard shelves of ivory-tagged books;
The starlight gleams on the sword laid crosswise there.
To be made a lord is not my wish —
I only pray the waves of the sea be calm and fair.
--
Qi Jiguang was the Ming general who drove the *wokou* pirates from China's south-east coast in the 1550s and 1560s. 封侯非我意，但愿海波平 — "to be made a lord is not my wish; I only hope the sea's waves are calm" — expresses the soldier's true aim: not glory, but peace.

== 皂罗袍（原来姹紫嫣红开遍） | Black Silk Robe (So All the Gorgeous Flowers Have Bloomed) — from The Peony Pavilion
by: 汤显祖
form: 散曲
tags: 春 花 女性 时光 爱情
scene: spring flowers palace pavilion rain boat
原来姹紫嫣红开遍，似这般都付与断井颓垣。
良辰美景奈何天，赏心乐事谁家院？
朝飞暮卷，云霞翠轩；雨丝风片，烟波画船——锦屏人忒看的这韶光贱！
--
So all the gorgeous purples and the reds have bloomed —
And all of them abandoned to the broken well and ruined wall!
A lovely day, a lovely scene — but what's heaven's will?
Who has the joy and pleasure of this garden, after all?
Flying at dawn, furled at dusk —
Rosy clouds round the green pavilion;
Silk threads of rain, a wind in flakes,
The misty waves, the painted boat —
The girl behind her brocade screen has held this spring too cheap, and small!
--
The most famous aria of Kun opera, from Tang Xianzu's *The Peony Pavilion* (1598). Sixteen-year-old Du Liniang, kept indoors all her life, steps into her family's neglected garden for the first time and discovers the full glory of spring — wasted on broken walls, and on her. She goes on to dream of a young scholar, die of love, and return to life.
