== 乌衣巷 | Black Robe Lane
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 怀古 时光 鸟 春
scene: dusk sun swallows bridge cottage grass
朱雀桥边野草花，乌衣巷口夕阳斜。
旧时王谢堂前燕，飞入寻常百姓家。
--
By Vermilion Bridge the wild grass is in flower;
At Black Robe Lane, the sunset slants its light.
The swallows that once nested in the Wang and Xie halls of power
Now fly into the homes of common folk tonight.
--
Black Robe Lane in Nanjing was home to the Wang and Xie clans, the greatest aristocratic families of the 4th and 5th centuries. Four hundred years later the mansions have vanished — but the swallows still return to the same spot, now full of ordinary houses. The rise and fall of the great, seen in a swallow's flight.

== 石头城 | Stone City
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 怀古 月 夜 江河
scene: night moon wall river mountains
山围故国周遭在，潮打空城寂寞回。
淮水东边旧时月，夜深还过女墙来。
--
The hills still ring the fallen capital around;
The tide beats on the empty city, and lonely, ebbs away.
East of the Qinhuai, the moon that once shone here
Still climbs the parapets at midnight, as in the olden day.
--
Stone City was the fortress of Nanjing, capital of six fallen dynasties. Only the hills, the tide and the moon — which shone on all those vanished courts — remain. Bai Juyi said that after this poem, later poets would have nothing left to say about Nanjing.

== 竹枝词（其一） | Bamboo Branch Song (I)
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 爱情 春 柳 江河 雨
scene: spring willow river rain sun boat
杨柳青青江水平，闻郎江上唱歌声。
东边日出西边雨，道是无晴却有晴。
--
Green, green the willows; the river's calm and wide;
I hear my lover singing on the river, drifting by.
Sun in the east, and rain upon the western side —
You'd say there's no sunshine, yet there's sunshine in the sky.
--
Liu Yuxi adapted the "Bamboo Branch" folk songs he heard in exile in the south-west. 晴 (clear weather) and 情 (love) are both pronounced *qíng*, so the last line means both "you'd say it isn't sunny, yet it is" and "you'd think he has no feelings for me, yet he does" — a girl's uncertainty in a sun-shower.

== 秋词（其一） | Autumn Song (I)
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 秋 天空 壮志 鸟
scene: autumn day crane clouds mountains
自古逢秋悲寂寥，我言秋日胜春朝。
晴空一鹤排云上，便引诗情到碧霄。
--
Since ancient times, men sigh at autumn's lonely gloom;
I say that autumn days surpass the mornings of the spring.
In the clear sky, one crane soars up through the clouds' plume,
And draws my poet's heart into the blue upon its wing.
--
Written in exile in 805. Against a thousand years of poems mourning autumn, Liu Yuxi declares autumn better than spring — the crane breaking through the clouds is his own unbroken spirit. A rare poem of defiant optimism.

== 酬乐天扬州初逢席上见赠 | Reply to Bai Juyi at Our First Meeting in Yangzhou
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七律
tags: 友情 人生 哲理 饮酒 失意
scene: spring river sail boat two cup
巴山楚水凄凉地，二十三年弃置身。
怀旧空吟闻笛赋，到乡翻似烂柯人。
沉舟侧畔千帆过，病树前头万木春。
今日听君歌一曲，暂凭杯酒长精神。
--
In Ba's bleak hills and Chu's cold streams I spent
Twenty-three years, discarded and alone.
I mourn lost friends, and chant "The Flute" in lament;
Home now, I'm like the woodsman who came back to a world unknown.
Beside the sunken ship, a thousand sails go by;
Before the withered tree, ten thousand trees break into spring.
Today I've heard you sing a song for me —
Let this cup of wine lift up my spirits on its wing.
--
Liu Yuxi met Bai Juyi in Yangzhou in 826, after twenty-three years of exile. "Hearing the Flute" is a rhapsody mourning executed friends; the woodsman who stopped to watch immortals play chess went home to find his axe-handle rotted and a century gone. Then comes the great couplet — the sunken ship and the withered tree, while life sails and blooms on all around — which Bai Juyi called divine: there is no self-pity, only the knowledge that life goes on.

== 望洞庭 | Gazing at Dongting
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 秋 月 山水 夜
scene: night autumn moon lake hills
湖光秋月两相和，潭面无风镜未磨。
遥望洞庭山水翠，白银盘里一青螺。
--
The lake's sheen and the autumn moon are blended into one;
The calm pool's face — a bronze mirror, not yet polished, dim.
From afar I gaze at Dongting's isle, so green —
A green conch lying on a silver platter's rim.
--
Dongting Lake on a still autumn night: the lake is an old bronze mirror, slightly misted; Junshan Island at its centre is a small green conch shell on a silver plate. A perfect miniature of a vast scene.

== 西塞山怀古 | Thinking of the Past at Xisai Mountain
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七律
tags: 怀古 战争 江河 秋
scene: autumn river mountains reeds sail fort
王濬楼船下益州，金陵王气黯然收。
千寻铁锁沉江底，一片降幡出石头。
人世几回伤往事，山形依旧枕寒流。
今逢四海为家日，故垒萧萧芦荻秋。
--
Wang Jun's towered warships sailed down from Yizhou's shore;
The royal aura over Jinling faded and was gone.
The iron chains, a thousand fathoms long, sank to the river floor;
From Stone City a flag of surrender was hoisted on.
How many times have men lamented what has passed?
The hill still lies, as ever, pillowed on the cold stream's flow.
Now the four seas are one family, at last —
The old forts sigh among the autumn reeds below.
--
In 280 the Jin general Wang Jun sailed great towered warships down the Yangtze from Sichuan to conquer the kingdom of Wu; Wu's iron chains across the river at Xisai Mountain were melted with torches, and its last ruler surrendered at Stone City (Nanjing). Kingdoms rise and fall; the mountain stays. The ending is a quiet warning: the empire is united now — but the ruined forts in the reeds show how fragile unity is.

== 浪淘沙（其一） | Waves Washing Sand (I)
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 江河 天空 爱情
scene: day river desert stars clouds
九曲黄河万里沙，浪淘风簸自天涯。
如今直上银河去，同到牵牛织女家。
--
The Yellow River's nine great bends, ten thousand li of sand,
Washed by waves and tossed by winds, come from the edge of sky.
Now let us ride straight up the Silver River, hand in hand,
To the Herdboy's and the Weaver's home, way up high.
--
An old legend held that the Yellow River joins the Milky Way (the "Silver River"), and that a man who sailed up it reached the stars. In a folk-song voice, the poet proposes to follow the river all the way to the two star-lovers.

== 浪淘沙（其八） | Waves Washing Sand (VIII)
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 哲理 人生 江河 失意
scene: day river person reeds sun
莫道谗言如浪深，莫言迁客似沙沉。
千淘万漉虽辛苦，吹尽狂沙始到金。
--
Don't say that slander runs as deep as waves can go;
Don't say an exile sinks like sand, to stay below.
Though panning, sifting countless times is hard and slow,
When all the sand is blown away, the gold will show.
--
Slandered and exiled for twenty-three years, Liu Yuxi answers with a gold-panner's patience: truth, like gold, survives every sifting. 千淘万漉, "a thousand washings, ten thousand siftings", became an idiom for hard-won results.

== 玄都观桃花 | Peach Blossoms at Xuandu Temple
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 春 花 失意 哲理
scene: spring peach temple rider petals
紫陌红尘拂面来，无人不道看花回。
玄都观里桃千树，尽是刘郎去后栽。
--
Along the purple lanes, red dust brushes every face;
No one comes by who hasn't just been out to see the flowers.
A thousand peach trees bloom in Xuandu Temple's grounds —
All planted after Master Liu was driven from the city's towers.
--
Full title: *Written in Jest for the Gentlemen Viewing the Flowers, on Coming to the Capital from Langzhou in 815*. Recalled after ten years of exile, Liu Yuxi found everyone flocking to the peach blossoms of the Xuandu Daoist temple. His joke — all those trees were planted after I left — meant: all these newly powerful officials rose after my party fell. The court took the hint, and sent him away for fourteen more years.

== 再游玄都观 | Visiting Xuandu Temple Again
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 春 花 人生 哲理
scene: spring temple flowers grass person
百亩庭中半是苔，桃花净尽菜花开。
种桃道士归何处？前度刘郎今又来。
--
Half the great courtyard is covered now with moss;
The peaches are all gone; the cabbage flowers grow.
The Daoist who planted the peaches — where has he gone?
The Master Liu of last time is back again — hello!
--
Fourteen years later, in 828, Liu Yuxi was recalled again — and went straight back to Xuandu Temple. The peach trees (the upstarts) are gone, the gardener (the minister who exiled him) is gone, and Master Liu is back: one of the most cheerfully defiant poems in Chinese. 前度刘郎, "the Master Liu of last time", became a phrase for someone returning to an old haunt.

== 春词 | Spring Song
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 闺怨 春 花 女性
scene: spring tower flowers butterfly person
新妆宜面下朱楼，深锁春光一院愁。
行到中庭数花朵，蜻蜓飞上玉搔头。
--
Fresh make-up on her face, she leaves the crimson tower;
Spring's light is locked within a courtyard full of gloom.
She walks into the yard and counts each flower —
A dragonfly lands on her jade pin, mistaking her for a bloom.
--
A lament with a lovely detail: idly counting flowers for lack of anything else to do, she stands so still and looks so fresh that a dragonfly settles on her jade hairpin as if on a flower — but no one else comes to look at her.

== 赏牡丹 | Admiring the Peonies
by: 刘禹锡
form: 七绝
tags: 花 春 咏物
scene: spring flowers palace lotus
庭前芍药妖无格，池上芙蕖净少情。
唯有牡丹真国色，花开时节动京城。
--
The garden's herbaceous peonies are pretty, but have no grace;
The lotus on the pond is pure, but cool and passionless.
Only the tree peony has a true queen's face —
When it blooms, the whole capital is stirred with happiness.
--
In the Tang the tree peony (牡丹) was queen of flowers, and Chang'an went peony-mad every spring, paying fortunes for rare plants. The peony's epithet 国色天香, "beauty of the nation, fragrance of heaven", owes much to this poem.
