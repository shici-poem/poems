== 春晓 | Spring Morning
by: 孟浩然
form: 五绝
tags: 春 花 鸟 雨 风
scene: dawn spring rain petals birds cottage window
春眠不觉晓，处处闻啼鸟。
夜来风雨声，花落知多少。
--
In spring I sleep and miss the break of day;
Everywhere I hear the birds sing on.
Last night, the sound of wind and rain —
How many blossoms fell before the dawn?
--
After *Quiet Night Thoughts*, perhaps the most famous poem in Chinese. Half awake on a spring morning, the poet hears birdsong and remembers the night's storm: a moment of pure contentment shadowed by the thought of fallen petals — beauty and its passing in twenty characters.

== 过故人庄 | Visiting an Old Friend's Farm
by: 孟浩然
form: 五律
tags: 田园 友情 饮酒 节日 秋
scene: autumn hills cottage fields chrysanthemum two jar
故人具鸡黍，邀我至田家。
绿树村边合，青山郭外斜。
开轩面场圃，把酒话桑麻。
待到重阳日，还来就菊花。
--
My old friend made a meal of chicken and millet,
And invited me to his farm to dine.
Green trees encircle the village;
Blue hills slope beyond the town line.
We open the window on the threshing yard and garden,
And over wine we talk of mulberry and hemp.
When the Double Ninth comes round again,
I'll come back for the chrysanthemums — I won't be exempt!
--
The great pastoral poem of the Tang. Everything is plain — chicken and millet, talk of crops — and that is the joy. The promise to return on the Double Ninth, when people drank chrysanthemum wine, is the warmest possible thank-you.

== 宿建德江 | Mooring on the Jiande River
by: 孟浩然
form: 五绝
tags: 羁旅 月 江河 思乡 夜
scene: dusk moon river boat mist hills
移舟泊烟渚，日暮客愁新。
野旷天低树，江清月近人。
--
I steer my boat to moor by a misty isle;
As the sun sets, a traveller's grief comes new.
The plain is vast — the sky stoops below the trees;
The river's clear — the moon comes close to me, too.
--
Mooring at dusk on the Xin'an River in Zhejiang. The last two lines are among the most admired in Chinese: on the open plain the sky seems lower than the trees; in the clear river, the moon's reflection floats right beside the boat — the only companion the lonely traveller has.

== 望洞庭湖赠张丞相 | Gazing at Dongting Lake: To Chancellor Zhang
by: 孟浩然
form: 五律
tags: 山水 壮志 失意 江河
scene: autumn lake wall fisher boat mist
八月湖水平，涵虚混太清。
气蒸云梦泽，波撼岳阳城。
欲济无舟楫，端居耻圣明。
坐观垂钓者，徒有羡鱼情。
--
In the eighth month, the lake brims level with its shore;
It holds the void, and merges with the sky.
Its vapours steam over the Yunmeng Marsh;
Its waves shake Yueyang's walls on high.
I'd cross, but have no boat or oar;
To sit idle in this age of sages would be shame.
I sit and watch the men who fish,
And only envy the fish they claim.
--
A poem asking Zhang Jiuling, the chief minister, for an official post — but it is the first half that made it famous: the colossal lake in autumn flood, steaming over the marshes and shaking the city walls. The second half pleads, gracefully: "I'd cross over (into office) but have no boat; watching others fish, I can only envy their catch."

== 与诸子登岘山 | Climbing Mount Xian with Friends
by: 孟浩然
form: 五律
tags: 怀古 时光 人生 冬
scene: winter mountains river person two
人事有代谢，往来成古今。
江山留胜迹，我辈复登临。
水落鱼梁浅，天寒梦泽深。
羊公碑尚在，读罢泪沾襟。
--
Human affairs succeed each other, waxing and waning;
Comings and goings make up past and present.
The rivers and hills keep their famous sites,
And now we, in our turn, come climbing here.
The water has fallen — Fish Weir isle lies shallow;
The air is cold — the Yunmeng marsh lies deep.
Lord Yang's stone tablet still stands;
Reading it, we wet our robes and weep.
--
Mount Xian near Xiangyang held a memorial stone to Yang Hu, a beloved 3rd-century governor who loved to climb it, and once said sadly that all the worthies who had climbed it were forgotten. People wept on reading his tablet, so it was called the "Tablet of Falling Tears". Meng Haoran, a native of Xiangyang, adds his own tears and his own reflection on time.

== 夏日南亭怀辛大 | Thinking of Xin the Elder in the South Pavilion on a Summer Day
by: 孟浩然
form: 五古
tags: 夏 友情 月 音乐 相思
scene: night summer moon lotus bamboo pavilion
山光忽西落，池月渐东上。
散发乘夕凉，开轩卧闲敞。
荷风送香气，竹露滴清响。
欲取鸣琴弹，恨无知音赏。
感此怀故人，中宵劳梦想。
--
The mountain light drops suddenly into the west;
The pond-moon slowly rises in the east.
I loosen my hair to enjoy the evening cool,
And open the window, lying at ease, at least.
The lotus wind wafts its fragrance by;
Dew drips from bamboos with a crystal sound.
I'd like to take my zither down and play —
But there's no one who understands it around.
Moved by this, I think of my old friend,
And all night long, in dreams, I seek him out.
--
A summer night so quiet that dew dripping from bamboo leaves is audible. The music he would play needs a 知音 — "one who knows the tone", the word for a true friend, from the story of Boya, who broke his zither when the only man who understood his music died.

== 岁暮归南山 | Returning to the Southern Hills at Year's End
by: 孟浩然
form: 五律
tags: 失意 冬 隐逸 月 夜
scene: night winter moon pine window cottage
北阙休上书，南山归敝庐。
不才明主弃，多病故人疏。
白发催年老，青阳逼岁除。
永怀愁不寐，松月夜窗虚。
--
No more petitions to the northern palace gate;
I'm going home to my poor hut in the southern hills.
Lacking talent, I'm cast off by our wise ruler;
Often sick, I'm left by old friends, as old age fills.
White hairs hurry my years toward age;
The green spring sun drives out the year at last.
Brooding long, I lie awake in sorrow —
Pine and moon, through the empty window, cast.
--
Legend says that Meng Haoran was hiding in Wang Wei's office when the emperor walked in, and was made to recite a poem; he chose this one — and when the emperor heard "lacking talent, I'm cast off by our wise ruler", he said, "You never sought office; I never cast you off. Why slander me?" and sent him home. Whether true or not, it is a perfect story of a poet undone by a line.

== 留别王维 | Leaving Wang Wei
by: 孟浩然
form: 五律
tags: 离别 友情 失意 隐逸
scene: day cottage grass two person
寂寂竟何待，朝朝空自归。
欲寻芳草去，惜与故人违。
当路谁相假，知音世所稀。
只应守寂寞，还掩故园扉。
--
In silence, what is it I'm waiting for?
Each day, I come home empty-handed, as before.
I want to go and seek the fragrant grass,
But hate to part from my old friend once more.
Those in power — who will lend me a hand?
True friends who understand are rare in the land.
I should keep to my solitude,
And close my old garden gate, as I'd planned.
--
737, after failing to win office in the capital. "Fragrant grass" is the hermit's life; Meng's only regret is leaving Wang Wei, the one man in Chang'an who truly understood him. The two were later painted together and are always named together as the masters of landscape verse.

== 宿业师山房期丁大不至 | Waiting in Vain for Ding the Elder at Master Ye's Mountain Lodge
by: 孟浩然
form: 五古
tags: 友情 山 夜 月 音乐
scene: dusk moon pine mountains temple birds person
夕阳度西岭，群壑倏已暝。
松月生夜凉，风泉满清听。
樵人归欲尽，烟鸟栖初定。
之子期宿来，孤琴候萝径。
--
The setting sun crosses the western ridge;
The valleys all have suddenly gone dark.
The moon through the pines brings the cool of night;
Wind and springs fill my ears, clear to hark.
The woodcutters have nearly all gone home;
In the mist, the birds have just settled to rest.
You promised to come and stay the night —
With my lone zither, I wait on the vine-hung path, your guest.
--
A mountain evening described with exquisite precision, ending with the poet still waiting, zither in hand, for a friend who never comes. The disappointment is never stated.

== 秋登兰山寄张五 | Climbing Orchid Mountain in Autumn: To Zhang the Fifth
by: 孟浩然
form: 五古
tags: 秋 友情 节日 山 田园
scene: autumn dusk mountains geese river person clouds
北山白云里，隐者自怡悦。
相望试登高，心随雁飞灭。
愁因薄暮起，兴是清秋发。
时见归村人，沙行渡头歇。
天边树若荠，江畔洲如月。
何当载酒来，共醉重阳节。
--
Among the white clouds of the northern hill,
A hermit finds his quiet joy.
To look for you I climb up high;
My heart flies with the geese, and fades away.
Sorrow rises with the gathering dusk;
Delight is born of the clear autumn day.
Now and then I see villagers going home,
Walking the sand, resting at the ferry's quay.
At the sky's edge, trees look small as shepherd's purse;
By the river, the islet curves like a moon.
When will you bring wine, so we can both
Get drunk on the Double Ninth, and soon?
--
A climb to look toward a friend's home. The distant trees "like shepherd's-purse" (a tiny wild herb) and the sandbar "like the moon" give a precise sense of height and distance. The poem ends, as so often with Meng Haoran, with wine and the Double Ninth festival.

== 夜归鹿门歌 | Song of Returning to Deer Gate at Night
by: 孟浩然
form: 七古
tags: 隐逸 月 夜 山 江河
scene: night moon mountains pine temple river boat
山寺钟鸣昼已昏，渔梁渡头争渡喧。
人随沙岸向江村，余亦乘舟归鹿门。
鹿门月照开烟树，忽到庞公栖隐处。
岩扉松径长寂寥，惟有幽人自来去。
--
The mountain temple's bell has rung; day has turned to dusk;
At Fish Weir ferry, people jostle, noisy, to cross.
The crowd goes off along the sandy bank toward the river villages;
I, too, take a boat back home to Deer Gate.
At Deer Gate, moonlight parts the misty trees;
Suddenly I'm at the place where Lord Pang lived hidden.
The rock door and the pine path are always silent —
Only the recluse comes and goes alone.
--
Deer Gate Mountain near Xiangyang, where the Han-dynasty hermit Pang Degong refused all invitations to office and vanished into the hills gathering herbs. Meng Haoran retreated there too. The noisy crowd turns toward the villages; the poet alone turns toward silence.

== 早寒江上有怀 | Early Cold on the River: Thoughts of Home
by: 孟浩然
form: 五律
tags: 思乡 秋 羁旅 江河
scene: autumn geese river sail leaves wind
木落雁南度，北风江上寒。
我家襄水曲，遥隔楚云端。
乡泪客中尽，孤帆天际看。
迷津欲有问，平海夕漫漫。
--
Leaves fall; the wild geese cross toward the south;
The north wind blows; the river's growing cold.
My home is at a bend of the Xiang River,
Far off, beyond the clouds of Chu, I'm told.
My homesick tears are all used up in travel;
I watch a lonely sail at heaven's edge.
Lost, I'd ask the way to the ford —
But the sea-wide river spreads in the dusk past every ledge.
--
Homesick on the lower Yangtze. "Asking the way to the ford" (问津) alludes to Confucius sending a disciple to ask two hermits for the ford — and being told, in effect, that the world was lost. Meng's way home, and his way in life, are both unclear.

== 送朱大入秦 | Seeing Zhu the Elder Off to Qin
by: 孟浩然
form: 五绝
tags: 离别 友情 壮志
scene: day rider two mountains wall
游人五陵去，宝剑值千金。
分手脱相赠，平生一片心。
--
You're off to the Five Tombs, traveller;
My precious sword is worth a thousand gold.
At parting I take it off and give it to you —
With it, a whole life's loyal heart to hold.
--
The Five Tombs (五陵) near Chang'an were where wealthy young knights lived. A sword was a gentleman's most treasured possession; giving it away meant giving one's heart. Twenty characters of gallant friendship.
