== 虞美人（春花秋月何时了） | The Beautiful Lady Yu (Spring Flowers, Autumn Moons)
by: 李煜
form: 词
tags: 怀古 思乡 月 时光 春 江河
scene: night spring moon tower palace river
春花秋月何时了？往事知多少。
小楼昨夜又东风，故国不堪回首月明中。

雕栏玉砌应犹在，只是朱颜改。
问君能有几多愁？恰似一江春水向东流。
--
Spring flowers, autumn moons — when will they ever end?
How many past things haunt my memory!
Last night the east wind blew again around my little tower;
In the bright moon, my lost land — I can't bear to turn and see.

The carved rails and the marble steps must still be there,
But the rosy faces have all grown old.
You ask how much sorrow one man can bear? —
As much as a river in spring flood, flowing east, untold.
--
Li Yu's last poem. A prisoner of the Song in Kaifeng, he had this lyric sung on his birthday, the Seventh Night of 978; when the Song emperor heard of its longing for the lost kingdom, he had him poisoned that same night. Sorrow as a river in spring flood, flowing endlessly east, is one of the most famous images in Chinese. *Yu Meiren* ("Beautiful Lady Yu") is the name of the tune.

== 相见欢（无言独上西楼） | Joy at Meeting (Wordless, I Climb the West Tower)
by: 李煜
form: 词
tags: 秋 月 夜 离别 思乡
scene: night autumn crescent tower palace
无言独上西楼，月如钩。
寂寞梧桐深院锁清秋。

剪不断，理还乱，是离愁。
别是一般滋味在心头。
--
Without a word, alone, I climb the western tower;
The moon is like a hook.
Lonely wutong trees — the deep courtyard locks in autumn's hour.

Cut it — it won't break;
Untangle it — it's tangled more:
This is the grief of parting —
A taste the heart has never known before.
--
Written in captivity. Grief as a thread that cannot be cut or unravelled — 剪不断，理还乱 — is quoted for every hopelessly tangled feeling. The last line, refusing even to name the taste, is the most famous of all.

== 相见欢（林花谢了春红） | Joy at Meeting (The Woods Have Lost Their Red)
by: 李煜
form: 词
tags: 春 花 雨 风 人生 时光
scene: spring rain wind petals palace
林花谢了春红，太匆匆。
无奈朝来寒雨晚来风。

胭脂泪，相留醉，几时重？
自是人生长恨水长东。
--
The flowers in the woods have lost their red of spring —
All too soon!
What can they do, with cold rain every dawn and wind each night?

Rouge-stained tears
Bid me stay and drink —
When again?
Life is forever sorrow, as water forever flows east, out of sight.
--
Fallen blossoms battered by rain and wind are Li Yu's lost kingdom and his own life. The "rouge tears" are rain-wet petals — and the tears of a woman at parting. The last line joins two "forevers": endless regret (长恨) and water forever flowing east (长东).

== 浪淘沙令（帘外雨潺潺） | Waves Washing Sand (Beyond the Blind, the Rain)
by: 李煜
form: 词
tags: 思乡 春 雨 怀古 夜
scene: night spring rain window palace petals
帘外雨潺潺，春意阑珊。
罗衾不耐五更寒。
梦里不知身是客，一晌贪欢。

独自莫凭栏，无限江山。
别时容易见时难。
流水落花春去也，天上人间。
--
Beyond the blind, the rain is pattering;
Spring is fading fast.
My silken quilt can't keep out the cold before the dawn.
Dreaming, I forgot I was a captive guest,
And snatched a moment's joy — now past.

Alone, I must not lean upon the rail
And see the boundless land that once was mine.
Parting was easy; meeting again is hard.
The water flows, the flowers fall, the spring is gone:
Heaven above, and the world of men below.
--
Written shortly before Li Yu's death. The cruellest line is the fourth: in his dream he forgot he was a prisoner and enjoyed a moment of his old life — then woke to the cold. He must not lean on the railing alone, because the view of rivers and mountains reminds him of everything he lost.

== 破阵子（四十年来家国） | Breaking Through the Ranks (Forty Years of House and State)
by: 李煜
form: 词
tags: 怀古 思乡 战争 人生
scene: dusk palace tower clouds mist flag
四十年来家国，三千里地山河。
凤阁龙楼连霄汉，玉树琼枝作烟萝。几曾识干戈？

一旦归为臣虏，沈腰潘鬓消磨。
最是仓皇辞庙日，教坊犹奏别离歌。垂泪对宫娥。
--
Forty years my house and kingdom stood;
Three thousand li of mountains, rivers, lands.
Phoenix halls and dragon towers rose to touch the sky;
Jade trees and gem-like boughs were twined like misty vines.
When did I ever know of shields and spears?

One day I was a captive, bowing to the throne —
My waist grew thin, my temples grey, worn down with the years.
Worst of all, the day I left the ancestral shrine in haste,
The court musicians still were playing songs of parting —
In tears I faced my palace maids, alone.
--
Li Yu looks back on the Southern Tang's forty years and its rich lands, lost when he surrendered to the Song in 975. Shen Yue grew thin and Pan Yue grey with grief — classic images of suffering. Later moralists complained that at the moment of losing his kingdom he wept to his palace maids rather than to his ancestors or people; the poem's honesty is part of its power.

== 清平乐（别来春半） | Pure Serene Music (Since We Parted)
by: 李煜
form: 词
tags: 离别 相思 春 花 鸟
scene: spring petals plum geese palace person
别来春半，触目柔肠断。
砌下落梅如雪乱，拂了一身还满。

雁来音信无凭，路遥归梦难成。
离恨恰如春草，更行更远还生。
--
Since we parted, half of spring is gone;
All I see breaks my heart in two.
Fallen plum blossoms swirl like snow below the steps;
I brush them off — again I'm covered through.

The geese come back, but bring no word from you;
The road's too long for dreams to bring me home.
The sorrow of parting is just like spring grass:
The farther you go, the thicker it has grown.
--
Probably written when Li Yu's brother was held as a hostage at the Song court. The last couplet — parting's grief like spring grass, growing thicker the farther one travels — is one of the most admired images in the *ci*.

== 望江南（多少恨） | Gazing South of the River (How Much Regret)
by: 李煜
form: 词
tags: 怀古 思乡 春 月 花
scene: night spring moon palace flowers horse
多少恨，昨夜梦魂中。
还似旧时游上苑，车如流水马如龙。
花月正春风。
--
How much regret —
In last night's dream I was back again,
Roaming the royal park as in the days of old:
Carriages like flowing water, horses like dragons' train,
Flowers and moonlight in the breeze of spring, as then.
--
A dream of the old splendour of his capital: a stream of carriages, dragon-like horses, flowers and moonlight in the spring breeze. 车如流水马如龙 became the idiom for bustling traffic. The "regret" is that he woke.

== 长相思（一重山） | Endless Longing (One Range of Hills)
by: 李煜
form: 词
tags: 相思 秋 山 鸟 闺怨
scene: autumn maple mountains geese chrysanthemum window
一重山，两重山。
山远天高烟水寒，相思枫叶丹。

菊花开，菊花残。
塞雁高飞人未还，一帘风月闲。
--
One range of hills, then two;
Far hills, high sky, cold misty water too;
My longing burns as red as maple leaves.

Chrysanthemums bloom, chrysanthemums die;
The wild geese fly back high, but he has not come by;
The wind and moon stay idle at the blind as she grieves.
--
A woman's longing through the seasons: the maples redden, the chrysanthemums bloom and fade, the geese return — but her man does not. The attribution to Li Yu is traditional.

== 摊破浣溪沙（菡萏香销翠叶残） | Washing Silk in the Stream, Extended (The Lotus Scent Is Gone)
by: 李璟
form: 词
tags: 秋 闺怨 相思 花 雨 音乐
scene: autumn lotus lake tower rain wind
菡萏香销翠叶残，西风愁起绿波间。
还与韶光共憔悴，不堪看。

细雨梦回鸡塞远，小楼吹彻玉笙寒。
多少泪珠何限恨，倚阑干。
--
The lotus' scent is gone; its green leaves fade;
The west wind stirs up sorrow on the green waves' face.
She withers with the season's lovely light —
Too sad a sight to face.

In fine rain, waking from a dream — the frontier's far away;
In the small tower, she has played the jade pipes through, so cold.
How many tears, how endless her regret —
Leaning on the rail, alone, as of old.
--
By Li Jing, Li Yu's father and the second ruler of the Southern Tang. Wang Guowei admired the first two lines above all: the withered lotus in the west wind evokes "the sorrow of all beautiful things fading". In the second stanza a dream of the far frontier (Jisai), where her husband is, is broken by the rain, and she plays the reed-organ through the night until its tones — and she — grow cold.

== 谒金门（风乍起） | Visiting the Golden Gate (The Wind Springs Up)
by: 冯延巳
form: 词
tags: 春 相思 闺怨 鸟 风
scene: spring wind lake flowers birds person
风乍起，吹皱一池春水。
闲引鸳鸯香径里，手挼红杏蕊。

斗鸭阑干独倚，碧玉搔头斜坠。
终日望君君不至，举头闻鹊喜。
--
The wind springs up —
And ruffles a whole pond of spring.
Idly she lures the mandarin ducks along the fragrant way,
Crushing red apricot buds between her fingers as they cling.

Alone she leans on the rail where ducks once fought;
Her jade hairpin slips aslant.
All day she waits for him, and he does not come —
She looks up, and hears the magpies' joyful chant.
--
The opening — the wind suddenly rising and ruffling a pond of spring water — is a famous image of a heart stirred by nothing in particular. The ruler Li Jing teased Feng Yansi: "A wind ruffling a pond — what has that to do with you?" Magpies were believed to announce good news or an arriving guest, so the poem ends on a flicker of hope.

== 鹊踏枝（谁道闲情抛掷久） | The Magpie Treads the Branch (Who Says I Cast It Off)
by: 冯延巳
form: 词
tags: 春 人生 饮酒 月 柳
scene: dusk spring crescent willow bridge person wind
谁道闲情抛掷久？每到春来，惆怅还依旧。
日日花前常病酒，不辞镜里朱颜瘦。

河畔青芜堤上柳，为问新愁，何事年年有？
独立小桥风满袖，平林新月人归后。
--
Who says I'd long since thrown these idle feelings away?
Each time the spring returns,
The old melancholy burns.
Every day, among the flowers, I drink until I'm ill,
And never mind how in my mirror my young face grows thin.

The green weeds by the river, the willows on the dyke —
I ask: why does this new sorrow
Come back each year again?
Alone upon the little bridge, the wind fills up my sleeves;
Over the level woods, the new moon — when everyone's gone in.
--
Melancholy without a cause, renewed every spring like the grass — the essence of the early *ci*. The closing picture — a lone figure on a small bridge, sleeves full of wind, a new moon over the woods after everyone has gone home — is one of the most beautiful endings in the genre.
