About this collection

This is a free, open collection of 378 of the best-loved poems in the Chinese language, from the Book of Songs (c. 1000 BC) through the Tang and Song dynasties — the golden age of Chinese verse — to the Qing.

译 · Translations

Every poem has an English verse translation that tries to do two things at once: keep the images, allusions and turns of thought of the original, and still read as a poem — with rhyme and a steady beat wherever that can be done without betraying the sense. Classical Chinese packs an extraordinary amount into very few characters, so each poem also has a short note on its background and the meanings that a translation alone cannot carry.

旧译 · Earlier translations

Where one exists, a poem also shows a classic English verse translation, credited to its translator and linked to the book it comes from. All are in the public domain (published before 1930, translators who died long before 1972):

  • James Legge, The She King (1876)
  • Herbert A. Giles, Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) and Gems of Chinese Literature: Verse
  • L. Cranmer-Byng, A Lute of Jade (1909)
  • W. J. B. Fletcher, Gems of Chinese Verse (1919)

They are given as printed, except where a rhyme was broken or a line plainly mistook the Chinese; those few are marked as lightly adapted, and the printed text is always one click away.

拼 · Pinyin and reading aloud

The 拼音 button shows the pronunciation above each character. Readings follow today's school textbooks, checked by hand for the many characters a classical poem reads differently from modern speech — 一骑红尘 jì, 鬓毛衰 cuī, 明月何时照我还 huán. 朗读 reads the poem aloud with your device's own voice, nudged to say those characters right.

画 · Paintings

The ink-wash pictures are not image files. Each poem carries a few words describing its scene — night moon river boat — and the page paints it on the spot as a tiny SVG: in plain ink, pale ochre or Tang-style blue-green, composed by one of Guo Xi's three distances (level, deep or high), signed with the title and the poet's name. The whole gallery costs about as much bandwidth as a single photograph.

搜 · Search

Search in Chinese or English: a poet (李白 or Li Bai), a line or a word (明月), or what a poem is about (moon, wine, homesick, frontier, autumn). Combine words to narrow down: Du Fu autumn. Every list puts the best-known poems and poets first.

字 · Fonts

Poems are set in 霞鹜文楷 LXGW WenKai, titles in 马善政 Ma Shan Zheng brush script; the 字 button in the top bar switches to Song, brush or cursive faces. 繁 shows traditional characters, and 竖排 sets a poem vertically, right to left, as it would appear on a scroll.

读 · Your reading

Poems you have opened are marked 已读 read, Read on the home page lists them, newest first, and Unread only hides them; Random picks one you haven't read yet. This history is kept only in your own browser — nothing is sent anywhere.
