春夜喜雨

〔唐〕杜甫

好雨知时节，当春乃发生。

随风潜入夜，润物细无声。

野径云俱黑，江船火独明。

晓看红湿处，花重锦官城。

英译 English translation

Welcome Rain on a Spring Night

Chūn Yè Xǐ Yǔ
Du Fu · Tang · Five-character regulated verse

Good rain knows its proper season:

It comes in spring, when all things start to grow.

It slips in with the wind by night,

And nourishes the world — so fine, so silent, slow.

The country paths are black with cloud;

A lone boat's lantern on the river is aglow.

At dawn, look where it's red and wet:

Brocade City's flowers hang heavy, row on row.

About this poem

Chengdu, 761, during the happiest years of Du Fu's life, at his thatched cottage. The rain is a considerate friend who "knows" when it is needed and arrives quietly in the night. Brocade City (锦官城) is Chengdu, once the seat of the official in charge of the silk-brocade industry — and in the morning it is indeed brocaded with flowers.

Earlier translations 旧译

“The Little Rain”L. Cranmer-Byng, 1909

Oh! she is good, the little rain! and well she knows our need

Who cometh in the time of spring to aid the sun-drawn seed;

She wanders with a friendly wind through silent nights unseen,

The furrows feel her happy tears, and lo! the land is green.

Last night cloud-shadows gloomed the path that winds to my abode,

And the torches of the river-boats like angry meteors glowed.

To-day fresh colours break the soil, and drenched the blossoms cling

Down broidered lawns all bright with pearls in the garden of the King.

Translated by L. Cranmer-Byng, from A Lute of Jade (1909) · public domain. Lightly adapted: line 7: "and butterflies take wing" (not in the Chinese) changed to "and drenched the blossoms cling" to restore the rain-heavy flowers of the last line.

“The Kindly Rain”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

The kindly rain its proper season knows.

With gentle Spring aye born in fitting hour.

Along the Wind with cloaking Night it goes.

Enmoistening, fine, inaudible it flows.

The clouds the mountain paths in darkness hide.

And lonely bright the vessels' lanterns glower.

Dawn shows how damp the blushing buds divide.

And flowers droop head-heavy in each bower.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
