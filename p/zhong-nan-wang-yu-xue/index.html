终南望余雪

〔唐〕祖咏

终南阴岭秀，积雪浮云端。

林表明霁色，城中增暮寒。

英译 English translation

Gazing at the Lingering Snow on Zhongnan

Zhōng Nán Wàng Yú Xuě
Zu Yong · Tang · Five-character quatrain

Zhongnan's northern ridges rise in beauty proud;

The lingering snow floats high above the clouds.

The treetops glow with light as skies clear late;

And in the city, evening's cold grows great.

About this poem

An examination poem: required to write twelve lines, Zu Yong stopped at four, saying "the meaning is complete". From Chang'an, the snow on the shaded northern slopes of the Zhongnan mountains catches the clearing sunset — and makes the city below feel colder.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Snow on Chung-nan Hills”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

The shaded peaks of Chung-nan Hills their lovely forms display.

Their mass of snow amidst the clouds seems floating far away.

The woods stand out against the sky in colours clear and bright,

Yet stretching o'er the city's eve the chilly hands of night.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain. Lightly adapted: line 1: "Dawn-shaded" changed to "The shaded" and line 4: "morn" changed to "eve"; the Chinese is set at dusk, with the evening cold deepening in the city.
