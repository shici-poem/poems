水龙吟·登建康赏心亭

〔宋〕辛弃疾

楚天千里清秋，水随天去秋无际。

遥岑远目，献愁供恨，玉簪螺髻。

落日楼头，断鸿声里，江南游子。

把吴钩看了，栏杆拍遍，无人会，登临意。

休说鲈鱼堪脍，尽西风，季鹰归未？

求田问舍，怕应羞见，刘郎才气。

可惜流年，忧愁风雨，树犹如此！

倩何人唤取，红巾翠袖，揾英雄泪！

英译 English translation

Water Dragon Chant: Climbing the Pavilion of the Joyful Heart at Jiankang

Shuǐ Lóng Yín Dēng Jiàn Kāng Shǎng Xīn Tíng
Xin Qiji · Song · Ci lyric

A thousand li of southern sky in clear autumn's light;

The water runs with the sky; autumn has no end.

I gaze at the distant hills —

They offer only sorrow, bring only regret,

Like jade hairpins, like conch-shell knots of hair.

At sunset on the tower,

Among the cries of a lone wild goose,

A wanderer in the south am I.

I've stared at my Wu sword,

And slapped the railings all along —

But no one understands

Why I have climbed up here.

Don't tell me the perch is fine for slicing —

The west wind blows; yet has Zhang Han gone home?

To seek out fields and houses for myself

Would make me blush to face

Liu Bei and his great heart.

Alas, the years flow by,

In worry, wind and rain —

Even the trees grow old like this!

Whom can I ask to call

A girl in red scarf and green sleeves,

To wipe away a hero's tears?

About this poem

Nanjing, 1174. Xin Qiji, a northerner who came south to fight, climbs the Pavilion of the Joyful Heart. The distant hills, like a woman's hairpins and topknots, offer only sorrow; he slaps every railing in frustration. He will not go home like Zhang Han, who resigned to eat the perch of his native river, nor buy land like the petty men Liu Bei despised. As the general Huan Wen said, seeing willows he had planted grown huge: "If even trees age like this, how can men bear it?" — and there is no one to wipe his tears.
