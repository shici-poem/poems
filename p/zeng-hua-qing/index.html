赠花卿

〔唐〕杜甫

锦城丝管日纷纷，半入江风半入云。

此曲只应天上有，人间能得几回闻。

英译 English translation

For General Hua

Zèng Huā Qīng
Du Fu · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

In Brocade City, strings and pipes play every day,

Half on the river wind, half to the clouds they float away.

Such music surely only heaven should possess —

How many times can mortals ever hear it play?

About this poem

General Hua Jingding had put down a rebellion in Chengdu, then let his troops loot the city — and, many commentators believe, used music reserved for the emperor at his feasts. Du Fu's lavish praise may be a veiled rebuke: "this belongs in heaven" — that is, at the imperial court, not here.

Earlier translations 旧译

“The Music of the Spheres”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

The flutes that pipe in Chin-ch'eng town confuse the light of day.

Half lost in clouds, the river breeze the one half bears away.

Such music is confined to heaven, for Spirit ears alone.

How rarely can mere mortals catch the echo's distant play!

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
