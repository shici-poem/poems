江城子·密州出猎

〔宋〕苏轼

老夫聊发少年狂，左牵黄，右擎苍，锦帽貂裘，千骑卷平冈。

为报倾城随太守，亲射虎，看孙郎。

酒酣胸胆尚开张，鬓微霜，又何妨！

持节云中，何日遣冯唐？

会挽雕弓如满月，西北望，射天狼。

英译 English translation

River Town: Hunting at Mizhou

Jiāng Chéng Zǐ Mì Zhōu Chū Liè
Su Shi · Song · Ci lyric

This old man, for once, lets loose a young man's wild delight:

A yellow hound upon my left,

A grey hawk on my right.

In brocade caps and sable furs,

A thousand horsemen sweep the hills in flight.

To thank the townsfolk who have followed their governor in force,

I'll shoot the tiger myself, as Sun Quan did, from horse.

Warm with wine, my heart grows bold, my courage swells;

So what if frost has touched my hair?

What harm is there?

When will the court send Feng Tang with its tally to the frontier,

To call me back, and send me there?

I'll draw my carved bow round as the full moon,

Gaze to the north-west,

And shoot down the Wolf Star in the air.

About this poem

Mizhou, 1075. Su Shi, aged thirty-nine, leads a hunt and imagines himself a frontier general. Sun Quan, ruler of Wu, once shot a tiger from horseback. Feng Tang was sent by a Han emperor with a tally to pardon a disgraced general at Yunzhong — Su Shi hopes for such a recall. The Wolf Star (Sirius) stood for invaders from the north-west, the Western Xia and the Liao.
