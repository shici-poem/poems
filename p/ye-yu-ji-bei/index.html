夜雨寄北

〔唐〕李商隐

君问归期未有期，巴山夜雨涨秋池。

何当共剪西窗烛，却话巴山夜雨时。

英译 English translation

Written on a Rainy Night, to the North

Yè Yǔ Jì Běi
Li Shangyin · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

You ask me when I'm coming home — I cannot say just when;

Tonight in the hills of Ba, the rain is filling autumn's pool.

When shall we trim the candle by the west window again,

And talk of tonight, when the rain fell on Ba, so cool?

About this poem

Written in Sichuan (Ba) around 851 to his wife — or a friend — in the north. The present lonely, rainy night is imagined as a future memory shared by candlelight, so the poem folds time back on itself; "Ba hills, night rain" appears twice, once in sorrow and once in comfort. "Trimming the candle at the west window" (西窗剪烛) now means a heart-to-heart talk with someone dear.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Souvenirs”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

You ask when I'm coming: alas, not just yet

How the rain fills the pools on these hills, chill and wet!

Ah, when shall we ever snuff candles again,

And recall the lone hours of this evening of rain?

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain. Lightly adapted: lines 2 and 4 reworded: the rain is falling now, where the poet is, and the hope is to talk of it together later (the printed version makes it a memory of their meeting).

You ask when I'm coming: alas, not just yet

How the rain filled the pools on that night when we met!

Ah, when shall we ever snuff candles again,

And recall the glad hours of that evening of rain?
