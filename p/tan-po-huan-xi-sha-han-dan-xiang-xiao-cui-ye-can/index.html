摊破浣溪沙（菡萏香销翠叶残）

〔五代〕李璟

菡萏香销翠叶残，西风愁起绿波间。

还与韶光共憔悴，不堪看。

细雨梦回鸡塞远，小楼吹彻玉笙寒。

多少泪珠何限恨，倚阑干。

英译 English translation

Washing Silk in the Stream, Extended (The Lotus Scent Is Gone)

Tān Pò Huàn Xī Shā Hàn Dàn Xiāng Xiāo Cuì Yè Cán
Li Jing · Five Dynasties · Ci lyric

The lotus' scent is gone; its green leaves fade;

The west wind stirs up sorrow on the green waves' face.

She withers with the season's lovely light —

Too sad a sight to face.

In fine rain, waking from a dream — the frontier's far away;

In the small tower, she has played the jade pipes through, so cold.

How many tears, how endless her regret —

Leaning on the rail, alone, as of old.

About this poem

By Li Jing, Li Yu's father and the second ruler of the Southern Tang. Wang Guowei admired the first two lines above all: the withered lotus in the west wind evokes "the sorrow of all beautiful things fading". In the second stanza a dream of the far frontier (Jisai), where her husband is, is broken by the rain, and she plays the reed-organ through the night until its tones — and she — grow cold.
