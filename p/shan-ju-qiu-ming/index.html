山居秋暝

〔唐〕王维

空山新雨后，天气晚来秋。

明月松间照，清泉石上流。

竹喧归浣女，莲动下渔舟。

随意春芳歇，王孙自可留。

英译 English translation

Autumn Evening in the Mountains

Shān Jū Qiū Míng
Wang Wei · Tang · Five-character regulated verse

In the empty hills, after fresh rain,

The evening air brings autumn on.

The bright moon shines among the pines;

Clear spring water over stones runs on.

Bamboos rustle — washing girls come home;

Lotus leaves stir — down go fishing boats.

Let spring's fragrance fade as it will:

Here a prince may stay, content to remain.

About this poem

Wang Wei's best-loved regulated poem. The famous second couplet — moon among pines, water over stones — is a perfect ink painting. An old song called on "princes" (gentlemen) to leave the wild mountains, since "spring grasses grow, and you cannot stay"; Wang Wei turns it around: the hills, even in autumn, are where one belongs.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Late Summer”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

The vacant hills are fresh with recent rain.

The coming autumn threats in evening's chill.

Amid the firs the moon peeps in again.

Bright flashes o'er the stones each mountain rill.

With chat of maids, who take their homeward way

Their washing done, the bamboo groves resound.

The fisher's skiff the lotus brushes round:

The water ripples as they stir and sway.

Although the fragrance of the Spring be gone,

Yet Nature's lover well may linger on.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
