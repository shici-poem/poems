秋兴八首（其一）

〔唐〕杜甫

玉露凋伤枫树林，巫山巫峡气萧森。

江间波浪兼天涌，塞上风云接地阴。

丛菊两开他日泪，孤舟一系故园心。

寒衣处处催刀尺，白帝城高急暮砧。

英译 English translation

Autumn Meditations (I)

Qiū Xìng Bā Shǒu Qí Yī
Du Fu · Tang · Seven-character regulated verse

Jade dew has withered and wounded the maple woods;

Wu Mountain and Wu Gorge lie bleak and drear.

On the river, waves leap up to touch the sky;

Over the passes, wind-clouds darken the earth near.

Twice now the chrysanthemums bloom — tears of other days;

My lonely boat stays moored — my heart to my old garden tied.

Everywhere, winter clothes speed knife and ruler on;

High Baidi town, at dusk, hears the pounding mallets outside.

About this poem

The first of "Autumn Meditations", eight linked poems written at Kuizhou in 766 and often considered the summit of Du Fu's art. Autumn in the Three Gorges: the chrysanthemums have bloomed twice since he arrived — two years stranded — and the boat that should take him home stays tied, like his heart. The sound of women beating cloth for winter clothes closes the poem in dusk and cold.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Chants of Autumn”L. Cranmer-Byng, 1909

Shorn by the frost with crystal blade,

The dry leaves, scattered, fall at last;

Among the valleys of Wu Chan

Cold winds of death go wailing past.

Tumultuous waves of the great river rise

And seem to storm the skies,

While snow-bright peak and prairie mist combine,

And greyness softens the harsh mountain line.

Twice now chrysanthemums display

The tears of seasons that have flown.

I am the barque that chains delay:

My homeward thoughts must sail alone.

From house to house warm winter robes are spread,

And through the pine-woods red

Floats up the sound of the washerman's bat who plies

His hurried task ere the brief day wanes and dies.

Translated by L. Cranmer-Byng, from A Lute of Jade (1909) · public domain. Lightly adapted: lines 9–10 reworded to restore the twice-bloomed chrysanthemums and the tears of other days; "noon" changed to "day" in the last line (the Chinese has the evening pounding).

Shorn by the frost with crystal blade,

The dry leaves, scattered, fall at last;

Among the valleys of Wu Chan

I am the barque that chains delay:
