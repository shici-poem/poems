回乡偶书（其一）

〔唐〕贺知章

少小离家老大回，乡音无改鬓毛衰。

儿童相见不相识，笑问客从何处来。

英译 English translation

Coming Home (I)

Huí Xiāng Ǒu Shū Qí Yī
He Zhizhang · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

I left home young, and come back old;

My accent's the same, but my hair's grown thin.

The children see me, a stranger to them all —

"Where are you from, sir?" they ask, with a grin.

About this poem

He Zhizhang came home to Yuezhou (Zhejiang) in 744, aged eighty-five, after more than fifty years at court. The children's innocent question — asking a native where he comes from — holds all the gentle comedy and sadness of the poem.

Earlier translations 旧译

“The Return”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Bowed down with age I seek my native place,

Unchanged my speech, my hair is silvered now;

The village children do not know my face,

But smiling ask, "O stranger, whence art thou?"

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain. Lightly adapted: "My very children" changed to "The village children" in line 3 (the Chinese means the village children, not the poet's own).
