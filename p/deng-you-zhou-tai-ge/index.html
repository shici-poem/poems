登幽州台歌

〔唐〕陈子昂

前不见古人，后不见来者。

念天地之悠悠，独怆然而涕下。

英译 English translation

Song on Climbing Youzhou Terrace

Dēng Yōu Zhōu Tái Gē
Chen Zi'ang · Tang · Mixed-meter verse

Before me, I cannot see the ancients gone;

Behind me, I cannot see those yet to be.

I think of heaven and earth, which go on and on,

And alone, in sorrow, my tears fall free.

About this poem

Youzhou Terrace (near Beijing) was said to be the Golden Terrace built by King Zhao of Yan in the 4th century BC to welcome men of talent. Chen Zi'ang, a frustrated adviser on a military campaign in 697, climbs it: the wise kings are gone, the future cannot be seen, and he stands alone in infinite time and space. One of the loneliest poems ever written.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Regrets”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

My eyes saw not the men of old;

While heaven and earth roll on untold,

I weep—to think I shall not see

The heroes of posterity!

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain. Lightly adapted: line 2 reworded to restore the image of boundless heaven and earth, which the printed version drops.
