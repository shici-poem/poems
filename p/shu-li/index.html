黍离

〔先秦〕诗经

彼黍离离，彼稷之苗。

行迈靡靡，中心摇摇。

知我者，谓我心忧；

不知我者，谓我何求。

悠悠苍天，此何人哉？

彼黍离离，彼稷之穗。

行迈靡靡，中心如醉。

知我者，谓我心忧；

不知我者，谓我何求。

悠悠苍天，此何人哉？

彼黍离离，彼稷之实。

行迈靡靡，中心如噎。

知我者，谓我心忧；

不知我者，谓我何求。

悠悠苍天，此何人哉？

英译 English translation

The Millet Rows

Shǔ Lí
The Book of Songs · Pre-Qin · Four-character verse

The millet grows in rows, row on row;

The sorghum's sprouting green.

I walk on slowly, slowly,

My heart shaken, as in a dream.

Those who know me say I'm grieving;

Those who don't, ask what I seek.

O distant, boundless blue sky —

Who has done this? Speak!

The millet grows in rows, row on row;

The sorghum is in ear.

I walk on slowly, slowly,

My heart as drunk with grief, I fear.

Those who know me say I'm grieving;

Those who don't, ask what I seek.

O distant, boundless blue sky —

Who has done this? Speak!

The millet grows in rows, row on row;

The sorghum's grain is grown.

I walk on slowly, slowly,

My heart is choked, like stone.

Those who know me say I'm grieving;

Those who don't, ask what I seek.

O distant, boundless blue sky —

Who has done this? Speak!

About this poem

Tradition says a Zhou official, passing the ruins of the old Western Zhou capital after it fell in 771 BC, found its palaces turned to millet fields and wrote this lament. 黍离之悲, "the grief of the millet rows", became the standard phrase for mourning a fallen dynasty or a lost homeland.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Shoo le”James Legge, 1876

Where the palaces rose grand,

When Chow nobly ruled the land,

Millets, some with drooping head,

Some, just coming into blade,

All around abundant grew.

Slow the fields I wandered through,

Moved in heart such sight to view.

Friends who knew me understood

What induced my saddened mood.

Those who did not know me said,

'Some quest has him hither led.'

O thou azure Heaven, remote,

Who this desolation wrought?

Where the palaces rose grand,

When Chow nobly ruled the land,

Millets, drooping, heavy here,

There just coming into ear,

All around abundant grew.

Slow the fields I wandered through,

Drunk with grief such sights to view.

Friends who knew me understood

What induced my saddened mood.

Those who did not know me said,

'Some quest has him hither led.'

O thou azure Heaven, remote,

Who this desolation wrought?

Where the palaces rose grand,

When Chow nobly ruled the land,

Millets, heavy, drooping low,

Some the bursting grain that show,

All around abundant grew.

Slow the fields I wandered through,

Breath nigh stopt such scene to view.

Friends who knew me understood

What induced my saddened mood.

Those who did not know me said,

'Some quest has him hither led.'

O thou azure Heaven, remote,

Who this desolation wrought?

Translated by James Legge, from The She King; or, The Book of Ancient Poetry, Translated in English Verse (1876) · public domain. Lightly adapted: in the refrain, "There I search for something made" reworded to "'Some quest has him hither led'" (the Chinese: those who do not know me ask what I am seeking).
