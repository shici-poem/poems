送别（下马饮君酒）

〔唐〕王维

下马饮君酒，问君何所之？

君言不得意，归卧南山陲。

但去莫复问，白云无尽时。

英译 English translation

Farewell (Dismount and Drink)

Sòng Bié Xià Mǎ Yìn Jūn Jiǔ
Wang Wei · Tang · Five-character old-style verse

Dismount and drink this wine with me —

Where are you going, may I ask?

You say the world has not gone well for you;

You'll lie down by the Southern Mountains, leave your task.

Then go — I'll ask no more:

White clouds are endless, as before.

About this poem

A friend gives up his career to become a recluse. Wang Wei does not argue or console; the white clouds — symbol of the free, unworldly life — are "without end", unlike fame and office. The restraint is the comfort.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Goodbye to Meng Hao-jan”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Dismounted, o'er wine we had said our last say;

Then I whisper, "Dear friend, tell me whither away."

"Alas!" he replied, "I am sick of life's ills

"And I long for repose on the slumbering hills.

"But oh seek not to pierce where my footsteps may stray:

"The white clouds will soothe me for ever and ay."

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.

“"So Farewell. And If for Ever, Still for Ever Fare Ye Well."”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

Quitting my horse, a cup with you I drank.

And drinking, asked you whither you were bound.

Your hopes unprospered, said you, turned you round

To sleep amid the Range's outer ground.

You went. I asked no more. The White Clouds pass,

And never yet have any limit found.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
