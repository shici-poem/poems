过零丁洋

〔宋〕文天祥

辛苦遭逢起一经，干戈寥落四周星。

山河破碎风飘絮，身世浮沉雨打萍。

惶恐滩头说惶恐，零丁洋里叹零丁。

人生自古谁无死？留取丹心照汗青。

英译 English translation

Crossing the Lonely Sea

Guò Líng Dīng Yáng
Wen Tianxiang · Song · Seven-character regulated verse

My trials all began when I passed the exam on the Classics;

Four years now, amid the dying wars, we've fought on, few.

The land is shattered, like willow-down blown in the wind;

My life sinks and rises, like duckweed the rain beats through.

At Panic Rapids, I spoke of panic;

On the Lonely Sea, I sigh, alone.

Since ancient times, who has ever lived and never died?

Let my loyal heart shine on in history, my own.

About this poem

Written in 1279 as a prisoner on a Mongol warship crossing the Lingdingyang ("Lonely Sea") near today's Hong Kong, on the way to witness the final destruction of the Song fleet. The Mongol commander asked Wen Tianxiang to write urging the Song loyalists to surrender; he handed over this poem instead. Panic Rapids (惶恐滩) was where he had once retreated in defeat. The last couplet — 人生自古谁无死？留取丹心照汗青 — is the most famous declaration of patriotic faith in Chinese. He was executed in 1283.
