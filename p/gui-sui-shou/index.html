龟虽寿

〔魏晋〕曹操

神龟虽寿，犹有竟时。

腾蛇乘雾，终为土灰。

老骥伏枥，志在千里。

烈士暮年，壮心不已。

盈缩之期，不但在天；

养怡之福，可得永年。

幸甚至哉，歌以咏志。

英译 English translation

Though the Tortoise Lives Long

Guī Suī Shòu
Cao Cao · Wei & Jin · Four-character verse

Though the sacred tortoise lives long,

Its days must have an end.

Though the flying serpent rides the mist,

To dust and ash it will descend.

The old steed in the stable

Still dreams of a thousand li;

A hero in his late years

Has a heart still bold and free.

The length of a life

Is not decided by heaven alone;

With joy and care for oneself,

One may live long years of one's own.

What joy, what supreme good fortune —

I sing this song to make my heart's will known.

About this poem

Written by Cao Cao on the same campaign, at fifty-three — old for his time. 老骥伏枥，志在千里；烈士暮年，壮心不已 — "the old warhorse in the stable still longs to gallop a thousand li; a hero in his last years still has a fiery heart" — is the most quoted line in Chinese on ageing with ambition.
