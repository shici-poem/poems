白雪歌送武判官归京

〔唐〕岑参

北风卷地白草折，胡天八月即飞雪。

忽如一夜春风来，千树万树梨花开。

散入珠帘湿罗幕，狐裘不暖锦衾薄。

将军角弓不得控，都护铁衣冷难着。

瀚海阑干百丈冰，愁云惨淡万里凝。

中军置酒饮归客，胡琴琵琶与羌笛。

纷纷暮雪下辕门，风掣红旗冻不翻。

轮台东门送君去，去时雪满天山路。

山回路转不见君，雪上空留马行处。

英译 English translation

Song of White Snow: Seeing Judge Wu Off to the Capital

Bái Xuě Gē Sòng Wǔ Pàn Guān Guī Jīng
Cen Shen · Tang · Seven-character old-style verse

The north wind rolls the land and snaps the white grass low;

In the eighth month, the northern sky is flying thick with snow.

As if, in a single night, the breeze of spring had come,

A thousand trees, ten thousand trees, burst into pear-blossom.

It drifts through pearl curtains, soaks the silken tent within;

Fox furs give no warmth, and brocade quilts seem thin.

The general cannot draw his horn-bow, stiff with cold;

The Protector's iron armour is too chill to hold.

The desert sea is cracked with ice a hundred fathoms high;

Gloomy clouds, ten thousand li, are frozen as they lie.

In the central tent we set out wine to toast the parting guest,

With Hu fiddles, pipas and Qiang flutes playing their best.

At dusk the snow falls thick and fast upon the camp's front gate;

The red flag, frozen stiff, won't flutter in the wind's weight.

By Luntai's eastern gate I see you on your way;

Snow fills the road across the Tianshan as you leave today.

The mountain bends, the road turns, and you're lost from view;

Only hoofprints in the snow remain, where your horse went through.

About this poem

Luntai, in today's Xinjiang, 754: Cen Shen sees off a colleague returning to Chang'an. Snow arriving overnight like pear blossom on a spring wind is the most famous snow image in Chinese poetry. The ending — the friend gone, only hoofprints left in the snow — is pure longing.
