金缕衣

〔唐〕杜秋娘

劝君莫惜金缕衣，劝君惜取少年时。

花开堪折直须折，莫待无花空折枝。

英译 English translation

The Gold-Threaded Robe

Jīn Lǚ Yī
Du Qiuniang · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

Don't treasure, I beg you, your robe of golden thread;

Treasure your youth instead, while it is here.

When flowers are ripe for picking, pick them, then —

Don't wait to snap bare branches when the flowers disappear.

About this poem

Sung by Du Qiuniang, a singing girl of Jinling who became a consort in the Tang palace (the song is sometimes listed as anonymous). China's carpe diem — "gather ye rosebuds while ye may".

Earlier translations 旧译

“Golden Sands”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

I would not have thee grudge those robes which gleam in rich array,

But I would have thee grudge the hours of youth which glide away.

Go pluck the blooming flower betimes, lest when thou com'st again

Alas, upon the withered stem no blooming flowers remain!

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.
