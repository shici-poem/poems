夜上受降城闻笛

〔唐〕李益

回乐烽前沙似雪，受降城外月如霜。

不知何处吹芦管，一夜征人尽望乡。

英译 English translation

Hearing a Flute at Night on the Wall of Shouxiang

Yè Shàng Shòu Xiáng Chéng Wén Dí
Li Yi · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

Before Huile's beacon tower, the sand lies white as snow;

Outside the Surrender City walls, the moon is like the frost.

From somewhere — who knows where? — a reed pipe's playing low;

All night the soldiers gaze toward home, and feel what they have lost.

About this poem

Shouxiang ("Accepting Surrender") was a fortress on the Yellow River in the far north. Sand and moonlight turn the world white; an unseen reed pipe plays; every soldier turns toward home. Set to music, the poem was sung all over the empire.

Earlier translations 旧译

“The Flute That Wails by Night”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

Below the Hui-lo Peak the sand shines clear and white as snow.

Around Shou-chiang city like frost the moonbeams flow.

Who blows yon wailing reedy flute whose echo shrills my ear,

And tunes the warrior's heart all night to dream of home so dear?

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
