子衿

〔先秦〕诗经

青青子衿，悠悠我心。

纵我不往，子宁不嗣音？

青青子佩，悠悠我思。

纵我不往，子宁不来？

挑兮达兮，在城阙兮。

一日不见，如三月兮。

英译 English translation

Your Collar

Zǐ Jīn
The Book of Songs · Pre-Qin · Four-character verse

Blue, blue, your collar;

Long, long, my heart's desire.

Even if I don't come to you,

Can't you send word, or inquire?

Blue, blue, your girdle-jade;

Long, long, my longing grows.

Even if I don't come to you,

Why don't you come? Who knows?

I pace back and forth

Upon the city tower.

One day I do not see you

Feels like three months, every hour!

About this poem

A girl waits on the city wall for her lover, a student — the "blue collar" was a scholar's dress. 一日不见，如三月兮, "one day without seeing you is like three months", gave Chinese the idiom 一日三秋. Cao Cao borrowed the first couplet in his Short Song, turning it into a longing for men of talent.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Tsze k‘in”James Legge, 1876

You student, with the collar blue,

Long pines my heart with anxious pain.

Although I do not go to you,

Why from all word do you refrain?

O you, with girdle strings of blue,

My thoughts to you for ever roam!

Although I do not go to you,

Yet why to me should you not come?

How reckless you, how light and wild,

There by the tower upon the wall!

One day, from sight of you exiled,

As long as three long months I call.

Translated by James Legge, from The She King; or, The Book of Ancient Poetry, Translated in English Verse (1876) · public domain.
