燕歌行

〔唐〕高适

汉家烟尘在东北，汉将辞家破残贼。

男儿本自重横行，天子非常赐颜色。

𪭢金伐鼓下榆关，旌旆逶迤碣石间。

校尉羽书飞瀚海，单于猎火照狼山。

山川萧条极边土，胡骑凭陵杂风雨。

战士军前半死生，美人帐下犹歌舞。

大漠穷秋塞草腓，孤城落日斗兵稀。

身当恩遇恒轻敌，力尽关山未解围。

铁衣远戍辛勤久，玉箸应啼别离后。

少妇城南欲断肠，征人蓟北空回首。

边庭飘飖那可度，绝域苍茫更何有。

杀气三时作阵云，寒声一夜传刁斗。

相看白刃血纷纷，死节从来岂顾勋。

君不见沙场征战苦，至今犹忆李将军。

英译 English translation

Song of Yan

Yān Gē Xíng
Gao Shi · Tang · Seven-character old-style verse

Smoke and dust of war rise in the Han's north-east;

Han generals leave their homes to crush the rebels, to the least.

Men of spirit prize the chance to ride across the land;

The Son of Heaven grants them favour, rare and grand.

With clanging gongs and pounding drums they march down through Elm Pass;

Their banners wind among the crags of Jieshi, mass on mass.

The colonels' feathered war-reports fly over the desert sea;

On Wolf Mountain the khan's hunting fires blaze free.

Mountains and rivers are desolate to the edge of the frontier ground;

Hu horsemen charge with wind and rain, swarming all around.

At the front, the soldiers — half are dead and half alive;

Beneath the general's tent, the beauties sing and dance and thrive.

In the great desert, autumn's end; the frontier grass is sere;

The lone fort at sunset — few soldiers left to fight are here.

So favoured by the emperor, the general scorned the foe;

Now, strength spent at the passes, the siege won't lift or go.

In iron mail on distant guard through long and bitter years —

Since parting, their wives must weep their jade-chopstick tears.

South of the city, young wives' hearts are breaking with despair;

North in Ji, the soldiers turn their heads in vain, to stare.

The frontier's wild and windswept — how can it be crossed?

The far land's vast and empty — nothing there but loss.

All day the killing-air forms battle clouds on high;

All night the watchmen's gongs ring out their freezing cry.

They face each other's naked blades; the blood in spatters flies —

To die for duty, who would think of honours or of prize?

Have you not seen the bitterness of fighting on the sands?

To this day the soldiers long for General Li to lead their bands.

About this poem

Written in 738 after news of a disastrous campaign in the north-east, where a general's arrogance and false victory reports cost many lives. "At the front, half the soldiers dead; in the general's tent, beauties still singing and dancing" is the most damning couplet in frontier poetry. "Jade chopsticks" are a woman's two streams of tears. General Li is Li Guang of the Han, who shared every hardship with his men.
