采薇（节选）

〔先秦〕诗经

昔我往矣，杨柳依依。

今我来思，雨雪霏霏。

行道迟迟，载渴载饥。

我心伤悲，莫知我哀！

英译 English translation

Gathering Ferns (excerpt)

Cǎi Wēi Jié Xuǎn
The Book of Songs · Pre-Qin · Four-character verse

When I went away long ago,

The willows swayed so tenderly;

Now that I am coming home,

The snowflakes whirl and fly.

Slow, slow, I walk the road,

So thirsty, and so hungry, I.

My heart is full of sorrow —

No one knows my grief, or why.

About this poem

The last stanza of a soldier's song, describing his return from years of fighting the Xianyun nomads in the north. 昔我往矣，杨柳依依；今我来思，雨雪霏霏 — willows when he left, snow when he returns — is often called the most beautiful couplet in the Book of Songs: four images hold a whole lost youth.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Ts‘ae we”James Legge, 1876

Ah! vain our efforts to assuage our grief!

None know our sadness; nought can give relief.

Last year, when from our homes the field we took,

’Twas sweet on willows fresh and green to look.

When we return, ’twill be the winter stern,

And hard our path through snow-clouds to discern.

Alas! how great the toilsome journey’s length,

With thirst and hunger to exhaust our strength!

Translated by James Legge, from The She King; or, The Book of Ancient Poetry, Translated in English Verse (1876) · public domain.
