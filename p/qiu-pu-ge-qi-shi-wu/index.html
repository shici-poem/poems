秋浦歌（其十五）

〔唐〕李白

白发三千丈，缘愁似个长。

不知明镜里，何处得秋霜。

英译 English translation

Autumn River Song (XV)

Qiū Pǔ Gē Qí Shí Wǔ
Li Bai · Tang · Five-character quatrain

My white hair hangs three thousand yards —

So long, because my grief is long as well.

Into the bright mirror I stare:

Where did this autumn frost come from? I cannot tell.

About this poem

From a series of seventeen songs on the Qiupu River in Anhui, written in Li Bai's fifties. "White hair three thousand zhang" (about 10 km!) is the most famous exaggeration in Chinese poetry, and it is somehow perfectly true to the feeling of seeing oneself suddenly old.

Earlier translations 旧译

“In a Mirror”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

My whitening hair would make a long long rope,

Yet could not fathom all my depth of woe;

Though how it comes within a mirror's scope

To sprinkle autumn frosts, I do not know.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.

“Age's Rime”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

Ah me! to make such length of grizzled hair

How many days it grew along with care!

Indeed, how could in this pure mirror show

The origin of so much Autumn snow?

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
