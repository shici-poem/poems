鸟鸣涧

〔唐〕王维

人闲桂花落，夜静春山空。

月出惊山鸟，时鸣春涧中。

英译 English translation

Birdsong in the Ravine

Niǎo Míng Jiàn
Wang Wei · Tang · Five-character quatrain

At ease, I hear the cassia blossoms fall;

The night is still; the spring hills empty lie.

The rising moon startles the mountain birds —

From time to time, they in the spring ravine cry.

About this poem

So quiet that one can hear tiny flowers dropping; so still that moonrise startles the birds. Sound is used to deepen silence — a classic technique of Chinese verse. (桂花 here is probably a spring-flowering cassia or osmanthus.)

Earlier translations 旧译

“A Silent Night”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

My idle days are counted by the falling cassia flowers.

Upon the hills the Spring alone records the noiseless hours.

At sudden rising of the Moon loud shrieks each forest bird.

And yet amid the vernal streams their song is often heard.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
