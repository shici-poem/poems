寻隐者不遇

〔唐〕贾岛

松下问童子，言师采药去。

只在此山中，云深不知处。

英译 English translation

Seeking the Hermit, Not Finding Him

Xún Yǐn Zhě Bù Yù
Jia Dao · Tang · Five-character quatrain

I asked the boy beneath the pine;

"My master's gone for herbs," he said.

"He's on this mountain, somewhere there —

The clouds are deep; I can't say where."

About this poem

Visiting a hermit and not finding him is a whole genre in Chinese poetry. Three questions are implied, three answers given; the hermit himself, lost somewhere in the clouds, becomes part of the mountain.

Earlier translations 旧译

“A Mist Sketch”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

Beneath the firs the lad to me replied:—

"My master has but gone to simples seek."

He said: "He climbed this nearest mountain side."

(Then, with a pause, he added, gazing wide:—)

"The clouds are dense; he's hidden in the reek."

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
