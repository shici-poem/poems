木兰诗

〔南北朝〕北朝民歌

唧唧复唧唧，木兰当户织。

不闻机杼声，惟闻女叹息。

问女何所思，问女何所忆。

女亦无所思，女亦无所忆。

昨夜见军帖，可汗大点兵。

军书十二卷，卷卷有爷名。

阿爷无大儿，木兰无长兄。

愿为市鞍马，从此替爷征。

东市买骏马，西市买鞍鞯。

南市买辔头，北市买长鞭。

旦辞爷娘去，暮宿黄河边。

不闻爷娘唤女声，但闻黄河流水鸣溅溅。

旦辞黄河去，暮至黑山头。

不闻爷娘唤女声，但闻燕山胡骑鸣啾啾。

万里赴戎机，关山度若飞。

朔气传金柝，寒光照铁衣。

将军百战死，壮士十年归。

归来见天子，天子坐明堂。

策勋十二转，赏赐百千强。

可汗问所欲，木兰不用尚书郎。

愿驰千里足，送儿还故乡。

爷娘闻女来，出郭相扶将。

阿姊闻妹来，当户理红妆。

小弟闻姊来，磨刀霍霍向猪羊。

开我东阁门，坐我西阁床。

脱我战时袍，著我旧时裳。

当窗理云鬓，对镜帖花黄。

出门看火伴，火伴皆惊忙：

同行十二年，不知木兰是女郎。

雄兔脚扑朔，雌兔眼迷离。

双兔傍地走，安能辨我是雄雌？

英译 English translation

The Ballad of Mulan

Mù Lán Shī
Northern Dynasties Folk Song · Southern & Northern · Yuefu ballad

"Click-clack, and click-clack" — Mulan weaves at the door;

But we hear no sound of the shuttle — only her sighs, and more.

"What are you thinking of, girl? What's on your mind?"

"Nothing I'm thinking of — nothing at all to find.

Last night I saw the army's call — the Khan is raising men;

Twelve rolls of the army list, and Father's name in every one again.

Father has no grown son; Mulan has no elder brother;

I'll buy a saddle and horse, and go to war in place of Father."

At the east market, a fine horse; at the west, a saddle-cloth bright;

At the south market, a bridle; at the north, a long whip for the fight.

At dawn she leaves her parents; at dusk she camps by the Yellow River's side;

She hears no more her parents calling, only the Yellow River's rushing tide.

At dawn she leaves the Yellow River; at dusk she reaches Black Mountain's height;

She hears no more her parents calling, only Tartar horses neighing on the Yan hills in the night.

Ten thousand li she rides to war; she crosses passes and hills as if in flight.

The northern air carries the watchman's gong; the cold light glints on her armour bright.

Generals die in a hundred battles; after ten years, the brave come home.

Returning, she sees the Son of Heaven, in the Hall of Light, upon his throne.

He records her merit twelve times over, rewards her with a hundred thousand and more.

The Khan asks what she would wish for. "Mulan has no use for a minister's door;

Give me a steed that runs a thousand li, to carry me home once more."

When her parents hear she's coming, they go out of the town to meet her, arm in arm;

When her elder sister hears, she dresses up by the door, in all her charm;

When her little brother hears, he sharpens his knife — swish, swish — for the pigs and sheep.

"I open the door of my eastern room; I sit on my bed in the western room;

I take off my wartime gown, and put on my clothes of old;

At the window I comb my cloudy hair; at the mirror, stick on a flower of gold."

She goes out to see her comrades — and her comrades are all amazed:

"Twelve years we fought side by side — and never knew Mulan was a maid!"

The male hare's feet go hop, hop; the female hare's eyes squint;

But when two hares run side by side, who can tell which is which, by any hint?

About this poem

The Ballad of Mulan, an anonymous folk song of the Northern Dynasties (perhaps 5th–6th century). A girl takes her old father's place in the army, fights for twelve years disguised as a man, refuses high office, and goes home to put on her old clothes — to the astonishment of her comrades. The famous ending: when two hares run side by side, who can tell male from female? The story has been retold ever since, including in Disney's Mulan.
