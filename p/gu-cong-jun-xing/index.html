古从军行

〔唐〕李颀

白日登山望烽火，黄昏饮马傍交河。

行人刁斗风沙暗，公主琵琶幽怨多。

野云万里无城郭，雨雪纷纷连大漠。

胡雁哀鸣夜夜飞，胡儿眼泪双双落。

闻道玉门犹被遮，应将性命逐轻车。

年年战骨埋荒外，空见蒲桃入汉家。

英译 English translation

An Old Army Song

Gǔ Cóng Jūn Xíng
Li Qi · Tang · Seven-character old-style verse

By day we climb the hills to watch for beacon fires;

At dusk we water horses by the Jiao's cold tide.

The watch-gongs clang through the dark of wind and sand;

The princess's pipa sings of grief she cannot hide.

Wild clouds for ten thousand li, and not a town or wall;

Rain and snow come swirling down across the desert wide.

The northern geese cry mournfully, night after night they fly;

The Hu boys' tears fall down in pairs, and none can dry.

They say the Jade Gate's still shut against our coming home —

We must follow the light chariots, and stake our lives, and die.

Year after year, the bones of war are buried in the waste —

And all we get is grapes, brought into the Han to taste.

About this poem

An "old" frontier song, set in the Han but aimed at Xuanzong's wars. The princess's pipa recalls the Han princess married off to a Central Asian king, who played her grief on the pipa. When Emperor Wu of Han's army failed against Ferghana, he shut the Jade Gate and forbade the soldiers to return; the only gain from all the dead was the grapevine, brought back to China. The last line is one of the bitterest in Tang poetry.
