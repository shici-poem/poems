玉阶怨

〔唐〕李白

玉阶生白露，夜久侵罗袜。

却下水晶帘，玲珑望秋月。

英译 English translation

Jade Stairs Lament

Yù Jiē Yuàn
Li Bai · Tang · Yuefu ballad

White dew is forming on the jade stair;

The night grows long; it soaks her silken hose.

She lowers the crystal curtain — there,

Through its glinting beads, the autumn moon still glows.

About this poem

A palace lament in which nothing is said of feeling. A woman has waited on the steps so long that the dew soaks her stockings; she goes in and lowers the blind — and still cannot stop gazing at the moon through it. Ezra Pound admired the poem for saying everything by implication.

Earlier translations 旧译

“From the Palace”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Cold dews of night the terrace crown,

And soak my stockings and my gown;

I'll step behind

The crystal blind,

And watch the autumn moon sink down.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.

“The Steps of Disappointment”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

The dew forms white upon the marble stair.

Our silken socks are damp ere night outwear.

Returning drop our crystal blinds to see

The Autumn Moon gleam through them glintingly.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
