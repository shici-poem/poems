望月怀远

〔唐〕张九龄

海上生明月，天涯共此时。

情人怨遥夜，竟夕起相思。

灭烛怜光满，披衣觉露滋。

不堪盈手赠，还寝梦佳期。

英译 English translation

Gazing at the Moon, Longing for One Far Away

Wàng Yuè Huái Yuǎn
Zhang Jiuling · Tang · Five-character regulated verse

Over the sea the bright moon rises high;

At the ends of the earth, we share this hour.

Lovers resent the long, long night;

All night their longing grows in power.

I snuff the candle — the moonlight fills the room;

I throw a robe on, feeling the dew grow deep.

I cannot give you handfuls of its gleam,

So back to bed — to meet you in my sleep.

About this poem

海上生明月，天涯共此时 — "the bright moon rises over the sea; at the ends of the earth we share this moment" — is the line every Chinese person thinks of at the Mid-Autumn Festival when loved ones are far away. Written by Zhang Jiuling around 737, after his dismissal as chief minister.

Earlier translations 旧译

“By Moonlight”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Over the sea the round moon rises bright,

And floods the horizon with its silver light.

In absence lovers grieve that nights should be,

But all the livelong night I think of thee.

I blow my lamp out to enjoy this rest,

And shake the gathering dewdrop from my vest.

Alas! I cannot share with thee these beams,

So lay me down to seek thee in my dreams.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.
