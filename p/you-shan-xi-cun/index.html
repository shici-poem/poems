游山西村

〔宋〕陆游

莫笑农家腊酒浑，丰年留客足鸡豚。

山重水复疑无路，柳暗花明又一村。

箫鼓追随春社近，衣冠简朴古风存。

从今若许闲乘月，拄杖无时夜叩门。

英译 English translation

Visiting a Village West of the Hills

Yóu Shān Xī Cūn
Lu You · Song · Seven-character regulated verse

Don't laugh at the farmer's cloudy wine, brewed at the year's end;

In a good year there's chicken and pork enough to keep a guest.

Hill upon hill, stream upon stream — I thought the road would end;

Then dark willows, bright flowers — another village, blessed.

Flutes and drums go round: the spring festival draws near;

Their dress is plain, and the good old ways still linger here.

From now on, if I may come strolling by moonlight,

I'll knock upon your door with my staff, at any hour of night.

About this poem

1167, in Lu You's home district near Shaoxing, after he was dismissed for urging war against the Jin. 山重水复疑无路，柳暗花明又一村 — "where hills and streams seem to end the road, past dark willows and bright flowers another village appears" — became the Chinese proverb for unexpected hope when all seems lost.
