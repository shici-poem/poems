十五夜望月

〔唐〕王建

中庭地白树栖鸦，冷露无声湿桂花。

今夜月明人尽望，不知秋思落谁家。

英译 English translation

Gazing at the Moon on the Fifteenth Night

Shí Wǔ Yè Wàng Yuè
Wang Jian · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

The courtyard ground is white; crows roost upon the trees;

Cold dew, in silence, wets the cassia's blooms.

Tonight the moon is bright, and all look up to see —

I wonder in whose house the autumn longing looms?

About this poem

The Mid-Autumn Festival, when families gather under the full moon. The ground white with moonlight, crows silent in the trees, dew falling on the cassia (the tree that grows in the moon) — and a gentle question: everyone is looking at the moon, but whose heart is missing someone most?

Earlier translations 旧译

“The Autumn Festival”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

The slanting moonbeams light the court; the crows roost in their bowers.

Without a sound the chilly dew has wet the cassia flowers.

Upon the moon to-night so clear all human eyes must gaze.

But what fond breast will ponder on the coming Autumn days?

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
