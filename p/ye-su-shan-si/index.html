夜宿山寺

〔唐〕李白

危楼高百尺，手可摘星辰。

不敢高声语，恐惊天上人。

英译 English translation

Staying the Night at a Mountain Temple

Yè Sù Shān Sì
Li Bai · Tang · Five-character quatrain

The towering pagoda soars a hundred feet in height;

From here my hand could pluck the stars of night.

I dare not raise my voice above a sigh,

For fear of waking those who dwell on high.

About this poem

A temple tower on a mountaintop, so high that the stars are within reach and heaven's residents might overhear. Built entirely from playful exaggeration, it is one of the first poems Chinese children learn.

Earlier translations 旧译

“'Twixt Heaven and Earth”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Upon this tall pagoda's peak

My hands can nigh the stars enclose;

I dare not raise my voice to speak,

For fear of startling Heaven's repose.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain. Lightly adapted: "God's" changed to "Heaven's" in the last line (the Chinese speaks of the dwellers in heaven).
