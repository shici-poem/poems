静夜思

〔唐〕李白

床前明月光，疑是地上霜。

举头望明月，低头思故乡。

英译 English translation

Quiet Night Thoughts

Jìng Yè Sī
Li Bai · Tang · Five-character quatrain

Before my bed there gleams a silver light —

Could it be frost that settles on the ground?

I raise my head: the moon is shining bright;

I bow my head: my thoughts are homeward bound.

About this poem

The best-known poem in the Chinese language; nearly every child can recite it. Far from home, the traveller wakes to moonlight so pale he takes it for frost. The same moon shines over his distant hometown, and the simple movement of raising and lowering his head carries him there. (床 may be a bed, or — some scholars argue — the railing of a well in the courtyard.)

Earlier translations 旧译

“Night Thoughts”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

I wake, and moonbeams play around my bed,

Glittering like hoar-frost to my wondering eyes;

Up towards the glorious moon I raise my head,

Then lay me down,—and thoughts of home arise.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.

“Thoughts in a Tranquil Night”L. Cranmer-Byng, 1909

Athwart the bed

I watch the moonbeams cast a trail

So bright, so cold, so frail,

That for a space it gleams

Like hoar-frost on the margin of my dreams.

I raise my head,—

The splendid moon I see:

Then droop my head,

And sink to dreams of thee—

My Fatherland, of thee!

Translated by L. Cranmer-Byng, from A Lute of Jade (1909) · public domain.

“The Moon Shines Everywhere”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

Seeing the Moonbeams by my couch so bright,

I thought hoar frost had fallen in the night.

On the clear Moon I gazed with lifted eyes:

Then hid them full of home's sweet memories.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
