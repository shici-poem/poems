桃夭

〔先秦〕诗经

桃之夭夭，灼灼其华。

之子于归，宜其室家。

桃之夭夭，有蕡其实。

之子于归，宜其家室。

桃之夭夭，其叶蓁蓁。

之子于归，宜其家人。

英译 English translation

The Young Peach Tree

Táo Yāo
The Book of Songs · Pre-Qin · Four-character verse

The peach tree, young and fair,

Its blossoms burning bright!

This girl is going to be wed —

She'll make her home a delight.

The peach tree, young and fair,

Its fruit is round and full!

This girl is going to be wed —

She'll make her household whole.

The peach tree, young and fair,

Its leaves grow thick and green!

This girl is going to be wed —

She'll bless her new kin within.

About this poem

A wedding song. The bride is a young peach tree — first its blazing blossoms (her beauty), then its fruit (children), then its thick leaves (a flourishing family). 桃之夭夭，灼灼其华 is still written on wedding cards, and 灼灼, "burning bright", is the classic word for peach blossom.

Earlier translations 旧译

“T‘aou yaou”James Legge, 1876

Graceful and young the peach tree stands;

How rich its flowers, all gleaming bright!

This bride to her new home repairs;

Chamber and house she’ll order right.

Graceful and young the peach tree stands;

Large crops of fruit it soon will show.

This bride to her new home repairs;

Chamber and house her sway shall know.

Graceful and young the peach tree stands,

Its foliage clustering green and full.

This bride to her new home repairs;

Her household will attest her rule.

Translated by James Legge, from The She King; or, The Book of Ancient Poetry, Translated in English Verse (1876) · public domain.
