滁州西涧

〔唐〕韦应物

独怜幽草涧边生，上有黄鹂深树鸣。

春潮带雨晚来急，野渡无人舟自横。

英译 English translation

West Stream at Chuzhou

Chú Zhōu Xī Jiàn
Wei Yingwu · Tang · Seven-character quatrain

I love the hidden grasses growing by the stream,

While orioles sing high up in the deep green trees.

The spring tide, swelled with rain, at evening rushes strong;

At the lonely ferry, no one — the boat turns idly in the breeze.

About this poem

Chuzhou (Anhui), where Wei Yingwu was governor. The last line — an empty ferryboat swinging crosswise in the current — became a favourite subject of painters, and has been read as the poet himself: idle, unused, at peace.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Superseded”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

Alas for the lonely plant that grows beside the river bed,

While the mango-bird screams loud and long from the tall tree overhead!

Full with the freshets of the spring, the torrent rushes on;

The ferry-boat swings idly, for the ferryman is gone.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.
