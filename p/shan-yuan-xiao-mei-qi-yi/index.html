山园小梅（其一）

〔宋〕林逋

众芳摇落独暄妍，占尽风情向小园。

疏影横斜水清浅，暗香浮动月黄昏。

霜禽欲下先偷眼，粉蝶如知合断魂。

幸有微吟可相狎，不须檀板共金尊。

英译 English translation

Little Plum Tree in the Mountain Garden (I)

Shān Yuán Xiǎo Méi Qí Yī
Lin Bu · Song · Seven-character regulated verse

When all the flowers have fallen, it alone is fresh and fair,

And claims the charm of my small garden as its own.

Its sparse shadows lie aslant on water clear and shallow;

Its hidden fragrance drifts in the dusk where the moon has shone.

The frost-white birds, about to land, first steal a glance;

The powdered butterflies, if they knew, would faint, every one.

Thank heaven for quiet chanting, to keep it company —

No need for singers' clappers or gold wine cups, none.

About this poem

Lin Bu, the hermit of West Lake, "took plum blossom for his wife and cranes for his children". 疏影横斜水清浅，暗香浮动月黄昏 — "sparse shadows slanting over clear, shallow water; a hidden fragrance floating in the moonlit dusk" — became the definitive description of plum blossom; Jiang Kui named two of his finest lyrics "Hidden Fragrance" and "Sparse Shadows".
