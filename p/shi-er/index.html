示儿

〔宋〕陆游

死去元知万事空，但悲不见九州同。

王师北定中原日，家祭无忘告乃翁。

英译 English translation

To My Sons

Shì Ér
Lu You · Song · Seven-character quatrain

I always knew that death would bring an end to everything;

I only grieve I'll never see the nation whole again.

The day the royal army wins back the north for our king,

At the family offering, don't forget to tell your father then.

About this poem

Lu You's last poem, written on his deathbed in 1210 at eighty-five. His whole life's wish — to see the north recovered from the Jurchen Jin — remains unfulfilled, so he asks his sons to report it to his spirit at the ancestral sacrifice. (Seventy years later, the Song itself fell to the Mongols.)
