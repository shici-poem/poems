月下独酌（其一）

〔唐〕李白

花间一壶酒，独酌无相亲。

举杯邀明月，对影成三人。

月既不解饮，影徒随我身。

暂伴月将影，行乐须及春。

我歌月徘徊，我舞影零乱。

醒时同交欢，醉后各分散。

永结无情游，相期邈云汉。

英译 English translation

Drinking Alone by Moonlight

Yuè Xià Dú Zhuó Qí Yī
Li Bai · Tang · Five-character old-style verse

Among the flowers, a jug of wine;

I pour alone, with no one nigh.

I lift my cup to invite the moon —

With my shadow, we make three: moon, shade and I.

But the moon has never learned to drink,

And my shadow only does what I do;

Yet for a while, with moon and shadow,

Let's revel while the spring is new.

I sing — the moon lingers and sways;

I dance — my shadow reels and sprawls.

Sober, we share our joy together;

Drunk, we each go where slumber calls.

Let us pledge a friendship free of worldly ties,

To meet again beyond the Milky Way's far skies.

About this poem

The first of Li Bai's four poems "Drinking Alone by Moonlight". Loneliness becomes a party of three, then dissolves back into solitude. The closing pledge of a 无情游 — a companionship beyond human feeling, with moon and shadow who have none — to be renewed in the Milky Way (云汉), makes his two silent friends his companions for eternity.

Earlier translations 旧译

“Last Words”Herbert A. Giles, 1898

An arbour of flowers and a kettle of wine:

Alas! in the bowers no companion is mine.

Then the moon sheds her rays on my goblet and me,

And my shadow betrays we're a party of three!

Though the moon cannot swallow her share of the grog,

And my shadow must follow wherever I jog,—

Yet their friendship I'll borrow and gaily carouse,

And laugh away sorrow while spring-time allows.

See the moon,—how she glances response to my song;

See my shadow,—it dances so lightly along!

While sober I feel, you are both my good friends;

When drunken I reel, our companionship ends.

But we'll soon have a greeting without a goodbye,

At our next merry meeting away in the sky.

Translated by Herbert A. Giles, from Chinese Poetry in English Verse (1898) · public domain.
