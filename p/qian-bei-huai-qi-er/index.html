遣悲怀（其二）

〔唐〕元稹

昔日戏言身后意，今朝都到眼前来。

衣裳已施行看尽，针线犹存未忍开。

尚想旧情怜婢仆，也曾因梦送钱财。

诚知此恨人人有，贫贱夫妻百事哀。

英译 English translation

Venting My Grief (II)

Qiǎn Bēi Huái Qí Èr
Yuan Zhen · Tang · Seven-character regulated verse

We used to joke of what would come when one of us was dead;

Today it's all come true, before my eyes, as if we'd known.

Your clothes I've given away — they're almost gone, as you said;

Your sewing box I keep, but can't bear to open it alone.

Remembering your love, I'm kind to the maids you knew;

Once, after a dream, I burned paper money for you.

I know this sorrow is a thing that everyone must share —

But for a couple who were poor, each memory brings despair.

About this poem

The second of three elegies for Yuan Zhen's wife, who had shared his years of poverty before he rose in office. The details — her clothes given away, her sewing unopened, money burned for her after a dream — made these some of the most moving elegies in Chinese. 贫贱夫妻百事哀 does not mean poverty spoils marriage; it means that for a couple who suffered together, every memory now hurts.
