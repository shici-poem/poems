终南别业

〔唐〕王维

中岁颇好道，晚家南山陲。

兴来每独往，胜事空自知。

行到水穷处，坐看云起时。

偶然值林叟，谈笑无还期。

英译 English translation

My Retreat at Zhongnan

Zhōng Nán Bié Yè
Wang Wei · Tang · Five-character regulated verse

In middle age I came to love the Way;

In later years I made my home where the South Hills end.

When the mood comes, I often go alone;

Its joys are mine to know, and no one else's, friend.

I walk to where the stream runs out,

And sit to watch the clouds begin to rise.

By chance I meet an old man of the woods;

We talk and laugh, and lose track of the time that flies.

About this poem

"Walk to where the water ends, sit and watch the clouds rise" (行到水穷处，坐看云起时) is one of the most quoted couplets in Chinese — a Zen image of following life wherever it goes: where one path ends, another begins in the sky.

Earlier translations 旧译

“My Villa at Chung-nan”W. J. B. Fletcher, 1919

In middle age I loved to walk the path of Buddhist lore.

A home I've made these latter years on Chung-nan's frontier hoar.

As prompts the mood I ever come to wander here alone,

Where all the pleasure that I find is mine and all mine own.

Some stream I follow to its source; and there I set me down

To watch the clouds come drifting up across the mountains brown.

Perchance some aged rustic may light upon me there:

Forgetting time we chat and laugh, oblivious of care.

Translated by W. J. B. Fletcher, from Gems of Chinese Verse (1919) · public domain.
